# PRD Quality Review — BBSL Offseason Free Agent Auction (seventh pass, 2026-09-16)

*Lens: `prd-validation-checklist.md`. Attention weighted to the 2026-09-16 seventh-pass surface — §0 callout, §3 Glossary (Bid Retraction, Retraction Window, Committed Bids, Audit Log), FR-14, FR-15, FR-18, FR-20, FR-40, §6, §9, §10 ex. 47–52, §12, addendum §G — and to its coherence with the ~1100 unchanged lines.*

## Overall verdict

FR-15's rewrite is the strongest single requirement in this document: it states the reversal plainly, gives the reason (the typo), names the four rejected alternatives in §12 with what each cost, and makes six worked examples carry the arithmetic. What holds up is the rule itself and its integration with the money model — FR-14, the Glossary, FR-20 and FR-40 were all amended in step and none of them drifts. What is at risk is the *boundary* of the new mechanism against unchanged sections: FR-15 asserts its cut-short list is complete and it is not (a restoration under FR-40/FR-15 re-commits the displaced Team's capital and is not on the list); FR-18's "always runs its full five minutes" lets a lottery entry be retracted after its draw; and §4.7, FR-33 and FR-34 — none of them touched — do not carry the retraction event, the retraction Audit Log entry, or the pause interaction that FR-15 and §9 both depend on. Three high findings, all at that seam; none is in the arithmetic.

## Decision-readiness — strong

FR-15 is decision-shaped throughout. The retitle block (§4.4, FR-15) does not soften the reversal — *"That prohibition is now bounded rather than absolute. The number is kept because this is the same rule about the same question"* — and the **Why it exists** paragraph commits to a single purpose (*"The window exists for the typo. It is not a negotiating instrument"*) rather than balancing. §12's four seventh-pass entries each name the alternative and why it lost: the contention-entry entry records that *"the concern that a shrinking Contender list would force a dissolution carrying a **revealed seed** was **wrong**"*, which is a PM admitting a rejected line of reasoning rather than burying it. The directional-test entry goes further and names the cost it is taking on: *"the directional test must be **maintained** as a list, and the entry below — the roster acts — is the gap that list already had on the day it was written."* That is the §12 register this rubric wants and rarely sees.

The two new §9 risks are at real tensions, not safe checkpoints. *"A manager will find the retraction window closed for a reason they cannot see"* names the sharpest thing about the design — that the window's life is governed entirely by a third party the retracting Manager cannot observe — and does not claim to have solved it.

### Findings
- **medium** §0 and §12 both claim exactly two open items, and at least two other live `Confirm` asks survive (§0 status callout; §12) — §0 reads *"Two open items, both introduced 2026-09-16 by FR-15"* and §12's seventh-pass preamble says the two UX inferences *"are the only open items in this document."* But §12 still carries **§3 Glossary / FR-39** — *"**Confirm if you would rather see the true mean-of-two-middles and accept a second money format for it**"* — and **§4.10 / FR-41** — *"**Confirm after the moderator pilot** … **This is the one live inference introduced by the 2026-09-10 amendment**"*. Both are unanswered asks addressed to the commissioner. A reader trusting the §0 callout will not go looking for them. *Fix:* either retire the two older asks explicitly (as the 2026-08-17 block retires OQ-driven assumptions), or restate §0 as "two open items introduced by this pass, alongside two carried forward" and drop "the only open items" from §12.

## Substance over theater — strong

Nothing in the seventh-pass surface is furniture. Each of examples 47–52 pins a distinct rule: 47 the base case with the no-floor rebid, 48 the past-clock close, 49 the directional test in both directions, 50 the Drop trap, 51 both lottery shapes, 52 the termination argument. Example 52's closing observation — *"all three windows are independent: A's was governed by B's activity, B's by C's, and C's by whoever C outbid"* — is the kind of sentence that exists because someone worked the case, not because a template asked for one.

The §6 non-goal amendment is a single earned sentence rather than a restatement: *"A Trade is not a route around FR-15: the only Team that can take a Bid back is the one that made it, inside its own five minutes."* That closes a loophole the fifth pass opened. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis — *"nobody has to wonder whether the app got it right"* (§1) — survives the reversal, and FR-15 is argued from it rather than against it: the old rule made *"the Commissioner adjudicating typos, and an Audit Log that cannot tell a slip from a change of heart"* (FR-15, **Why it exists**). The insistence that the three withdrawal kinds stay distinguishable in the log (FR-15 *What has not changed*; FR-40 *"Three withdrawals, one restorer, and they must not be conflated"*) is the thesis applied, not decoration.

### Findings
- **low** No counter-metric for the mechanism whose stated risk is misuse (§8) — §8 carries four counter-metrics (SM-C1–C4) and the seventh pass added none, although §0 and FR-15 both name the failure mode explicitly: *"It is not a negotiating instrument."* SM-1's *"Validates FR-11 – FR-22"* picks up FR-15 only by range. A retraction rate, or retractions not followed by a rebid within the hour, is the natural counter-metric and the document's own discipline calls for one. *Fix:* add SM-C5 counterbalancing FR-15, or state in §8 why retraction volume is deliberately not measured.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

FR-15's consequences are unusually testable — the window is *"derived, never stored"*, the cut-short is an enumerated list of five, the treatment is stated on all three axes (erase / restore clock / remove League Clock reset), and the restoration path explicitly reuses FR-40's selector. Examples 47–52 give every one of those a worked case. This is the dimension the seventh pass spent most of its effort on and it shows.

It is *adequate* rather than *strong* because three boundaries of the new mechanism are undefined, and one of them is asserted to be defined when it is not.

### Findings
- **high** The cut-short list is asserted exhaustive and omits restoration (§4.4, FR-15, *The cut-short*) — FR-15 states *"Exactly five things do that"* and then relies on the claim: *"**Because the cut-short list is complete, the displaced Team is in practice always restorable**."* But a displaced Team can have its capital re-committed without doing any of the five: FR-40 *Restoration* says the next-highest Bid *"becomes the leading Bid and that Team's capital is re-committed"*, and FR-15's own *Who gets the Auction back* invokes the same path. So Team B, displaced on Auction X, can be restored to leadership on Auction Y by a third party's retraction or cancellation — capital committed, Maximum Bid down, strictly *harder* to restore on X — with no entry on the list. This is precisely the directional adversity the list exists to catch. The document half-knows: *"A retraction that reaches a Team other than the one it displaced is a signal that the cut-short list has a hole in it."* *Fix:* add a sixth trigger — *is restored as Leading Bidder on any Auction (FR-40 or FR-15)* — or replace the exhaustiveness claim with "the list is the best current enumeration; the skip walk is the guarantee," which is what the skip bullet already argues.
- **high** A Minimum-Bid Contention entry's window can outlive the draw that resolved it (§4.5, FR-18; §4.4, FR-15) — FR-18 says *"A join displaced nobody, so the window always runs its full five minutes"*, and FR-15 repeats it: *"The same is true of a Minimum-Bid Contention entry, which joins rather than displaces."* A join does **not** reset the Auction Clock (FR-18), so a Team may join at 08:59 a contention that expires at 09:00. Nothing in FR-15, FR-18 or FR-20 says a Close or a Draw ends the window. FR-15's only guard is *"only while that Bid still stands — … in Minimum-Bid Contention that the Team is still in the Contender list"*, and whether a drawn-and-closed lottery's Contender list still "stands" is exactly what is undefined. Read with FR-20 — *"The recorded list is the list after any … retractions under FR-15 have been applied"* — an implementer could plausibly build a retraction that mutates a recorded draw. In Standard Contention the hazard cannot arise (every Bid resets the clock to 24h), which is why it is easy to miss. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-15 stating that a Close or a Draw ends the window immediately regardless of elapsed time, and correct FR-18's *"always runs its full five minutes"* to name that bound. §10 example 51 is the place to work it.
- **medium** No requirement specifies the retract control; its only specification is a §9 risk mitigation (§4.4, FR-15; §4.6, FR-24; §9) — FR-15 says only *"Outside the window there is **no retraction and no control**"*, which implies a control inside the window without ever specifying one. The actual behavioural requirement lives in a mitigation clause: *"the control must show the **remaining time live and disappear the instant the window closes**, rather than sitting there and failing on submit; and the refusal must name **what closed it and when**"* (§9). FR-24, which enumerates the auction page's bid affordances and their disabled-with-reason rules, is unchanged and does not mention retraction. A story writer source-extracting from §4 will not build the control; one extracting from §9 will build it without the disabled-reason discipline FR-24 establishes for every other control. *Fix:* promote the §9 sentences into FR-15 consequences under a *The control* heading, and add a bullet to FR-24 placing it alongside the existing three distinctly-worded refusal reasons.
- **medium** FR-34's pause does not say what happens to an open Retraction Window (§4.10, FR-34; §4.4, FR-15) — FR-34 stops *"all Auction Clocks and the League Clock"* and refuses Nominations and Bids; it says nothing about retraction, and FR-15 says nothing about pause. Because the window is *"derived, never stored"* from *"a Bid's own timestamp"* (FR-15; addendum §G), a pause of more than five minutes silently expires every open window — the opposite of FR-34's stated posture that *"On resume, every Clock continues with exactly the remaining time it held at pause."* Note that FR-44, the other new Manager act, is explicitly *"**Refused while the auction is paused** (FR-34)"* with a worded-as-the-pause refusal; FR-15 has no equivalent. *Fix:* state in FR-15 whether the window is wall-clock or pause-aware, and add retraction to FR-34's consequences either way. A pause-aware window means the fold must subtract paused intervals, which contradicts addendum §G's *"a comparison between a Bid's own timestamp, the injected server time"* — so the choice has an architecture consequence and should be made here.
- **medium** "A Bid of its own is cancelled under FR-40" is a cut-short trigger that fails the document's own directional test (§4.4, FR-15, *The cut-short*) — the rule is *"the moment the displaced Team's position moves in a direction that could make restoring it fail"*, and the contrast case is *"capital **released** because it was outbid elsewhere … there is nothing to protect it from."* A Bid cancellation releases capital in exactly that way (FR-40: *"The cancelled Team's committed capital is released"*). The adverse change is the **Close the Team won**, which is already trigger two, so the cancellation item is at best redundant and at worst a counterexample to the directional principle stated three bullets later — *"**The test is directional, not 'has anything changed.'**"* A reader deriving the rule from the principle will not produce this list. *Fix:* either drop the item (the Close covers it) or add a half-sentence saying it is listed as a belt-and-braces proxy for the Close that caused it, not because a cancellation is itself adverse.

## Scope honesty — adequate

The seventh pass de-scopes nothing silently and adds two `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (no reason field; broadcast to the league), both indexed, both marked *"confirm at UX"*, both correctly characterised as low-stakes. The §6 amendment is an addition rather than a removal. The §0 consequence block — *"Three consequences a reader should not have to discover"* — continues the document's best habit.

Open-items density is low and appropriate for a green-light-to-build PRD: two UX-level inferences against a mechanism the document says *"Nothing had been built when this was written"*. The only dent is the overclaim recorded under Decision-readiness, which is a bookkeeping failure rather than a hidden omission.

### Findings
- **low** §7.1 was not updated for the retraction window (§7.1) — the in-scope line reads *"Bidding with continuous Maximum Bid computation, server-side rejection of illegal bids, conservative commitment on Minor League Eligible players, and Roster Capacity enforcement (FR-11 – FR-15, FR-35, FR-37)"*. FR-15 is inside the range but the prose enumerates every other capability by name, so the one reversed requirement is the only one the scope list does not describe. The same omission was caught and corrected for FR-41–FR-43 on 2026-09-12, per the parenthetical on that line. *Fix:* add "and bounded bid retraction" to the clause.

## Downstream usability — adequate

The Glossary discipline held: **Bid Retraction** and **Retraction Window** are added with cross-references that resolve, **Committed Bids** and **Audit Log** are amended in step, and the three-withdrawals distinction is stated identically in the Glossary, FR-15 and FR-40 — no synonym slipped in. Addendum §G is exemplary handoff work: it names AD-31, AD-22, `SPEC.md` CAP-5 and `epics.md` line 257 as the documents needing amendment and assigns each an owning workflow, so `bmad-spec` and `bmad-architecture` inherit the list rather than rediscovering it.

The gap is in §4.7 and §4.10, neither of which was touched, and both of which FR-15 delegates to.

### Findings
- **high** FR-15 delegates its broadcast and its notifications to a §4.7 that carries neither event (§4.4, FR-15, *Record*; §4.7, FR-26, FR-27) — FR-15 requires *"Every retraction is written to the **Audit Log and broadcast to the league** per §4.7"* and *"The **restored Team is notified that it is leading again**."* FR-26's posted-event list is *"Nominations, Bids, Auction Closes, Randomizer draws …, Auction Phase start, and Auction Phase end"* — no retraction. FR-27's mention list is *"when their Team is outbid, when an Auction their Team leads or contends in closes, when their Nomination Slot is released, when their unbid Nomination hits 24 hours …, and when the Contract Assignment Phase opens"* — no "restored to leading," and no "your Bid was cancelled" either, so FR-40 has carried the same defect since 2026-09-08 and FR-15 has now doubled it. A story writer extracting §4.7 alone builds neither. Compounding it, §12's broadcast assumption says *"**Confirm at UX**, with FR-27's notification settings in view"* — but FR-27's three mutable categories were not extended, so there is nothing in view to confirm against. *Fix:* add retraction and cancellation to FR-26's event list, and add "restored as Leading Bidder" and "your Bid was cancelled" to FR-27's mention list, with a decision on whether either is mutable.
- **medium** FR-33's entry list omits both Bid Retraction and Bid Cancellation, contradicting the amended Glossary (§4.10, FR-33; §3 Glossary) — the Glossary now defines **Audit Log** as *"the append-only, league-visible record of every Nomination, Bid, Bid Retraction, Bid Cancellation, Auction Close, Randomizer draw, and Commissioner override."* FR-33's consequence still reads *"Entries cover every Nomination, Bid, Auction Close, Randomizer draw with seed, Commissioner override, import, and export."* The Glossary is the authority the §3 preamble establishes, so this is drift rather than ambiguity — but FR-33 is the requirement a story will be written from, and it is the one that is wrong. *Fix:* restate FR-33's list from the Glossary definition, adding import and export to the Glossary side so the two match exactly.
- **medium** FR-40's "same selector, same skip rule" claim is not supported by FR-32's unchanged text (§4.5, FR-40, *The trigger*; §4.10, FR-32) — FR-40's amended bullet asserts of cancellation, void and retraction that *"**All three** hand the Auction to the next-highest surviving Bid by the same selector, under the same skip rule."* FR-32 was not amended and still says only *"Voiding a Bid restores the Auction to its state before that Bid, including the prior Leading Bidder and the prior Auction Clock value"* — no re-evaluation against the money and capacity gates, and no skip. Addendum §G asserts AD-31 already has *"one restorer, two callers"*, so the architecture treats void as a restorer caller; the PRD's own FR-32 does not. An implementer reading FR-32 builds an unconditional restore-prior-leader that can seat a Team into a breach of its own cap. *Fix:* amend FR-32's void consequence to name the gate re-evaluation and the skip, cross-referencing FR-40.
- **low** Retract-then-rejoin the same contention is unaddressed (§4.5, FR-18) — FR-18 says *"A Team already a Contender cannot join twice"*; after a retraction the Team is not a Contender, so it may rejoin, and FR-15's no-cooldown rule (*"no fee, no cooldown"*) appears to permit it deliberately. Harmless in outcome, but it is a loop with no stated bound and the Audit Log carries every iteration. *Fix:* one clause in FR-18 confirming rejoining is permitted, so an implementer does not guess at a guard.

## Shape fit — strong

Unchanged and still right. This is a rules-engine spec for a single league and it is shaped as one: Glossary-first, globally numbered FRs with testable consequences, worked examples as the executable contract. The four UJs remain load-bearing for a multi-manager product and none was padded for this pass. The seventh pass correctly put its weight on §10 rather than adding a fifth journey for the retracting manager — the mechanic is a correction path, not a journey, and §9's comprehension risk carries the human framing instead. No findings.
