# PRD: BBSL Offseason Free Agent Auction
*Working title — confirm.*

## 0. Document Purpose

This PRD is for the builder-commissioner of the BBSL (a 30-team dynasty fantasy basketball league, now entering its 6th season) and for the downstream BMad workflows that will consume it — UX, architecture, and epic/story breakdown. It is structured Glossary-first because this product is fundamentally a *rules engine*: almost every requirement is a precise statement about money, timers, or eligibility, and a single synonym slipping into the vocabulary is how a league loses trust in its auction. §3 fixes the vocabulary; §4 groups features with globally numbered FRs nested underneath; §10 walks worked rule-resolution examples that exist specifically to keep implementers from guessing at the edge cases. Inferences are tagged inline as `[ASSUMPTION]` and indexed in §12.

No prior design or research artifacts exist for this product. Technical mechanism — CSV column shapes, timer implementation strategies, concurrency approach — deliberately lives in `addendum.md` alongside this file, not here.

> **Status: no blocking items. Two open items introduced 2026-09-16 by FR-15, both UX-level and both indexed in §12** — what a retraction records, and what it announces. Every rule needed to compute a bid is settled. Older live asks elsewhere in §12 are unaffected and still stand. **The rule most likely to surprise a reader is now §3's *Slot Placement*: every won Player takes an Active/Bench Slot, at the full winning amount, whatever his Minor League Eligibility and however empty his Team's minors.** You cannot win a Free Agent into your minors — you have to be able to fit him on your active roster first, and **FR-44** is the act that moves him down afterwards and reopens the Slot. That rule replaced **FR-35**, which said the opposite and is **retired**; a reader who remembers *“no cap limit”*, *Minors Exposure* or *unbounded bidding* is remembering a rule this league does not have. The rule most likely to surprise a *manager* is **FR-40**: a bid they are winning can be cancelled by a close on a different auction. Read **§3's Slot Placement**, **FR-14**, **FR-37**, **FR-44**, **FR-40** and **FR-15**, and §10 examples 23–24, 29–34, 43–45 and 47–57, before touching cap or roster logic — and note that §10 examples **16–22, 25 and 35 are retired in place**, kept as records of the corrected rule rather than as cases. FR-15 is on that list because since 2026-09-16 it **carries a rule rather than a prohibition** — a Bid can be taken back inside ninety seconds — making it the one requirement here whose title reversed its meaning. Read it rather than remembering it.

The League Median is defined as the *lower* middle value instead. §10 example **28** was added to make that executable.

> **Amended 2026-09-08 (fourth pass)** when the commissioner recorded a league rule that had never reached this document — and this one *does* add a rule to the auction engine. It arrived after Epics 1–5 shipped, and it contradicts two requirements this PRD previously stated as settled:
>
> - **The Outstanding Bid Allowance (FR-37, rewritten).** A Team may hold **one more outstanding bid than it has Free Active/Bench Slots**, so that a manager pursuing two players need not idle for a day waiting on a close. The purpose is auction speed. The ceiling of 12 is untouched — the allowance is a bidding permission, never a thirteenth Roster Slot.
> - **Bid Cancellation (FR-40, new).** The allowance is only safe because the surplus is taken back. When a Close leaves a Team holding commitments with no Slot to receive them, the system **cancels them and restores each affected Auction to its next-highest bidder**. This is the first mechanism in the product that removes a Bid without a Commissioner acting, and it required narrowing **FR-15**, which previously said no accepted Bid could ever be withdrawn except by override.
> - **Unlimited lottery participation (FR-18).** A Minimum-Bid Contention entry no longer consumes roster capacity at all. A Team with one open Slot may enter as many lotteries as its **cap space** allows, on the reasoning that the expected outcome of a lottery is losing. Cap space remains the only quantitative limit.
>
> Two consequences a reader should not have to discover. First, **a Team at Roster Count 12 holds no Active/Bench-bound commitment at all** — the allowance requires at least one open Slot, because a Team with none could win its allowance bid with nothing available to cancel it first, landing on 13. *(Still true, and since 2026-09-18 it is the **whole** of what such a Team may hold: it can no longer bid on Minor League Eligible Players either.)* Second, ~~**`Overflow Count` split in two**: the money side still counts lottery entries, the slots side no longer does, so the Glossary now names **Active/Bench Overflow** separately.~~ *(**Both figures were retired on 2026-09-18** and are permanently zero. The split itself was right and outlived them: **Projected Active/Bench Additions** and **Reserve Additions** are the surviving pair that differ by exactly the lottery entries.)* Examples **29–35** make all of it executable; **23–25** were rewritten against the new ceiling.
>
> **Amended 2026-09-10 (fifth pass)** when the commissioner recorded that managers **trade and drop players in Fantrax while the auction is running**. Both change Cap Space, Roster Count and slot occupancy — the inputs to Maximum Bid — so an unrecorded one leaves the app computing against a roster that stopped being true. Two non-goals gave way, and a second gap surfaced while specifying the first:
>
> - **Roster Trades (FR-41, new).** *"Not a trade machine"* becomes **"not a trade broker."** The app hosts no proposal or negotiation; a trade is agreed in Discord, executed in Fantrax, and its **effect on rosters** is recorded here. Both Existing and Auction Contracts may move, in both directions **as one act with one evaluation at the end**. **Committed capital never moves** — a leading Bid, a contention entry and Cap Space cannot be traded, and FR-15 is untouched.
> - **Drops and Dead Money (FR-43, new).** A Drop frees the Roster Slot but **does not release the Cap Hit**, which carries as **Dead Money**. The governing rule is one sentence — *a Drop converts the Player's **charged** Cap Hit into Dead Money, at the same amount* — and all three Slot kinds fall out of it. ~~The exception is a second-round rookie Contract of the current draft class, which clears entirely.~~ *(The exception was **removed 2026-09-16** — see the sixth-pass block below. The governing rule stands alone and is unchanged.)*
> - **Roster Divergence (FR-42, new, contingent).** Fantrax has **no write path at all** — verified 2026-09-10, every documented method is a GET — but its `getTeamRosters` read returns membership, and membership is the only fact the app lacks, since it already holds every Cap Hit. So the app can **detect** a trade nobody reported. The read proposes and never writes.
>
> Three consequences a reader should not have to discover. First, **a Trade can be refused because of the Team giving players up** — a freed Slot costs $1,000,000 of Roster Reserve against a Cap Hit that may be worth less. Second, ~~**a Drop moves Maximum Bid in either direction**: down from Active/Bench, up from a Minor League Slot via Minors Exposure.~~ *(**Corrected twice.** From 2026-09-16 an Active/Bench Drop moves it in one direction only; and from **2026-09-18** a Minor League Drop moves it in **no** direction — the Player was charging $0, so nothing is released, and the freed Slot buys no bidding power. A Drop now either lowers Maximum Bid or leaves it alone. FR-43's table says so.)* Third, **a Trade or Drop is refused, never cancelled around** — FR-40's cancellation trigger is still an Auction Close and nothing else. Examples **36–43** make all of it executable.
>
> **Amended 2026-09-16 (sixth pass)** when the league confirmed that **FR-43 has no exception**, and never did. *(The 2026-09-12 addition of the Roster Move — FR-44 — took no block of its own; it added a requirement and contradicted nothing. This one removes a rule.)*
>
> - **The rookie-scale carve-out is gone (FR-43).** The PRD said a second-round rookie Contract of the current draft class cleared entirely on a Drop — its row removed, its Cap Hit released back to Cap Space. **The league has no such rule during the auction.** Dead Money is waived in an **amnesty period that runs *before* the auction opens**; it is settled in Fantrax and is already reflected in the rosters this app imports (FR-1). By the time a Drop can be recorded here there is nothing left to waive. **This product therefore needs no amnesty capability and gains none** — the waiver is a league process that happens upstream of the import, not a feature.
> - **The governing rule now stands alone, unchanged:** *a Drop converts the Player's **charged** Cap Hit into Dead Money, at the same amount.* All three Slot kinds still fall out of it, and FR-43's table is untouched. A Minor League row still leaves nothing behind — because it was charging $0, **not** because anything was waived.
> - **The designation still survives the import, and still decides nothing.** `2RK31` and `2031` remain distinguishable end to end: the round is parsed, staged, persisted and written into the Drop's record, because it is a fact about the Contract and the events already in the log carry it. What is gone is its **rule effect**. Story 7.6's work stands.
>
> **One consequence a reader should not have to discover.** **An Active/Bench Drop can no longer *raise* a Team's Maximum Bid under any circumstance.** Nothing returns to Cap Space on any release, so only three directions remain, and FR-43's existing table states all three: Active/Bench **lowers** it by the $1,000,000 reserve on the freed hole, Injury Reserve leaves it **unchanged**, Minor League ~~**raises** it via Minors Exposure~~ **leaves it unchanged too** *(corrected 2026-09-18 — there are now only two directions, and the table says so)*. §10 example **41** exists only to demonstrate the removed exception and is **retired** rather than deleted.

Washington dropped Malique Lewis in Fantrax on 2026-09-16 and a Commissioner recorded it; his imported row read `2RK` with a full five-year term unelapsed, so the row was deleted and the Team was handed back **$1,000,000 it should have kept charging**. The row has since been restored as Dead Money directly in production. The code shipped first — commit `5222c13` — and this document is the last thing to catch up.
>
> **Amended 2026-09-16 (seventh pass), the same day as the sixth**, when the commissioner asked for a **grace period on bids**. This one **reverses** a requirement rather than removing an exception from one, and it is the only reversal in this document's history:
>
> - **FR-15 is now a rule, not a prohibition.** It read *No voluntary bid retraction* from the first draft, and on 2026-09-08 it was narrowed to *no **voluntary** retraction* **specifically so that FR-40's system cancellation could not be read as a Team-invokable one** — "the Team's own options are unchanged." A Team may now **retract its own Bid within ninety seconds**, at no cost, and the prohibition survives only outside that window. The number is kept: same question, different answer.
> - **The window is bounded by the other Team, not by the clock alone.** It **ends early** the moment the displaced Team's position moves in a direction that could make restoring it fail — it bids, wins a Close, wins a Draw, has a Bid cancelled, or has a Trade, Drop or Move recorded against it. A change that only makes restoration **easier** — capital released because it was outbid elsewhere — leaves the window open. **The test is directional**, and a rule written as "has anything changed?" would be both wrong and more annoying.
> - **A retraction takes the void's treatment, not the cancellation's.** The Bid is **erased** from the Auction's standing, the **Auction Clock is restored** to what it displaced, and the **League Clock reset is removed**. It is the third thing that stands a Bid down, and the three must stay distinguishable: a **void** names a Commissioner and a reason, a **cancellation** names the Close that caused it, a **retraction** names a Manager and carries **no reason at all**.
> - **The length is load-bearing, not a round number.** Ninety seconds is under the reaction time this product already claims for itself (§2.1, UJ-1), which is what keeps a *bystander* from acting on a price and being stranded when it is withdrawn. The cut-short protects the displaced Team; only the window's length protects everybody else.
>
> **Four consequences a reader should not have to discover.** First, **there is no floor and no cooldown** — a Team may re-bid the same Player immediately at a *lower* amount, which costs the league nothing, because the Bid Board is open outcry and the number it is bidding against was already public. Second, **restoring the clock can close the Auction immediately**: a Bid placed seconds before expiry and retracted a minute later hands the Auction back with its clock already past, and it closes at the next sweep — late, not wrong (§10 example 48). Third, **a retraction can shorten the Auction Phase**, because removing the League Clock reset is the void's behaviour and the recomputation runs prospectively (FR-22, §10 example 27). Fourth, and the one nobody would guess: **a Bid placed within ninety seconds of your own retraction does not reset the League Clock at all**, because without that rule a Team could hold the Auction Phase open indefinitely and for free by joining and re-joining a $1,000,000 lottery. Examples **47–57** make all of it executable — 54 and **55** together on the League Clock rule, since 54 alone cannot tell a correct implementation of it from one that reopens the stall.
>
> **Nothing had been built when this was written.** The PRD leads for the first time since the fifth pass, and FR-15's rewrite is the specification rather than the record of one.

> **Amended 2026-09-18 (tenth pass)** when the league corrected a rule this document had held since 2026-08-16, and had built six requirements on. It is the **largest** correction here and the **second** reversal, and unlike the seventh pass it reverses a rule that had already shipped:
>
> - **You cannot win a Free Agent straight into your minors.** *“You have to be able to fit them on your active roster first, and then you can move them into your minors to reopen the space.”* Every Auction win lands in an **Active/Bench Slot** at the **full winning amount** — whatever the Player's Minor League Eligibility, however empty the Team's minors, and with no Manager or Commissioner choice (§3 **Slot Placement**, FR-21).
> - **FR-35 is retired, and the whole of its arithmetic with it.** There is no unbounded Maximum Bid, no *“no cap limit”* on any auction page, and no `Eligible Leading Bids`, `Overflow Count`, `Minors Exposure` or `Active/Bench Overflow` — all four are permanently zero and are kept in §3 only so a reader meeting the names downstream is told they compute nothing. **FR-14's flat commitment is the whole rule again**: every leading amount, plus $1,000,000 per Minimum-Bid Contention, at full value, for the Auction's whole life. A Team at Roster Count 12 can bid on **nobody** (FR-37), and a Team with no Free Active/Bench Slot can enter **no** lottery (FR-18).
> - **The $0 Cap Hit is real, and it arrives later.** It is **FR-44**'s — a Manager's own Roster Move, available only once the Player is on the roster — and FR-44 is now the **only** route into a Minor League Slot. So the stash survives in full; what died is the idea that it could pay for the win.
> - **Minor League Eligibility is no longer an input to any bidding-time rule.** It survives in FR-44, and in FR-41's re-placement of a Contract arriving by Trade — which is **unchanged**, because a traded Contract is already settled rather than being won onto a roster.
>
> **Two consequences a reader should not have to discover.** First, **the direction of §10 example 45 inverts**: the Roster Move that used to *buy* $10,000,000 of bidding power now *costs* $2,000,000 of it, which is what everybody's intuition said in the first place. Second, **a cap refusal has to name the Auctions holding the money** — FR-35 required that naming and took it away when it went, so **FR-13** now requires it back. §10 examples **16–22, 25 and 35 are retired in place**, each with a regression that fails if the old rule returns; **38, 43 and 45 are rewritten**.

> **This pass follows the code, and did so deliberately.** Commit `7bb7a26` shipped first because the old gates were permitting bids mid-auction that the league's rule forbids, and a wrong gate in a live auction decides who gets a Player. **No settled data needed repair** — no auction on a Minor League Eligible Free Agent had completed. §12 records the reversal, and the false premise behind the 2026-08-16 decision that created it.
>
> **Amended 2026-09-17 (ninth pass)** when the notification path was found still running the rule **FR-9 stopped using on 2026-09-14**. FR-9 was rewritten that day — a Nomination Slot is released by **winning** a Player, and by nothing else — and FR-27 was never brought with it. This pass changes no auction rule. It corrects what the app *says*, and it removes a setting:
>
> - **A Close mentions the winner, and nobody else.** It used to mention the nominating Team as well, and tell them their Slot had been released. Under FR-9 as it now stands that is false for exactly the Manager it was addressed to: nominating and losing **keeps** the Slot held. The nominator is no longer a party to the Close. The public post still records it (FR-26).
> - **The Slot release rides the winner's own line** rather than being a notice of its own. It is the same event, to the same Team, about the same Auction — they led the Auction into its Close *and* their Slot came back — and the mention states both, conditioned on whether that Team was holding a Slot at all.

The settings surface now states that nothing is mutable rather than presenting a control that can only be refused.

>
> **This pass follows the code rather than leading it.** The rule changed on 2026-09-14; the copy and the setting were corrected on 2026-09-17, and this document is the last of the three to catch up. §12 records the superseded 2026-08-17 decision rather than overwriting it.

## 1. Vision

For five offseasons the BBSL has run its free agent auction by hand. The rules are genuinely good — an open ascending auction with a rolling 24-hour clock, a nomination system that paces the market, a minimum-salary lottery that gives every team a shot at depth, and a contract-length allotment that forces real dynasty tradeoffs. The rules are also, run manually, a part-time job. Somebody has to notice a clock expired. Somebody has to check whether a team can actually afford the bid it just posted. Somebody has to remember that a team already spent its 4-year contract. And when somebody gets it wrong at 1am, thirty people find out in the morning.

This app runs that auction. It is a responsive web app the whole league lives in for the two or three weeks the auction takes: nominate a player, watch the board, place a bid, get told the instant you're outbid. It knows every team's cap space to the dollar, knows exactly what each team is allowed to bid right now, and refuses invalid bids at the moment they're attempted rather than after the damage is done. It runs the clocks itself. It draws the minimum-salary lottery with a seed anyone can verify. It ends the auction on its own when the league goes quiet for 48 hours, walks every manager through assigning contract years against their allotment, and hands the commissioner a file to upload back into Fantrax.

The point is not automation for its own sake. The point is that the commissioner gets to play in their own league again, and that when a team loses a player by $500k at 4 in the morning, nobody has to wonder whether the app got it right.

## 2. Target User

Thirty-one people: thirty team managers (one team is co-managed by two people) plus one of them wearing a commissioner hat. They are adults with jobs who check their phone. They have been in this league five years and care about it disproportionately.

### 2.1 Jobs To Be Done

- **Don't let me lose a player because I wasn't looking.** Tell me the moment I'm outbid, on the channel I actually read.
- **Tell me what I can afford, right now, without me doing arithmetic.** I have pre-existing contracts, roster holes to fill, and live bids out on three other players. Just show me my number.
- **Stop me from making an illegal bid** before I make it, not after everyone's seen it.
- **Let me bid from my phone in ninety seconds** — I'm not opening a laptop to move $500k.
- **Make the lottery provably fair.** When I lose a $1M coinflip I want to be able to check.
- **Let me nominate strategically** — put a player on the board to shake loose someone else's cap space, without being forced to bid on him.
- **(Commissioner) Let me run this without being the timer, the calculator, and the referee.** And when something does go wrong, let me fix it with a record of what I did.
- **(Commissioner) Get the results back into Fantrax** without transcribing 60 contracts by hand.

### 2.2 Non-Users (v1)

- **Other dynasty leagues.** This is single-tenant and hard-wired to BBSL rules. Making the ruleset configurable is a v2 conversation, not a v1 constraint.
- **Spectators / the public.** No anonymous read-only view of the board.
- **In-season users.** This app runs the offseason auction and then goes quiet. Waivers, lineups, and scoring all stay in Fantrax — as does the *execution* of a trade. The app records a trade's effect on rosters (FR-41) because that effect changes what both Teams may bid, and detects one it was not told about (FR-42). It brokers none.

### 2.3 Key User Journeys

- **UJ-1. Marcus gets sniped at work and answers in ninety seconds.**
  Marcus manages a rebuilding team with real cap room and has been leading on a starting center at $14M for most of a day. Already signed in on his phone from a previous session. Mid-afternoon his phone buzzes — Discord, where the league channel has just named him: he's been outbid at $14.5M. He taps through to the player's auction page. The page shows the current price, the bid history with team names, the clock now reading 23h 58m, and — the part that matters — **his** maximum allowable bid of $19M, already net of the two other auctions he's leading and the $2M he must reserve for his last two roster holes. He taps the $15M quick-bid chip and confirms. The board updates; the clock resets to 24h; the Discord channel announces it. Total elapsed: under two minutes, standing in a hallway. **Edge case:** had he tried $19.5M, the app would have refused before submission and shown him the arithmetic — cap space, committed bids, roster reserve — rather than a bare "invalid bid."

- **UJ-2. Dana takes a flyer on a rookie and wins a coinflip she can audit.**
  Dana's team is capped out and she needs bodies at the minimum. She opens the board on her laptop and nominates a fringe wing nobody has mentioned, opening at $1,000,000. The player card immediately shows a distinct **Minimum-Bid Contention** state: a lottery badge, a clock that reads *24h, does not reset*, and a list of contending teams — currently just hers. Over the next day three other managers add their own $1M bids; each appears in the contention list, the clock keeps counting down untouched. At expiry the app draws. Dana loses. The player page now shows the winner, the four contenders, the seed the draw used, and the resulting draw order — she can re-run the check herself and see her name wasn't third for convenient reasons. She's annoyed but not suspicious. **Edge case:** had anyone bid $1.5M during that day, the lottery would have dissolved on the spot, the clock would have reset to a fresh 24h, and the auction would have continued under ordinary ascending rules.

- **UJ-3. Priya and her co-manager split a team without stepping on each other.**
  Priya co-manages with her brother; they each have their own login bound to the same team. She opens the board and sees their team is leading on two players — the bid history shows **her brother's name** on one, not just the team name. Their shared max-bid figure already accounts for both. She wants a third player; the app tells her the team's remaining nomination slot is already spent on a player still sitting unbid, so she can't nominate — but she can still bid on anything on the board. She bids, and the notification that goes out to the league names her specifically. **Edge case:** if her brother is composing a bid on the same player at the same moment, the second bid to land is validated against the state the first one created, and one of them is told the price moved.

- **UJ-4. Meakel closes the books and gets it back into Fantrax.**
  As commissioner, Meakel gets the notification that 48 hours have passed with no nomination and no bid. The app has moved the league into the contract-assignment phase and every manager has been told they have a deadline to spend their year allotment. He watches the completion tracker fill in — 26 of 30 teams done, four nudged. When the last one lands, he opens the export screen, reviews the full contract table on screen, and downloads the Fantrax-shaped CSV. He uploads it through Fantrax commissioner controls, comes back, and marks the auction archived. **Edge case:** two teams miss the deadline; he uses commissioner controls to assign their remaining players 1-year deals, and the override is stamped with his name and reason in the audit log for anyone to read.

## 3. Glossary

*Downstream workflows and readers must use these terms exactly. Introducing a synonym anywhere is a discipline violation.*

- **League** — the BBSL. Exactly one, containing 30 Teams. Single-tenant.
- **Team** — one of 30 roster-holding entities. Has a Salary Cap, a set of Contracts, and one or more Managers.
- **Manager** — a human user account. 31 exist. Each is bound to exactly one Team; one Team has two Managers.
- **Commissioner** — a Manager additionally holding league-administrative privileges. Exactly one. Retains full Manager rights over their own Team.
- **Salary Cap** — $165,000,000. Identical for every Team.
- **Contract** — a Player held by a Team at a stated annual Cap Hit for a stated number of years. Contracts predating this auction are **Existing Contracts**; those created by it are **Auction Contracts**.
- **Cap Hit** — the annual dollar amount a Contract charges against the Salary Cap. Players in Minor League Slots have a Cap Hit of $0; players in Injury Reserve Slots have their full Cap Hit.
- **Cap Space** — Salary Cap minus the sum of all Cap Hits currently on a Team's roster.
- **Committed Bids** — the money a Team must hold against its cap for auctions still open: the **plain sum of its leading amounts on every open Auction**, plus **$1,000,000 for each Minimum-Bid Contention in which it is a Contender**, since any Contender may win. **Minor League Eligibility does not enter this figure.** Every Auction win lands in an Active/Bench Slot (see **Slot Placement**), and until an Auction closes its leader cannot move the Player anywhere at all — so a leading Bid holds its **full amount** for the Auction's whole life, whatever the Player's eligibility and however empty the Team's minors. See FR-14, FR-18. Committed capital is released when a Team ceases to be Leading Bidder **for any reason** — by being outbid, by a Bid Cancellation under FR-40, or by a Bid Retraction under FR-15 — and is re-committed for a Restored Leading Bidder. *(Rewritten 2026-09-18. From 2026-08-17 it split by eligibility and carried a **Minors Exposure** term; see §12.)*
- **Available Cap Space** — Cap Space minus Committed Bids.
- **Free Minor League Slots (M)** — 3 minus the number of Minor League Slots the Team currently occupies. A real fact about a roster, and since 2026-09-18 it is **read by FR-44 alone**: it is the room a Manager has to move down a Contract the Team already holds. It is **not** an input to any bidding-time rule, and it never authorises a Bid — a Free Minor League Slot cannot receive a won Player.
- **~~Eligible Leading Bids (N)~~, ~~Overflow Count~~, ~~Minors Exposure~~, ~~Active/Bench Overflow~~ — RETIRED 2026-09-18.** The four were terms of a single arithmetic: how much of a Team's eligible leading money would **overflow out of the minors** and have to be paid after all. Nothing lands in the minors at a close, so nothing overflows out of it, and all four are now permanently **zero over an empty set**. They are recorded here rather than deleted because this document, its §10 examples and the shipped code were written in them for a month, and a reader meeting the names downstream needs to be told they compute nothing. **Committed Bids is the plain sum it was before them, and Projected Active/Bench Additions and Reserve Additions each lost their overflow term.** See §12.
- **Roster Slot** — a position on a Team's roster. Three kinds: **Active/Bench Slot** (**exactly 12 per Team — 8 active plus 4 bench**; incurs Cap Hit), **Injury Reserve Slot** (2 per Team, incurs Cap Hit, does **not** count toward the 12), **Minor League Slot** (3 per Team, no Cap Hit, does not count toward the 12, requires a Minor League Eligible player).
- **Roster Count** — the number of players a Team holds in Active/Bench Slots. Injury Reserve and Minor League players are excluded. **Bounded by 12 at both ends: a Team must reach 12 before the export will accept it (FR-30) and can never exceed 12 (FR-37).**
- **Free Active/Bench Slots** — `12 − Roster Count`. The Active/Bench counterpart to Free Minor League Slots, and the plain-language answer to "how many roster holes does that Team still have". It never authorises a Bid on its own — Roster Capacity (FR-37) is the gate — but it is **the base the Outstanding Bid Allowance is measured against**, so every surface publishing it must publish the Team's outstanding commitments beside it. Without both figures a manager cannot tell whether they may bid. See FR-39.
- **Roster Capacity** — the constraint that a Bid must satisfy `Free Active/Bench Slots ≥ 1` *and* `Projected Active/Bench Additions ≤ Free Active/Bench Slots + Outstanding Bid Allowance`. A Bid that would breach it is refused regardless of the Team's cap position, because there is no Slot to place the won Player in. Independent of Maximum Bid: a Team can fail this check with unlimited money and pass it with none. See FR-37. *(Amended 2026-09-18: it carried an alternative first branch, `Projected Active/Bench Additions = 0`, which is unreachable for a Bid now that every win lands in Active/Bench. It remains reachable for the roster acts of FR-41 and FR-44, which project no Bid.)*
- **Outstanding Bid Allowance** — the **one** Bid a Team may hold beyond its Free Active/Bench Slots, so that a manager pursuing two Players need not wait for one Auction to close before entering the next. It exists for auction speed and nothing else. It requires **at least one** Free Active/Bench Slot: a Team with none holds no Active/Bench-bound commitment at all, because it could win the allowance Bid with nothing available to cancel it first. It is **not a thirteenth Roster Slot** — the surplus is taken back under FR-40 — and it is constrained by cap space exactly like any other Bid.
- **Bid Cancellation** — the system standing down a Team's Bid, without that Team's consent and without a Commissioner acting, because an Auction Close has left the Bid with no Roster Slot to land in. Distinct from a Commissioner void (FR-32) in three ways: the cancelled Bid **was valid when placed**, it **remains** in the Auction's history rather than being erased from the fold, and it **keeps** its League Clock reset. See FR-40.
- **Restored Leading Bidder** — the Team whose earlier Bid becomes the leading Bid again after a Bid Cancellation, and whose capital is re-committed at that moment. A Team is restored only if it still passes the money and capacity gates; otherwise the next Bid down is tried.
- **Slot Placement** — the automatic assignment of a won Player to a Roster Slot at Auction Close. **Every won Player takes an Active/Bench Slot**, whatever his Minor League Eligibility and however empty the Team's minors: a Team has to be able to fit a Player on its active roster before it can win him. No Manager or Commissioner choice is involved. Only afterwards may a Manager move him down under **FR-44**, which is what reopens the Active/Bench Slot — and that is the **only** route into a Minor League Slot. *(Rewritten 2026-09-18. It read that a Minor League Eligible Player took a free Minor League Slot if one existed; see §12.)*
- **Roster Trade** — the Commissioner recording that one or more Contracts have changed hands between two Teams, as one act. **Both Existing Contracts and Auction Contracts may move.** The Contract itself travels unchanged — same value, same years; what changes is which Team holds it and, possibly, which Roster Slot receives it. **Slot Placement is re-evaluated against the receiving Team's occupancy at the moment of the move**, so a Minor League Eligible Player stashed at a $0 Cap Hit on the sending Team lands in an Active/Bench Slot on a receiving Team with no Free Minor League Slot — and charges his full amount there. Cap Hit follows placement, never the other way round. A Roster Trade never transfers Committed Bids, a Minimum-Bid Contention entry, or Cap Space, and is **refused** outright if it would leave either Team failing a gate it currently passes. See FR-41. *(**Unchanged in substance on 2026-09-18, and deliberately so** — but read “Slot Placement is re-evaluated” as **FR-41’s own rule** rather than as the **Slot Placement** entry above, which since that date sends every *won* Player to Active/Bench. A Contract arriving by Trade is already settled — bought, priced and rostered — rather than being won onto a roster, so it is re-placed against the receiving Team’s occupancy exactly as described here. §12.)*
- **Roster Move** — a Team moving its **own** Contracts between its **own** Roster Slots, with no Contract changing hands and no counterparty. One act, one evaluation at the end. Only a Minor League Eligible Contract may occupy a Minor League Slot; Injury Reserve is rearrangeable by the Commissioner only *(FR-44, amended 2026-09-23)* and Dead Money is not a Slot. **Cap Hit follows placement** exactly as it does in a Trade, so a Move is a deliberate cap decision: a Contract promoted into a Minor League Slot charges $0, and one demoted to Active/Bench charges its full amount. A Move is the **only** act by which a Manager changes their own Team's cap position without bidding. See FR-44. *(The name was FR-41's until 2026-09-12, when that requirement became the Roster Trade.)*
- **Roster Divergence** — a difference between the Team membership Fantrax reports and the membership the app holds, excluding Players won in this auction, who are not yet in Fantrax. A divergence is a **signal, not a change**: it raises a proposed Roster Trade or Drop for the Commissioner to confirm or dismiss, and never alters app state on its own. See FR-42.
- **Drop** — a Team releasing a Player in Fantrax. It frees the Roster Slot he occupied but **does not release his Cap Hit**, which continues to charge as **Dead Money**. A Drop from an Active/Bench Slot therefore *lowers* the Team's Maximum Bid: Roster Count falls, so Roster Reserve rises by $1,000,000 for the new hole, while Cap Space is unchanged. A Drop from a Minor League Slot moves it **nowhere at all**: the Player was charging $0, so nothing is released; Roster Count never counted him, so no hole opens; and the Minor League Slot he gives back buys no bidding power. See FR-43. *(The Minor League case read "moves it the other way" until 2026-09-18, through a Minors Exposure that no longer exists.)*
- **Dead Money** — the Cap Hit a dropped Player was **charging**, which continues to charge against the Salary Cap at that same amount. It occupies **no Roster Slot**, counts toward **no ceiling**, and can never receive a won Player. It is a fourth Roster Slot *kind* in the data and not a fourth *Slot*: Active/Bench, Injury Reserve and Minor League Slots are finite and allocable, and Dead Money is neither. Because a stashed Player charges $0 by placement, dropping him produces **no Dead Money at all**. Within this auction the charge is simply the charge; the Contract's remaining years are carried for the export and are **not** a future-year projection, which stays out of scope (§6).
- **Rookie-Scale Contract** — a Contract carrying Fantrax's `NRK` designation, where `N` is the draft round and the year is the end year: `2RK31` is a second-round deal ending in 2031. The designation is parsed at import and **survives end to end** — `2RK31` and `2031` are distinguishable everywhere the app carries a Contract, and the round is recorded in a Drop's own record. **It decides nothing.** It is a fact about the Contract and not an input to any rule: a rookie-scale Contract becomes Dead Money on a Drop exactly like every other Contract. *(Until 2026-09-16 a second-round deal of the current draft class was the one Contract whose Cap Hit cleared entirely. That exception is removed — see FR-43.)*
- **Projected Active/Bench Additions** — the number of Active/Bench Slots a Team's open bids will fill in the worst case, counting the bid being placed: its leading amounts on **every** open Auction, computed as though the prospective bid were already placed. Every win lands in Active/Bench, so **every lead projects exactly one addition** and eligibility changes nothing. **Minimum-Bid Contention entries are excluded** — however many the Team holds. A contention entry is governed by FR-18's own capacity rule and contributes nothing to this count. *(This is the **capacity** figure and, since 2026-09-18, it is the only thing it is. Roster Reserve reads **Reserve Additions** below — see that entry for why one figure could not serve both. The **Active/Bench Overflow** term it used to carry was retired the same day, with the rest of the overflow arithmetic.)*
- **Reserve Additions** — the number of Active/Bench Slots a Team's outstanding **capital** has already funded, counting the bid being placed: its leading amounts on every open Auction **including its Minimum-Bid Contention entries**. Identical to Projected Active/Bench Additions but for the entries, and they differ because the two figures answer two questions. Capacity asks how many Slots a Team may be *rationed* against, and FR-18 answers that a lottery entry is rationed against none. Roster Reserve asks how many Slots a Team must still *fund*, and a held entry funds one already — its $1,000,000 sits in Committed Bids from the instant it is placed. **Added 2026-09-18, correcting a defect:** with entries excluded here too, the reserve held back a second $1,000,000 for the very Slot that committed capital would fill, so a minimum bid cost a Team its full $1,000,000 of Maximum Bid while a $1,100,000 bid cost $100,000 — offering more money left a Team with more room to bid. Neither outcome wants the second charge: if the entry wins, the committed $1,000,000 bought the Slot; if it loses, the $1,000,000 is released and the hole is reserved again.
- **Roster Reserve** — money a Team must hold back to fill its remaining Active/Bench holes at the minimum: `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − (Roster Count + Reserve Additions))`.
- **Maximum Bid** — the largest legal bid a Team may place at this instant: `Available Cap Space − Roster Reserve`. It is **one figure per Team**, governing every open Auction alike: there is no unbounded branch and no per-Auction variation, because no Player costs less to win than the amount bid for him. **Maximum Bid governs money only; Roster Capacity is a separate gate a Bid must also pass (FR-37), and passing either never exempts a Bid from the other.** *(Rewritten 2026-09-18. From 2026-08-17 it was a per-Auction figure, **unbounded** on a Minor League Eligible Player a Free Minor League Slot could absorb; see §12.)*
- **Minor League Eligible** — a Player who has played fewer than 82 lifetime NBA games. Required to occupy a Minor League Slot. **The Fantrax export does not carry this flag, so it is Commissioner-set app data (FR-38), applied before the auction opens and defaulting to *not* eligible.** **Since 2026-09-18 it is not an input to any bidding-time rule.** It survives in exactly one place — **FR-44**, deciding who may occupy a Minor League Slot once a Contract is already on a roster — and in FR-41's re-placement of a Contract arriving by Trade.
- **Free Agent** — a Player in the imported pool, not under Contract to any Team, and not yet won in this auction.
- **Nomination** — the act of placing a Free Agent onto the Bid Board. Does not obligate the nominating Team to bid.
- **Nomination Slot** — a Team's right to have one Nomination in play. Released when that Team **wins a Player** — any Player, not necessarily the one it nominated. Losing the auction for your own nomination does not give it back.
- **Bid Board** — the set of all Players with open auctions.
- **Auction** — the contest for a single nominated Player. Has a state: **Awaiting Opening Bid**, **Standard Contention**, **Minimum-Bid Contention**, or **Closed**.
- **Opening Bid** — the first Bid placed on an Auction. Minimum $1,000,000. Starts the Auction Clock.
- **Bid** — a Team's offer on an Auction. Must be a whole multiple of **$500,000**. Because that equals the Minimum Increment, every legal Bid in Standard Contention is exactly one or more increments above the current high; a sub-increment Bid is necessarily also off-grid.
- **Leading Bidder** — the Team holding the highest Bid on an Auction in Standard Contention.
- **Minimum Increment** — $500,000. The least a new Bid must exceed the current high Bid by in Standard Contention.
- **Bid Retraction** — a Team withdrawing its own Bid inside the Retraction Window (FR-15). The **third** way a Bid stops leading other than being outbid, alongside a Commissioner void (FR-32) and a system Bid Cancellation (FR-40), and the only one the bidding Team itself invokes. A retracted Bid is **erased** from the Auction's standing — the void's treatment, not the cancellation's — while remaining visible in the Audit Log.
- **Retraction Window** — the ninety seconds following a Bid, during which its Team may retract it. **Derived, never stored**, and measured from that Bid's own timestamp rather than from any later event, so being restored to leadership never grants a fresh one. It **ends early** the moment the displaced Team's position moves in a direction that could make restoring it fail (FR-15).
- **Auction Clock** — the 24-hour countdown whose expiry closes an Auction.
- **Minimum-Bid Contention** — the Auction state entered when the Opening Bid is exactly $1,000,000. Additional Teams may join by bidding exactly $1,000,000; the Auction Clock does **not** reset; the winner is drawn by Randomizer at expiry.
- **Contender** — a Team that has bid exactly $1,000,000 into a Minimum-Bid Contention.
- **Randomizer** — the seeded, auditable random draw that selects the winner of a Minimum-Bid Contention.
- **Standard Contention** — the ordinary ascending-auction state. Minimum Increment applies; every valid Bid resets the Auction Clock.
- **Auction Close** — the moment an Auction's Clock expires and a winner is determined.
- **League Clock** — the 48-hour countdown, reset by any Nomination or Bid, whose expiry ends the Auction Phase.
- **Auction Phase** — the period from auction open until the League Clock expires.
- **Contract Assignment Phase** — the period after the Auction Phase in which Teams choose contract lengths for the Players they won.
- **Year Allotment** — each Team's per-offseason budget of contract lengths: one 4-year, one 3-year, two 2-year, unlimited 1-year.
- **League Median** — the middle value of a figure across all 30 Teams, used only on the Teams index (FR-39) as a comparison line. **With an even number of Teams it is the lower of the two middle values, not their mean.** The conventional mean-of-two-middles can land halfway between two grid values — a median of $4,000,000 and $4,500,000 is $4,250,000 — which is off the $500,000 grid and therefore *not* losslessly renderable at one decimal place, breaking the property that makes abbreviated money safe everywhere else in the product. The lower middle value is always a figure some real Team actually holds, and is always on the grid. See §10 example 28.
- **Audit Log** — the append-only, league-visible record of every Nomination, Bid, Bid Retraction, Bid Cancellation, Auction Close, Randomizer draw, and Commissioner override.

## 4. Features

### 4.1 League Setup and Fantrax Import

**Description:** Before the auction opens, the Commissioner loads the league's starting state from Fantrax. Fantrax offers no supported write API and does not expose salary or contract data through its documented read surface, so v1 uses a CSV round-trip: the Commissioner exports from Fantrax commissioner controls and uploads the files here. The app validates them hard and refuses to open the auction on a bad import — a silently wrong cap figure discovered mid-auction is unrecoverable. Import is re-runnable up until the auction opens, and locked afterward except through §4.10 overrides. Realizes UJ-4.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-1: Import team rosters and existing contracts

Commissioner can upload Fantrax-exported roster/salary files establishing, for all 30 Teams, their Existing Contracts, each Contract's Cap Hit, and each player's Roster Slot kind. **Fantrax exports one file per Team, so this is thirty files, supplied together as a batch — not one file covering the league.**

**Consequences (testable):**
- System computes and displays each Team's Cap Space as `$165,000,000 − Σ(Cap Hits)`, treating Minor League Slot players as $0.
- System resolves each file to exactly one of the 30 Teams, and rejects the file — naming the file, not a row — when it resolves to no Team or to a Team already supplied.
- System rejects the import and names the offending row when a Team's computed Cap Space is negative, a required column is absent, a Team ID does not resolve to one of the 30 Teams, or **a Team's Roster Count exceeds 12, its Injury Reserve players exceed 2, or its Minor League players exceed 3** — the slot ceilings of FR-37 must hold from the starting state onward.
- System reports parse status per file and **names** every Team still outstanding rather than reporting a count.
- A single Team's file can be re-supplied to replace that Team's rows without re-supplying the other twenty-nine.
- System reports, before commit, a per-Team preview of Roster Count and Cap Space across all 30 Teams for Commissioner confirmation. Commit is all-or-nothing; a partially imported League is never a reachable state.
- Re-importing before auction open replaces prior state entirely; re-importing after auction open is refused.

#### FR-2: Import the free agent pool

Commissioner can upload the Fantrax free agent export, establishing the set of Players eligible for Nomination.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Each imported Player carries a stable Fantrax player ID, name, position, and NBA team.
- **Minor League Eligible is not imported.** The Fantrax export does not carry the flag; every imported Player defaults to **not** eligible and the Commissioner sets it by hand under FR-38.
- A Player appearing both in the free agent pool and on a Team's roster is rejected as a conflict, naming the Player and Team.
- System reports the imported pool size for confirmation before commit.

#### FR-3: Open the auction

Commissioner can transition the League from setup to Auction Phase.

**Consequences (testable):**
- System refuses to open unless the FR-2 pool import and all thirty FR-1 Team imports are committed and every Team has at least one bound Manager. Anything outstanding is named.
- System reports the count of Players marked Minor League Eligible (FR-38) for explicit Commissioner confirmation before opening. It does **not** block on that count: nothing external defines completeness, and the default of *not* eligible is the safe direction.
- On open, all 30 Teams receive an unused Nomination Slot, the League Clock starts at 48 hours, and every Manager is notified.
- Auction open is recorded in the Audit Log with timestamp and actor.

#### FR-38: Set Minor League Eligibility

*(Numbered after FR-37 because it was added when OQ-2 resolved; it belongs to this feature.)*

Commissioner can mark which Players in the Free Agent pool are Minor League Eligible.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Eligibility is app-owned data, not an imported column. Every Player defaults to **not** eligible. *(Until 2026-09-18 the default was load-bearing for the **cap**: an unset flag could otherwise hand out FR-35's unbounded bidding by omission. With FR-35 retired the flag buys no bidding advantage at all, so a wrong setting costs a Manager a **placement** rather than a Player — a real cost, and a far smaller one. §9.)*
- The Commissioner can set and unset the flag on any pooled Player, individually and in bulk, up until the auction opens.
- After the auction opens the flag is locked and changeable only by Commissioner override (FR-32). *(Amended 2026-09-18: the reason was that it changed cap arithmetic under FR-35 for every open Auction on that Player. It no longer touches cap arithmetic anywhere. The lock is kept on its remaining ground — it decides who may occupy a Minor League Slot under FR-44, and a Manager who stashes a Player under one answer should not find it changed under them without a reason in the log.)*
- Every change is written to the Audit Log with actor, Player, before and after values, and — post-open — a reason. Because eligibility is reference data that cap arithmetic folds against, each change is recorded as an event so a state rebuild reproduces the flag as it stood, not as it is now.
- The screen shows, per Player, the flag and the Team-facing consequence in words, so the Commissioner is setting *"a Team that wins this Player may afterwards move him down to a $0 Cap Hit"* rather than an unexplained checkbox. **The wording must not promise a cheaper win:** a won Player is paid for in full whoever he is (FR-14, FR-21), and the flag speaks only to what his Team may do with him next (FR-44).

### 4.2 Identity, Teams, and Roles

**Description:** Thirty-one accounts, thirty teams, one team with two managers. Ceremony is kept to a minimum — this is a private league, not a product with a signup funnel. Every action in the app is attributed to the individual human who took it, not merely to their team, because a co-managed team needs to be able to reconstruct who did what. Realizes UJ-3.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-4: Manager authentication

A Manager can sign in and reach their Team's auction view without a password.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Sign-in is by **Discord OAuth**. The Commissioner pre-registers each Manager's Discord account; no self-service registration exists.
- A Discord account not pre-registered cannot obtain a session.
- A session persists at least 30 days so a returning mobile user is not re-authenticating under time pressure — and so a Discord outage does not log the league out.
- Sign-in records the Manager's Discord user ID, which FR-27 uses to address them. Authentication and notification addressing are the same fact, captured once.
- **This system sends no email for any purpose.** There is no address to verify, no magic link, and no password to reset.
- A Commissioner-only sign-in path that does not depend on Discord must exist, so FR-34's pause remains reachable when Discord is the component that has failed.

#### FR-5: Team binding and co-management

A Manager acts on behalf of exactly one Team; a Team may have more than one Manager.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Both Managers of a co-managed Team see identical Team state — same Cap Space, same Maximum Bid, same Nomination Slot status.
- Every Nomination and Bid records the acting Manager's identity alongside the Team's, and both are shown in the Audit Log.
- Public-facing displays (Bid Board, notifications) show the Team name with the acting Manager's name attached.
- Neither co-Manager can outbid their own Team (see FR-11).

#### FR-6: Commissioner role

The Commissioner holds league-administrative privileges in addition to full ordinary Manager rights over their own Team.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Commissioner-only capabilities (§4.1, §4.9, §4.10) are unreachable by a non-Commissioner Manager, by UI and by direct request alike.
- The Commissioner's own Team is subject to every ordinary rule without exception; no commissioner privilege alters their Cap Space, Maximum Bid, or Nomination Slot.
- Every Commissioner action taken in an administrative capacity is written to the Audit Log with actor, timestamp, and stated reason.

### 4.3 Nomination

**Description:** Nomination paces the auction. Each Team may have one Nomination in play at a time, and gets its Nomination Slot back only by **winning a Player**. Crucially, nominating does not oblige you to bid — you can put a player on the board purely to make rivals spend. The league has explicitly chosen to keep this rule as written, accepting its consequence: a Team that never wins a Player nominates exactly once, for the whole auction. Realizes UJ-2, UJ-3.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-7: Nominate a free agent

A Manager can nominate any Free Agent to the Bid Board when their Team's Nomination Slot is unused.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The nominated Player appears on the Bid Board in **Awaiting Opening Bid** state with no Auction Clock running.
- The Team's Nomination Slot is marked used and shows which Player holds it.
- Nomination does not commit any of the nominating Team's cap space and does not make them Leading Bidder.
- A Team may nominate a Player it could not afford to bid on.
- The League Clock resets to 48 hours.
- The Nomination is written to the Audit Log and broadcast per §4.7.

#### FR-8: Nomination eligibility enforcement

The system refuses Nominations that violate league rules.

**Consequences (testable):**
- A Nomination is refused when the Team's Nomination Slot is in use, naming the Player currently holding it.
- A Nomination is refused when the Player is already on the Bid Board, already won in this auction, or under Contract to any Team.
- A Nomination is refused when the League is not in Auction Phase.
- Two Managers nominating the same Player concurrently result in exactly one Nomination; the loser is told the Player is already on the board.

#### FR-9: Nomination Slot release

A Team's Nomination Slot is released when that Team **wins a Player**, and at no other time.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The Slot is released by any win, not only a win of the Player that Team nominated. A Team that spent its Slot on one Player and then won a different one gets the Slot back on that second Auction's Close.
- The Slot is **not** released by the Close of the Player it nominated when another Team won: losing does not give the Slot back.
- The Slot is **not** released by an Auction that ends with no winner at all — an unbid nomination at Phase End, or a Minimum-Bid Contention whose Contenders were all cancelled (FR-40). Nobody won, so nobody pays a Slot back. The Player returns to the pool; the Slot stays spent.
- A Team holds at most one Slot, so a win releases whichever Slot it holds and a Team holding none is unaffected.
- The Team's Managers are notified that they may nominate again. This is **not a notice of its own**: it is stated on the same mention that reports the Close they won (FR-27), because it is the same event told to the same Team. A winner who was holding no Slot is told what they won and nothing about a Slot.
- A Team that never wins a Player nominates exactly once, for the whole auction. `[ASSUMPTION: this is the intended consequence of the rule, confirmed by the league; the app states it plainly on the nomination surface rather than mitigating it — see FR-10. It supersedes the earlier rule under which the Slot returned at the nominated Player's Auction Close.]`

#### FR-10: Dead nomination visibility

The system makes an unbid Nomination visible to its owner and to the league.

**Consequences (testable):**
- A Player in **Awaiting Opening Bid** for more than 24 hours is visually flagged on the Bid Board as having attracted no bids.
- The nominating Team's own view states plainly that this Nomination is holding their Slot and will continue to until that Team wins a Player.
- **No notification is sent at the 24-hour mark.** This requirement carried one from the first draft; it was **removed from scope on 2026-09-04** as unnecessary rather than deferred, and the PRD is corrected here rather than left promising a message nothing sends. Visibility is the requirement, and the Bid Board flag and the Team's own view are what deliver it. *(§12.)*
- No automatic expiry, withdrawal, or return-to-pool occurs; only a Commissioner override (FR-31) can clear it.

### 4.4 Bidding and Cap Enforcement

**Description:** The heart of the app. A Team may bid on anything on the board up to its Maximum Bid, which the app computes continuously from cap space, money already committed to auctions it is leading, and the money it must hold back to fill its roster to twelve at the minimum salary. The number is always on screen — a manager should never have to derive it. Invalid bids are refused at submission with the arithmetic shown, never silently accepted and reversed later. Realizes UJ-1, UJ-3.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-11: Place a bid

A Manager can bid on any Auction on the Bid Board that their Team is not currently leading.

**Consequences (testable):**
- In Standard Contention, a Bid is valid only if it is at least `current high Bid + $500,000`.
- Every Bid must be a whole multiple of **$500,000**. Since that equals the Minimum Increment, the two rules coincide: no off-grid Bid can be valid, and no grid-valid Bid above the current high can be sub-increment. An implementer should still write both checks — the granularity check is what catches an off-grid amount in **Minimum-Bid Contention**, where the increment rule does not apply.
- A Bid at or below the current high Bid is refused.
- A Team already holding the leading Bid on an Auction cannot bid against itself; the control is disabled and a direct submission is refused.
- On a valid Bid the Team becomes Leading Bidder, the previous Leading Bidder's Committed Bids are released immediately, and the previous Leading Bidder is notified per §4.7.
- A Team may hold **one more leading Bid than it has Free Active/Bench Slots** (FR-37). The bid control states this before the Bid is placed — which Bid is the allowance Bid, and that it is the one at risk of cancellation under FR-40 — rather than leaving a Manager to infer it or to meet it for the first time as a loss.
- The League Clock resets to 48 hours.
- The Bid is written to the Audit Log with Team, Manager, amount, and timestamp.

#### FR-12: Compute and display Maximum Bid

The system continuously computes each Team's Maximum Bid and displays it wherever bidding occurs. *(Amended 2026-09-18: it read **for each open Auction**, because FR-35 made the figure per-Auction. With FR-35 retired it is one figure per Team again.)*

**Consequences (testable):**
- `Maximum Bid = Available Cap Space − Roster Reserve`, where `Available Cap Space = Cap Space − Committed Bids` and `Roster Reserve = $1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − (Roster Count + Reserve Additions))`. **One formula, every Player, every Auction** — Minor League Eligibility is not read here or anywhere else in the bidding gates (2026-09-18). *(Read **Reserve Additions**, not Projected Active/Bench Additions — the two split on 2026-09-18 and differ by a Team's held Minimum-Bid Contention entries. See the Glossary.)*
- Worked: a Team with $12,000,000 Cap Space, Roster Count 9, no leading Bids, bidding on any Player. Reserve Additions = 1, so Roster Reserve = $1,000,000 × (12 − 10) = $2,000,000 and Maximum Bid = $10,000,000.
- Worked: the same Team, now leading two other Auctions at $3,000,000 and $2,000,000. Available Cap Space $7,000,000; Reserve Additions = 3; Roster Reserve $0; Maximum Bid $7,000,000.
- Maximum Bid recomputes for all affected Teams and Auctions within one second of any Bid, Auction Close, or Commissioner override.
- The figure is shown with its components broken out — Cap Space, Committed Bids, Roster Reserve — not as a bare number.
- A Team whose Maximum Bid is below the minimum legal Bid for a given Auction sees its bidding controls disabled there, with the reason stated.
- Maximum Bid is a **money** figure only. A Bid must additionally pass Roster Capacity (FR-37), and a healthy Maximum Bid does not exempt it from that gate; where capacity is the binding constraint the Auction says so rather than showing a money figure the Team cannot in fact use.

#### FR-13: Reject over-cap bids

The system refuses any Bid exceeding the bidding Team's Maximum Bid.

**Consequences (testable):**
- A Bid exceeding Maximum Bid is refused before it enters the Auction; it never appears on the Bid Board or in the Audit Log as a valid Bid.
- The refusal message shows the full arithmetic: Cap Space, Committed Bids, Roster Reserve, and the resulting Maximum Bid.
- **Committed Bids names the Auctions holding the money**, Player by Player and amount by amount, so a Manager refused at $1,500,000 against a Maximum Bid of −$28,000,000 can see *which* of their own leads spent it and act on one rather than guessing. An itemised figure and its total must agree to the dollar. *(New 2026-09-18, and it is a **restoration**. FR-35 required an Overflow refusal to name the specific earlier Auction creating the exposure, and `exposingBids` carried that set; when the overflow arithmetic was retired the naming went with it and the refusal became a bare pair of numbers. The Team's `leading` list still holds every Player name, so nothing was lost but the requirement to print it. It is filed **here**, in the requirement that governs what a refusal says, rather than in FR-14, which defines what the figure **is** — the capability that was lost was a refusal's, and it is at the refusal that a Manager needs it. §12.)*
- Validation is performed server-side against committed state at the moment of submission, not against state the client held when the page rendered.
- A Bid valid when composed but invalid by the time it lands (because another Auction the Team leads was outbid-then-reclaimed, or the price moved) is refused with the current figures shown.

#### FR-14: Commit and release capital

The system holds a Team's leading money against its cap **in full**, for the life of the Auction.

**Consequences (testable):**
- A Team's Committed Bids include its leading amount on **every** open Standard Contention Auction, at the **full** amount, from the instant the Bid is placed.
- A Team's Committed Bids include **$1,000,000 for every open Minimum-Bid Contention in which it is a Contender**, because any Contender may win.
- **Minor League Eligibility is not read here.** There is no eligibility branch, no Minors Exposure term and no conditional commitment: a winner lands in an Active/Bench Slot at the winning amount (FR-21), and he cannot be moved down under FR-44 until the Auction has closed — so there is no instant in an Auction's life at which its leader owes less than what they bid. *(Restored 2026-09-18. This flat rule was FR-14's from the first draft; it was overruled in favour of FR-35 on 2026-08-16 and is now reinstated, the contradiction having resolved the other way. See §12.)*
- Committed capital is released the instant the Team ceases to be Leading Bidder — **whether by being outbid, by a Bid Cancellation under FR-40, or by its own Bid Retraction under FR-15** — or the instant a Minimum-Bid Contention it lost is drawn.
- A **Restored Leading Bidder**'s capital is re-committed at the moment of restoration, at the amount of the Bid being restored. Restoration is refused and the next Bid down tried if that Team can no longer cover it (FR-40).
- On Auction Close the winning Team's committed amount converts to an Auction Contract Cap Hit and its Roster Count increases by one.

#### FR-15: Bid retraction inside a ninety-second window

A Bid may be retracted by the Team that placed it, at no cost, within **ninety seconds** of when that Bid was placed — and only while the Team it displaced is **no harder to restore than when it was displaced**.

*(Retitled 2026-09-16. It read **No voluntary bid retraction** from 2026-08-16, and the narrowing to "no ***voluntary*** retraction" on 2026-09-08 was written specifically to keep the Team locked out while FR-40 let the system act — "the Team's own options are unchanged." That prohibition is now bounded rather than absolute. The number is kept because this is the same rule about the same question — may a Team take its own Bid back — answered differently.)*

**Why it exists.** A Bid is one irreversible keystroke against a six-figure sum, typed on a phone, on a $500,000 grid where the difference between $5,000,000 and $50,000,000 is a single character. Under the old rule a Manager who fat-fingered one had no remedy but a Commissioner override — which means the Commissioner adjudicating typos, and an Audit Log that cannot tell a slip from a change of heart. **The window exists for the typo.** It is not a negotiating instrument, and two things stop it becoming one: the cut-short rule below, and its length.

**Why ninety seconds and not five minutes.** A typo is noticed in seconds. Five minutes is long enough for a *third* Team to react to a price and commit its capital elsewhere — and the cut-short protects only the Team that was displaced, never that third party. A Team could post a scare price, watch a rival stand down and spend its money on another Auction, then withdraw and re-bid low. The argument that makes a re-bid floor unnecessary — that the Bid Board is open outcry — does **not** reach this case: it shows that the *displaced* Team's price was already public, and says nothing about a bystander who acted on a price that was never real. Ninety seconds is under the reaction time this product already claims for itself — §2.1's *"let me bid from my phone in ninety seconds"*, and UJ-1 — so a bystander cannot realistically see the price, decide, and commit inside it. It is also, by the same token, comfortably longer than reading back a number you just typed.

**Consequences (testable):**

*Who may retract, and what it costs*
- A Team may retract **only its own most recent Bid on an Auction, and only while that Bid still stands** — in Standard Contention that means it is the Leading Bid; in Minimum-Bid Contention, that the Team is still in the Contender list. A Team that has since been outbid is holding nothing to take back, and is refused on that ground rather than on the window.
- Retraction is **free**: no fee, no cooldown, and **no floor on what the Team may bid next**. It may re-bid the same Player immediately at any legal amount, including one below what it just retracted. The Bid Board is open outcry, so the amount it would be bidding against was already public, and a retract-then-lower sequence gains a Team nothing it could not have had by bidding the lower amount first. A floor would defeat the typo the window exists for.

*The window*
- The window is **ninety seconds from the retracted Bid's own timestamp**, and from nothing later. It is **half-open**: open while less than ninety seconds have elapsed, **closed at exactly `90.000`**. *(Stated 2026-09-16. "Within ninety seconds" did not pin the boundary, and this is the same posture FR-21 takes on an Auction at its close instant — an expiry that has arrived has arrived. **One convention serves both this window and the League Clock suppression below**, so neither needs its own rule and the two cannot drift apart; §10 example 57 works both halves.)* Being restored to leadership does **not** hand a Team a fresh window. This is what bounds a chain of retractions without a depth limit: every step down the history is anchored to a strictly older Bid, so a chain cannot extend itself and must terminate.
- The window is **derived, never stored**. There is no countdown to schedule and nothing for the close sweep to do. A retraction arriving one second late is refused by the same arithmetic that would have allowed it one second earlier — expiry is authoritative here exactly as it is for the Auction Clock (FR-21), and the server's clock decides, not the client's.
- **A pause stops the window** (FR-34). While the auction is paused every Clock stops advancing and Bids are refused, so a window that kept running would expire while the Manager was locked out of acting on it — this product taking a remedy away from someone for a reason wholly outside their control. On resume the window continues with exactly the time it held at pause, which is what FR-34 already promises every other Clock. A retraction is itself **refused while paused**, like any other write.
- **A closed Auction cannot be retracted from, whatever the window says.** In Standard Contention this needs no rule, because a Bid resets the Auction Clock to 24 hours and an Auction therefore cannot close inside its own Bid's window. **In a Minimum-Bid Contention it is reachable**, because a join does not reset the Clock (FR-18): a Team joining at 08:59 against a 09:00 draw holds a window that outlives the Auction. **The draw closes the window**, whether that Team won or lost. A retraction accepted after the draw would unwind a recorded, seed-verifiable result, which is the one outcome FR-20's auditability cannot survive (§10 example 53).
- Retraction exists **only during the Auction Phase**, which is the only phase in which a Bid can be placed. Nothing in the Contract Assignment Phase or after archive can be retracted, and a Phase that ends inside an open window ends it (FR-22).

*The cut-short*
- The window **ends early the moment the displaced Team's restoration figures move in a direction that could make restoring it fail.** Those figures are named and finite — **Cap Space, Committed Bids, Roster Count and Minor League occupancy**, which are exactly the four the restoration re-evaluation reads — and the test is whether the Team is **at least as restorable as it was when the Bid displaced it**. From the first move against it, the retraction is refused and the Bid stands.
- **The test is stated over those figures and not over a list of acts, deliberately, and the list is why.** An enumeration has to be maintained, and this one was found incomplete three times before a line of code existed. It catches the displaced Team **placing a Bid** on any Auction, **winning a Close** and **winning a Draw**. It misses a **Roster Trade, Drop or Roster Move** recorded against it (FR-41, FR-43, FR-44). It also misses a Commissioner **Cap Space adjustment** and a post-open **eligibility change** on a Player it leads (FR-32, FR-38). And least obviously, it misses the Team being **restored on some other Auction**, because FR-40 re-commits a Restored Leading Bidder's capital and so raises its Committed Bids without it acting at all. Every one moves one of the four figures; not one is bid-shaped. **Treat the enumeration as a reader's checklist. The figures are the rule.**
- **A Bid of the displaced Team's being cancelled under FR-40 is *not* a cut-short**, and reading it as one is the mistake this framing prevents. A cancellation **releases** capital, which makes restoration easier, never harder. What is adverse is the **Close that caused it** — a Close the Team won, which is already on the list in its own right.
- **A Drop is the case worth naming explicitly**, because it moves the figures against the Team while looking like relief: it *frees* a Roster Slot and still **lowers** Maximum Bid by putting $1,000,000 of Roster Reserve on the hole it opened (§10 example 40; example 50 works the retraction case).
- A change that can only make restoration **easier** does **not** cut the window short. A displaced Team that is outbid elsewhere, or whose losing lottery is drawn, has capital **released** rather than committed — it is strictly better able to take the Auction back than when it lost it, and there is nothing to protect it from. **The test is directional, not "has anything changed."**
- **This rule is not about fairness to the retracting Team.** FR-40's restorer already re-validates the Team it is about to restore and **skips** one that fails, so a retraction into a changed position cannot breach anything — it would simply hand the Auction to somebody else, or to nobody. The window closes so that the retracting Manager is **refused with a reason** instead of handed an outcome they did not intend and cannot undo.
- An **Opening Bid displaces nobody**, so it has no cut-short subject at all and its window always runs its full ninety seconds. The same is true of a Minimum-Bid Contention entry, which joins rather than displaces (FR-18).

*What a retraction does*
- The retracted Bid is **erased from the Auction's standing**, not retained as history stripped of leadership. This is the Commissioner void's treatment (FR-32), not the cancellation's (FR-40). It is **not deleted** — the Audit Log shows that it was placed and that it was retracted — but it does not appear as a Bid on the Auction and no restoration walk can land on it.
- The **Auction Clock is restored** to the value the retracted Bid displaced. An Auction with three hours left when the Bid arrived has three hours left again, not twenty-four. **If that instant is already past, the Auction closes at the next evaluation** — late, not wrong, exactly as a void behaves.
- The **League Clock reset the Bid earned is removed** and the Auction Phase recomputes **shorter**. The void's treatment again: a Bid that never counted did not buy the league another 48 hours. The recomputation is **prospective only** — if it lands the expiry in the past, the Phase ends at the next evaluation and nothing accepted in the interim is invalidated (FR-22, FR-32, §10 example 27).
- The retracting Team's committed capital is **released** (FR-14).
- **A Bid placed by a Team within ninety seconds of that same Team's own retraction does not reset the League Clock.** **The ninety seconds run from the RETRACTION, not from the Bid that was retracted.** *(Stated 2026-09-16. The rule never said which, and the two readings diverge on a late-window retraction — retract at `T+85s`, re-bid at `T+91s` — where only the retraction-anchored one suppresses. Anchored to the retracted Bid instead, the stall below simply returns: a Team retracts at the end of its window and re-bids past the original Bid's ninety seconds, earning a fresh reset every cycle. §10 example 54 cannot tell the two apart — it re-joins ten seconds after retracting, which both readings suppress — so §10 example 55 is the case that discriminates them.)* Without this, a Team holds the Auction Phase open **forever, for free**. FR-18 resets the League Clock to 48 hours on every lottery join, and a retraction removes only its own reset. Joining a $1,000,000 lottery and retracting it every minute — or alternating between two — therefore renews the only thing that ends the auction, at no cost and with no risk of ever winning anything. Before this requirement existed the same stall was **bounded**, because every join was a commitment the Team kept and might have to honour; retraction is what would have made it free. The rule costs the honest case a little and is stated plainly: **a Manager who mistypes, retracts and re-bids correctly buys the league no extra time.** That is the right answer as well as the safe one — the first Bid's reset was removed with the Bid, and the pair is one act, not two.

*Who gets the Auction back*
- The Auction is handed to its **next-highest surviving Bid by the same selector FR-40 uses**, under the same rules: each candidate is re-evaluated against the money and capacity gates at that instant, and one that fails is **skipped** in favour of the next below.
- **Because the cut-short test is directional and stated over the four restoration figures, the displaced Team is always restorable**, and a retraction gives the Auction back to exactly the Team it outbid — or, where it outbid nobody, returns it to **Awaiting Opening Bid** with no leading Bid and its Auction Clock **cleared**, exactly as FR-40 specifies. The Player stays on the Board and the Nomination Slot stays held (FR-9).
- **The skip is nevertheless still evaluated, and that is deliberate.** Nothing may rely on the cut-short being exhaustive: the two rules are independent, one of them is a test a future requirement can silently outgrow, and the failure mode of trusting it is restoring a Team into a breach of its own cap. If a candidate fails, the next-highest below is tried and so on down the history — the FR-40 walk, unchanged. **A retraction that reaches a Team other than the one it displaced is a signal that the cut-short test has a hole in it**, and should be treated as a defect to investigate rather than an outcome to accept.
- The **Nominator gets no say**, before or after. Nothing in this product gives a Nominator a veto over what becomes of the Auction they started, and a retraction does not invent one.
- A restoration **never** triggers a cancellation, and a retraction is not a cancellation trigger either. FR-40's trigger is still an Auction Close and nothing else.

*In a Minimum-Bid Contention*
- Retracting an entry **removes the Team from the Contender list and releases its $1,000,000**. There is **no restoration** — a lottery has no next-highest Bid — and **no seed is revealed**, because a shrinking Contender list is not a dissolution. Only FR-19's conversion dissolves a contention.
- The **Auction Clock is untouched** while any Contender remains, because a join never reset it (FR-18) and so displaced no clock value to restore. This holds whoever retracts, the Team that placed the **Opening Bid** included: once others have joined, the Clock belongs to the contention rather than to the Team that started it.
- A list reduced to **one** Contender is **not** a special case: FR-20 already resolves a single-Contender lottery to that Contender.
- **A list emptied by retraction returns the Auction to Awaiting Opening Bid with its Clock cleared**, exactly as an exhausted history does anywhere else (FR-40). **This is deliberately not what happens when cancellations empty a list**, which leaves the Clock running and closes with no winner at expiry (FR-20). The two differ because the withdrawals differ, and that is the void-versus-cancellation distinction doing its job rather than an inconsistency: a cancelled Bid stood and its Clock stands with it, while a retracted Bid never counted, so the Clock it started never counted either. An Auction with no Contenders and a running Clock is the state §12 records the commissioner as having ruled out.
- **The Bid that DISSOLVES a contention cannot be retracted at all.** A Bid of $1,500,000 or more converts a Minimum-Bid Contention to Standard Contention, discards the Contender list and **releases every Contender's $1,000,000 immediately** (FR-19). That is not reversible by anything in this product: the Contenders have been told the lottery is over and have had their capital back, with no window and no cut-short protecting them, and re-committing it minutes later on an Auction they had stopped watching is precisely the harm FR-15 exists to avoid causing. **A converting Bid is therefore final on submission**, and the retract control is absent rather than refused. The Commissioner's void (FR-32) remains the only remedy, as it was for every Bid before this requirement existed.

*What has not changed*
- Outside the window there is **no retraction and no control**. A Bid older than ninety seconds, or one whose displaced Team has moved against it, cannot be withdrawn by the Team that placed it under any circumstance.
- There is still **no control to edit or lower a Bid**. Retract-and-rebid is two acts and both are recorded; nothing amends a Bid in place.
- A **Commissioner override (FR-32)** and a **system cancellation (FR-40)** are untouched, and the three withdrawals stay distinguishable in the Audit Log (FR-33, FR-40). What retraction adds to that set is a negative: it carries **no reason field at all**, where a void carries one. Requiring a justification for a typo is how a correction turns into an argument. `[ASSUMPTION: no reason field. Inferred from the requirement's purpose, not stated by it; confirm at UX. §12.]`

*Record*
- Every retraction is written to the **Audit Log and broadcast to the league** per §4.7 (FR-26, FR-27, FR-33). A retraction visible only to the two Teams involved would be a private probing instrument; publishing it is what keeps it a correction. `[ASSUMPTION: league-wide broadcast rather than notifying only the two Teams. Inferred from §4.7's posture; confirm at UX. §12. (Originally read “confirm against FR-27's mute categories” — there are none as of 2026-09-17, so the question is now about the broadcast alone.)]`
- The **restored Team is notified that it is leading again**, having been told minutes earlier that it was outbid. Where the Auction instead returns to Awaiting Opening Bid, the displaced Team is told that, and why.

#### FR-35: Unbounded bidding on Minor League Eligible players, bounded by overflow — RETIRED 2026-09-18

*(Retired, not deleted. The number stays spent so no future requirement reuses it, and a reader arriving from one of the many places that still cite FR-35 needs to learn here what became of it.)*

It read: **a Team may bid without cap limit on a Minor League Eligible Player its Free Minor League Slots can absorb, and is constrained only by the surplus that would overflow into Active/Bench Slots.** A Minor League Slot placement yielded a Cap Hit of $0 whatever the winning amount, so an eligible lead committed nothing while a Slot stood free, the auction page showed *"no cap limit"* instead of a figure, and a Team $2,000,000 under the cap could win a $30,000,000 Player.

**The premise was false, and the league has corrected it.** *"You cannot win a Free Agent straight into your minors. You have to be able to fit them on your active roster first, and then you can move them into your minors to reopen the space."* Every Auction win therefore lands in an **Active/Bench Slot** at the winning amount (**Slot Placement**, FR-21), whatever the Player's eligibility and however empty the Team's minors — and until the Auction closes the leader cannot move him anywhere at all, so a leading Bid holds its **full amount** against the Cap for the Auction's whole life. The $0 Cap Hit is real but it arrives **later**, by a Manager's own act under FR-44, and it is never available to pay for a win.

**Everything this requirement added is gone with it:** the unbounded branch, the overflow arithmetic (`Eligible Leading Bids`, `Overflow Count`, `Minors Exposure`, `Active/Bench Overflow`), the $40,000,000 worked example, the eligible-contention carve-out, and the exposure-naming refusal. **Minor League Eligibility is no longer an input to any bidding-time rule.** FR-14's flat commitment is the whole rule again and FR-12's single formula is the whole of Maximum Bid.

**One bullet survives, and it was always about Fantrax rather than about bidding:**

- **This app does not model or enforce activation.** Moving a stashed Player from a Minor League Slot to an Active/Bench Slot requires the Team to absorb the full contract amount against its cap, and that constraint is enforced in Fantrax during the season, not here. See §6. *(It now hangs off **FR-44**, the only act in this product that puts a Player into a Minor League Slot or takes him out of one. Note what it no longer does: it was the argument that made unbounded bidding *self-correcting*, and there is no longer anything for it to correct.)*

**Where the rest went:** FR-12 and FR-13 (the money figure and the refusal), FR-14 (commitment), FR-37 (capacity), FR-18 (contention entries), FR-21 (placement) and FR-44 (the sole route into a Minor League Slot). §10 examples 16–22, 25 and 35 are retired with it and 38, 43 and 45 rewritten; §12 records the reversal and the false premise behind the decision that created it.


#### FR-37: Enforce Roster Capacity

*(Numbered after FR-36 because it was added when OQ-1 resolved; it belongs to this feature.)*

A Team has exactly 12 Active/Bench Slots — 8 active plus 4 bench — and the system refuses any Bid that would commit it to more players than it has Slots for, **plus one**.

**Consequences (testable):**
- A Bid is refused unless the Team holds at least one Free Active/Bench Slot **and** `Projected Active/Bench Additions ≤ Free Active/Bench Slots + 1` — computed as though the prospective Bid were already placed, the same post-bid basis Roster Reserve uses. *(Amended 2026-09-18. The rule read "**either** `Projected Active/Bench Additions = 0`, **or** …", and the first branch is now **unreachable for a Bid**: every win lands in Active/Bench, so a prospective Bid projects at least one addition and `P = 0` cannot occur. It existed for the eligible win that added nothing to Active/Bench, which is the case that no longer exists. `P = 0` is still reachable for the **roster acts** — FR-41 and FR-44 project no Bid — and the shared evaluator still handles it there; it is stated here as one branch because a Bid only ever takes one.)*
- The `+ 1` is the **Outstanding Bid Allowance**, and it exists to speed the auction: a Manager may pursue one more Player than they have room for rather than idling for a day until a close.
- The allowance is **not a thirteenth Roster Slot.** A Team may never hold more than 12 Active/Bench players. The surplus Bid is cancelled under FR-40 the moment a win leaves it nowhere to land, and a Team can therefore reach Roster Count 12 but never 13.
- **At least one Free Active/Bench Slot is a precondition.** A Team at Roster Count 12 holds no Active/Bench-bound commitment at all, because a Bid it could win would breach the ceiling with no other close available to cancel it first. The allowance extends a Team's reach; it never creates it.
- This is a **second, independent refusal ground alongside Maximum Bid**. A Team can fail it with unlimited cap space and pass it with none; neither check subsumes the other, and both are evaluated on every Bid.
- **A Team at Roster Count 12 cannot bid on any Player at all**, whatever its Cap Space and however many Minor League Slots stand free. Those Slots are real and they are useless to a bidder: the only Player who can enter one is a Player the Team already holds, moved down under FR-44. *(Amended 2026-09-18. This bullet carved out Minor League Eligible Players, on the retired reading that such a win added nothing to Active/Bench.)*
- **Minimum-Bid Contention entries are not subject to this requirement at all.** They are governed by FR-18, contribute nothing to Projected Active/Bench Additions, and do not consume the allowance.
- The refusal message states the capacity arithmetic — Roster Count, Projected Active/Bench Additions, **Free Active/Bench Slots and the allowance** — and never reports a capacity refusal as a cap refusal. A Manager refused at their third outstanding bid with one free Slot must be able to see that the second was the allowance and a third was never available.
- A Team that reaches Roster Count 12 has its bidding controls disabled on **every** Auction on the board, with the reason stated, not only on discovery at submission. This state is reachable directly from import, so it must be announced rather than found.
- A Team **at** its allowance — holding one more outstanding Bid than it has Free Active/Bench Slots — has its controls disabled on the same terms, and the board says it is at the allowance rather than merely out of room. The two conditions have different remedies: one waits for a close, the other cannot bid at all.
- Roster Reserve's `max(0, …)` clamp is **reachable in ordinary play**, whenever a Team bids at its allowance: `Roster Count + Reserve Additions` reaches 13 and the reserve correctly falls to $0. It is not a Commissioner-override-only safeguard, though it still serves that purpose too (FR-32).
- Slot ceilings hold on import as well as on bid: FR-1 rejects a starting state with more than 12 Active/Bench, more than 2 Injury Reserve, or more than 3 Minor League players.

#### FR-44: Rearrange a Roster's Slot Placements

*(New 2026-09-12. It exists because FR-41 creates the need: a Contract arriving from a Trade is placed by FR-21's automatic rule against whatever the receiving Team's occupancy happened to be at that instant, and before this requirement nothing could ever move it again. §10 example 44 is the case. Filed here rather than in §4.10 because it is a **Manager** act whose whole point is its effect on Maximum Bid — the subject of this section.)*

A Manager can move their own Team's Contracts between an Active/Bench Slot and a Minor League Slot, as one act, and the system recomputes the Team's position from it. The Commissioner can do the same on any Team's behalf.

**Since 2026-09-18 this is the *only* route into a Minor League Slot.** No Auction win lands in one (§3 **Slot Placement**, FR-21, FR-35 retired), so every stash in this league is now either an import (FR-1) or a Manager's own act here — plus FR-41's re-placement of a Contract arriving by Trade, which chooses a Slot rather than opening one. That makes this requirement load-bearing in a way it was not when it was written: it is what the corrected league rule *points at*. "Fit them on your active roster first, **and then you can move them into your minors to reopen the space**" is FR-44, named.

**Consequences (testable):**

*What moves*
- A Roster Move names **one Team** and one or more of its own Contracts, each with the Slot it is to occupy. **No Contract changes hands**, there is no counterparty, and no amount is edited.
- It is **one act with one evaluation at the end**, for FR-41's reason and not as a convenience. A swap applied one leg at a time transiently breaches a ceiling that the act itself never breaches — §10 example 44 is that counterfactual, and it is §10 example 39's lesson reached by a different act.
- **Active/Bench and Minor League Slots participate for a Manager; Injury Reserve participates for the Commissioner only.** *(Amended 2026-09-23 to match commit `370c026`. This read “**Injury Reserve is not rearrangeable**: IR is a Fantrax fact about a player's health, not a placement this app assigns, and an app that moved a player out of it would be inventing a medical opinion.”)* For a Manager that reasoning stands. The Commissioner is not inventing a medical opinion but **recording a designation the league has made** — including the league rule that during free agency an IR Slot may hold only a player not expected to start the season, whatever his Fantrax flag. That judgement is the Commissioner's and is never derived by the app. **Dead Money is not a Slot** and never participates (FR-43).
- **Only a Minor League Eligible Contract may occupy a Minor League Slot**, and eligibility is **remembered rather than inferred from the present**: the eligible set is every Contract the live pool flags eligible (so every auction-won Contract) **union every Contract the app has ever observed in a Minor League Slot** — from the import snapshot or from any recorded act since. Without the second half, demoting an imported stash would destroy the only fact the app held saying he may be stashed, and the act would be irreversible. **Demotion to Active/Bench requires no eligibility at all.**
- An imported Active/Bench Contract the app has **never** seen in a Minor League Slot and that was never in the pool **cannot be promoted**, and the refusal says exactly that — *the app has never been told he is eligible* — rather than asserting he is ineligible, which is something the app does not know. §10 example 46.
- **Only a settled Contract moves.** A Player contested in an open Auction is on no roster, so he cannot be named; the refusal comes from the rules core, checked before the money and slots gates, exactly as FR-41 requires.

*What it costs and what it buys*
- **Cap Hit follows placement** (FR-21, §3 **Slot Placement**). A Contract promoted into a Minor League Slot charges **$0**; one demoted to Active/Bench charges its **full** amount. Its value never changes and no expression reads one from the other.
- **Cap Space and Roster Count both move, and they move together.** A promotion into a Minor League Slot takes the Cap Hit to $0 and takes the Player out of the twelve; a demotion does both in reverse. A Move is therefore a Maximum Bid lever in **both directions**, and since 2026-09-18 it works through **Cap Space alone**. The direction is now the intuitive one: *promoting* a Contract into a Minor League Slot **raises** Maximum Bid by releasing what he charged, and *demoting* one **lowers** it by starting the charge — offset by the $1,000,000 of Roster Reserve the newly-filled hole stops requiring, wherever that reserve is not already clamped to $0. §10 example 45 works both halves. *(Rewritten 2026-09-18. It read that demoting a stash could **raise** Maximum Bid by reducing **Minors Exposure**. That was a real lever while FR-35 stood and is a lever through a figure that is now permanently zero, so example 45's conclusion inverts with it: the Move that bought bidding power now costs it.)*
- **No assigned contract length is cleared.** The Contract does not change hands, so §10 example 42's reasoning does not apply, and the Year Allotment is untouched.

*Gates and refusal*
- The Team's **money and slots gates are evaluated once**, over post-Move state, by the **same** pure derivations a Bid is judged by. This requirement writes no affordability check of its own.
- Where either gate fails, **the whole Move is refused and nothing is written**, naming the gate, the Auction and the arithmetic.
- **It never cancels a Bid.** FR-40's cancellation trigger is an Auction Close and only a Close, and the sheet offers no control that would change that.

*Who, when, and the record*
- A **Manager** may act on **their own Team only** — the Team is resolved from the session, never from a form field. The **Commissioner** may act on any Team, through the override reason sheet with a mandatory free-text reason. A Manager's own act requires a **confirmation** sheet and **no reason**: it is a Manager's ordinary strategic decision, not a referee intervention, and the control separation of the UX spec must hold.
- Permitted in the **Auction** and **Contract Assignment** Phases; refused in Setup and once Archived.
- **Refused while the auction is paused** (FR-34). A Move changes Maximum Bids, and a paused auction changes nothing. The refusal is worded as the pause, distinctly from every rules refusal.
- **Unlimited.** A Move is reversible and costs nothing to undo, so no cap on frequency is imposed. The accepted cost is Audit Log volume.
- One transaction under the global write lock, mutating the roster's Slot kind for Existing Contracts and appending **one** record event carrying the **whole delta** — every Contract, both placements, both Cap Hits, and the Team's figures before and after. Auction Contracts have no row and move by the event alone, folded latest-placement-wins.
- Recorded in the league-visible Audit Log (FR-33) and **not broadcast to Discord**, consistent with FR-41.
- **The app becomes authoritative over placement.** Placement arrives from Fantrax at import (FR-1) and from FR-21 at a close; from the first Move onward the app decides it and the export carries the decision out (FR-30, FR-31). A placement difference between the app and Fantrax before that export is therefore **expected and is not a divergence** — see FR-42.

### 4.5 Auction Clock, Contention, and Closing

**Description:** Every open auction runs a 24-hour clock. In ordinary ascending bidding, each valid bid resets it. The one exception is the minimum-salary lottery: when a player is opened at exactly $1,000,000, the clock is fixed from that moment and does not reset, additional teams may join at exactly $1,000,000, and at expiry a random draw picks the winner among them. Any bid of $1,500,000 or more dissolves the lottery, resets the clock, and returns the auction to ordinary rules — regardless of how many teams had joined. Clocks run 24/7 with no overnight freeze and no anti-snipe extension, per league rules as written. Realizes UJ-1, UJ-2.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-16: Standard Contention clock

In Standard Contention, the Auction Clock is set to 24 hours by each valid Bid.

**Consequences (testable):**
- An Auction enters Standard Contention when its Opening Bid exceeds $1,000,000, or when a Bid of $1,500,000 or more is placed into a Minimum-Bid Contention.
- Every subsequent valid Bid sets the Clock to exactly 24 hours from that Bid's timestamp.
- The Clock runs continuously; no pause, freeze window, or business-hours adjustment applies.
- The Clock's absolute close time is displayed alongside the countdown, in the viewer's local timezone.

#### FR-17: Enter Minimum-Bid Contention

An Auction whose Opening Bid is exactly $1,000,000 enters Minimum-Bid Contention.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The Auction Clock is set to 24 hours from the Opening Bid and is thereafter fixed.
- The opening bidder is recorded as the first Contender.
- The Auction displays a state visually distinct from Standard Contention, showing the full Contender list, the lottery mechanic in plain language, and the fact that the clock will not reset.

#### FR-18: Join a Minimum-Bid Contention

A Manager can join an open Minimum-Bid Contention by bidding exactly $1,000,000.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The joining Team is added to the Contender list and **$1,000,000 is added to its Committed Bids**, whatever the Player's Minor League Eligibility, because any Contender may win and a winner lands in an Active/Bench Slot at $1,000,000 (FR-14, FR-21). *(Amended 2026-09-18: an entry on a Minor League Eligible Player used to commit nothing while a Free Minor League Slot stood ready to absorb the win.)*
- The Auction Clock is **not** reset, extended, or otherwise altered.
- A Team already a Contender cannot join twice. A Team that has **retracted** its entry is no longer a Contender and **may rejoin**, which follows from FR-15 having no cooldown and costs the lottery nothing: the list and the $1,000,000 are identical either way, and both acts are in the Audit Log.
- An entry may be **retracted within ninety seconds** of being made, which removes the Team from the Contender list and releases its $1,000,000. The rule and its consequences are FR-15's; two of them follow from *this* requirement and are worth meeting here. A join displaces nobody, so the window has **no cut-short subject**. And because the Clock does not reset, **a late entry's window can outlive the Auction** — a Team joining seconds before expiry is drawn before its ninety seconds are up, and **the draw closes the window**, won or lost (§10 example 53).
- A Bid strictly between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000 is refused as neither a valid lottery entry nor a valid ascending Bid.
- Joining resets the League Clock to 48 hours.
- A Team may be a Contender in **any number** of open Minimum-Bid Contentions simultaneously, provided the win it would create has somewhere to land: **at least one Free Active/Bench Slot**. A Free Minor League Slot does not qualify and never did the work it was credited with — no win lands in one — so a Team at Roster Count 12 may enter **no** contention at all. *(Amended 2026-09-18: the test read "at least one Free Active/Bench Slot, or — for a Minor League Eligible Player — at least one Free Minor League Slot".)*
- Contention entries **do not count toward Projected Active/Bench Additions**, are not subject to Roster Capacity, and do not consume the Outstanding Bid Allowance (FR-37). The expected outcome of a lottery is losing, and a capacity rule sized as though a Team would win them all would throttle the mechanism that exists to clear cheap Players quickly.
- **The exemption is a capacity exemption and stops there** (clarified 2026-09-18). An entry's $1,000,000 is committed capital: it charges Committed Bids, it lowers Available Cap Space, and it therefore **does** count toward **Reserve Additions**, so Roster Reserve stops holding back a second $1,000,000 for the Slot that capital would fill. Read as a blanket exclusion, this bullet cost a Team the full $1,000,000 of Maximum Bid for a minimum bid while a $1,100,000 bid cost it $100,000 — the money rule inverted by the capacity rule's rationale.
- They remain fully subject to **cap space**. FR-14's flat $1,000,000 per contention reaches Committed Bids on every Player alike. **Cap space is the only quantitative limit on how many lotteries a Team may enter**, and a Team with no Available Cap Space can enter none.
- A Team whose Slots fill has its remaining entries cancelled under FR-40 **before those lotteries are drawn**, which the sequential close ordering of FR-21 guarantees. This is what makes unlimited participation safe rather than a route past the ceiling.

#### FR-19: Dissolve a Minimum-Bid Contention

A Bid of $1,500,000 or more into a Minimum-Bid Contention converts it to Standard Contention.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The Contender list is discarded and every Contender's $1,000,000 commitment is released immediately.
- The Auction Clock is reset to 24 hours from the converting Bid.
- The converting Team becomes Leading Bidder and all ordinary rules apply from that point, including the $500,000 Minimum Increment.
- Conversion occurs regardless of Contender count — one Contender or twenty, the result is identical.
- A Team that was a Contender may itself be the converting bidder.
- Every former Contender is notified that the lottery dissolved and the auction is now ascending.
- **The converting Bid cannot be retracted** (FR-15). Releasing every Contender's capital is irreversible in a way no other Bid's effects are, and nothing in this product can re-commit it against Teams that have since moved on. The ninety-second window does not apply here and the control is absent.

#### FR-20: Draw a Minimum-Bid Contention winner

At Clock expiry in Minimum-Bid Contention, the system selects the winner by Randomizer.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The winner is drawn uniformly at random from the Contender list; every Contender has probability `1/n`.
- A single-Contender lottery resolves to that Contender without ambiguity.
- The draw records a seed, the ordered Contender list as it stood at expiry, and the resulting selection — all three permanently visible on the closed Auction and in the Audit Log.
- The recorded list is the list **after any cancellations under FR-40 and any retractions under FR-15 have been applied**. Neither a cancelled nor a retracted Contender appears in it or can be drawn; the Auction's history still shows that they entered and how their entry ended, so the record stays complete without the draw being run over Teams that were no longer eligible to win.
- A list reduced to one Contender by cancellation or retraction resolves to that Contender by the bullet above, with the seed opened at the draw like any other (FR-15, FR-19).
- A Contention whose Contender list is **empty at expiry** — every Contender having been **cancelled** under FR-40 — closes with no winner, and the Player returns to the Free Agent pool unclaimed. The empty list and the reason are recorded exactly as a drawn one would be.
- **A list emptied by RETRACTION never reaches expiry, and is FR-15's case rather than this one.** *(Corrected 2026-09-16. This bullet read "cancelled **or retracted**" and was wrong about the second: FR-15 returns such an Auction to **Awaiting Opening Bid with its Clock cleared**, so there is no expiry left for this requirement to govern. The word was added when retraction arrived without re-deriving what it does.)* The two differ because the **withdrawals** differ, not because the lottery does: a **cancelled** Bid stood and its Clock stands with it, so the Contention runs down to a winnerless close; a **retracted** Bid never counted, so the Clock it started never counted either and the Auction goes back on the Board. **Whichever act empties the list decides**, and the Team that placed the Opening Bid holds no special status — once others have joined the Clock belongs to the Contention (FR-15), so a Contention opened by a Team that later retracts, and *then* emptied by a cancellation, **keeps its Clock** and closes here.
- The recorded seed and Contender list are sufficient for any Manager to independently reproduce the result. `[ASSUMPTION: verifiable-fairness treated as a hard requirement rather than a nicety, on the grounds that an unauditable lottery is the single most trust-corrosive thing this app could ship.]`
- Losing Contenders' committed capital is released at the draw.

#### FR-21: Close an auction

At Auction Clock expiry, the system closes the Auction and awards the Player.

**Consequences (testable):**
- In Standard Contention the Leading Bidder wins at their bid amount; in Minimum-Bid Contention the drawn Contender wins at $1,000,000.
- The Player is removed from the Bid Board and from the Free Agent pool, and is recorded as an Auction Contract at the winning amount with contract length unset.
- Slot Placement is applied automatically and it has **one outcome**: the won Player takes an **Active/Bench Slot**, whatever his Minor League Eligibility and however many Minor League Slots stand free (§3, **Slot Placement**). *(Amended 2026-09-18: an eligible Player used to take a free Minor League Slot if one existed.)*
- The winning Team's Roster Count **increases by one**, always, since every placement is an Active/Bench one.
- The Cap Hit recorded is **the winning amount**. The Team's commitment on this Auction is released at the same instant and reappears as that Cap Hit — the money does not vanish at a Close, it **moves**, and Available Cap Space lands in the same place either way. A Manager who wants the $0 Cap Hit must go and get it, by moving the Player down under **FR-44** once he is on the roster.
- The **winning** Team's Nomination Slot is released, if it held one (FR-9). The nominating Team's is not, unless they are the winner.
- The Close then evaluates the winning Team's **remaining** commitments against Roster Capacity and applies FR-40, cancelling any that no longer have a Slot to land in. This happens **inside** the sequential close ordering — committed to the state the next Close is evaluated against — so two Auctions closing in the same sweep cannot both place a Player into the same last Slot.
- Auction Close does **not** reset the League Clock.
- Close occurs within 60 seconds of the Clock's nominal expiry even if no user is looking at the page.
- Winner, price, and full bid history are written to the Audit Log and broadcast per §4.7.

#### FR-40: Cancel a surplus commitment and restore the Auction

*(Numbered after FR-39 because it was added on 2026-09-08, when the commissioner recorded the Outstanding Bid Allowance. It belongs to this feature, and it is placed here rather than at the end because it is a consequence of FR-21 and unreadable apart from it.)*

When an Auction Close leaves a Team holding commitments with no Roster Slot to receive them, the system cancels them, most recent first, and restores each affected Auction to its next-highest bidder.

**This is the other half of FR-37's allowance.** A Team is permitted one more outstanding Bid than it has room for precisely because the surplus is taken back automatically; without this requirement the allowance would be a route to a thirteenth Player.

**Consequences (testable):**

*The trigger*
- Cancellation is triggered **only** by an Auction Close that reduces the winning Team's free Slots, and by nothing else. No Bid, no Nomination, no clock, no restoration and **no retraction** triggers it. The bound is unchanged by FR-15: a retraction stands a Bid down and calls the same restorer, but it is not a cancellation and causes none.
- **Three withdrawals, one restorer, and they must not be conflated.** A **cancellation** (here) is caused by a Close, retains its Bid as history stripped of leadership, and leaves both clocks alone. A **void** (FR-32) is caused by the Commissioner, erases its Bid, restores the Auction Clock and removes the League Clock reset. A **retraction** (FR-15) is caused by the bidding Team inside ninety seconds and takes the **void's** treatment on all three counts. All three hand the Auction to the next-highest surviving Bid by the same selector, under the same skip rule.
- The trigger counts **both** Slot kinds. A Close that fills the last Free Minor League Slot cancels eligible commitments that would now overflow, exactly as a Close filling the last Active/Bench Slot cancels Active/Bench-bound ones. A rule reading only "Active/Bench" would let a Team at Roster Count 12 with one free Minor League Slot win two eligible Auctions and land on 13.
- It runs **inside** FR-21's sequential close ordering, each cancellation committed to the state the next Close is evaluated against.

*What is cancelled*
- Commitments are cancelled **one at a time, most recent first by log sequence**, re-testing Roster Capacity (FR-37) and contention capacity (FR-18) after each, until the Team is within both. Nothing further is cancelled once it is.
- Only commitments with nowhere to land are cancelled. An eligible leading Bid a Free Minor League Slot can still absorb is untouched, however recent.
- A cancellation appends a compensating event. The original Bid is **never** deleted or mutated: it remains a visible line in the Auction's history, it keeps its League Clock reset, and the Audit Log shows both that it was placed and that it was cancelled.
- The cancelled Team's committed capital is released, and the Team is notified — naming the Auction that was cancelled **and the win that caused it**, because "your bid was cancelled" without the cause reads as a defect.

*Restoration*
- On an Auction in Standard Contention, the **next-highest surviving Bid becomes the leading Bid** and that Team's capital is re-committed.
- The restored Team is re-evaluated against the **money and capacity gates at that instant** — the same two grounds FR-13 and FR-37 apply to any Bid. If it fails either, the next-highest Bid below is tried, and so on down the history.
- If **no** surviving Bid passes, the Auction returns to **Awaiting Opening Bid** with no leading Bid. Its Auction Clock is **cleared**, not left running: an Auction with no Bid has no Clock, exactly as a nominated Player who has never been bid on has none (FR-17 starts the Clock at the Opening Bid). The Player stays on the Board, the nominating Team's Nomination Slot stays held (FR-9) — as it would have anyway, since only a win releases one — and the Auction resolves the way any unbid nomination does — a fresh Opening Bid starts a fresh 24 hours, or the Auction Phase ends and the Player returns to the pool unclaimed (FR-22, §10 example 12).
- A restoration **never** itself triggers a cancellation. A restored Team that would breach its own capacity is skipped rather than restored-then-cancelled, so the cascade cannot re-enter.

*Clocks*
- The **Auction Clock is not reset, extended, or otherwise altered** by a cancellation or a restoration where a Bid survives. A cancellation is not a Bid, and AD-22's rule that only a Nomination or a valid Bid resets a Clock is unchanged. A Restored Leading Bidder may inherit very little time, and that is correct: the Auction was always going to close then.
- The **League Clock is not reset, and no prior reset is removed.** This is the sharpest distinction from a Commissioner void (FR-32), where the Bid's reset *is* removed because the Bid should never have counted. A cancelled Bid was entirely valid when placed, so its reset stands and the Auction Phase does not shorten.

*Contentions*
- Cancelling a Minimum-Bid Contention entry removes the Team from the Contender list and releases its $1,000,000. There is no restoration — a lottery has no next-highest bid — and the lottery proceeds among the remaining Contenders, or closes with no winner if none remain (FR-20).

*Record*
- Every cancellation, every restoration, and every resulting return to Awaiting Opening Bid is written to the Audit Log and broadcast per §4.7, with the causing Auction Close named.
- Both affected Managers are notified: the cancelled Team, and the Restored Leading Bidder — who is being told they are now winning something they had stopped watching.

#### FR-22: End the Auction Phase

The system ends the Auction Phase when the League Clock expires.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The League Clock **starts** at 48 hours when the auction opens (FR-3) and is **reset** to 48 hours by any Nomination or any valid Bid, and by nothing else — **with one exception on each side, both from FR-15**. A Bid placed within ninety seconds of the same Team's own retraction **earns no reset**, and a Bid that is **retracted loses the reset it earned**. Neither is a special case of the clock; both are consequences of a retracted Bid being one the league agreed never counted.
- Starting and resetting are distinct: a Bid **voided** under FR-32 or **retracted** under FR-15 removes that Bid's reset, but nothing removes the start, so the Clock never recomputes to earlier than 48 hours after the auction opened.
- The League Clock is **derived from the surviving reset events**, not carried as a stored countdown. The Clock is recomputed from what survives — prospectively, in every case.
- **A Bid Cancellation under FR-40 removes nothing.** A cancelled Bid was valid when placed and keeps its reset; only a void and a retraction take one back. This is the sharpest of the three distinctions, and the one most easily built wrong, since all three end a Bid's leadership.
- On expiry, Nomination and bidding are disabled league-wide and the League transitions to Contract Assignment Phase.
- Any Auction still in **Awaiting Opening Bid** at expiry is terminated with no winner and its Player returns to the Free Agent pool unclaimed.
- All Managers are notified that the auction has ended and contract assignment has begun.

### 4.6 The Bid Board

**Description:** The screen the league lives on. It must be genuinely usable one-handed on a phone, because that is where most bids will be placed, and it must make the three things that matter — what's closing soon, where am I leading, what can I afford — legible without scrolling or arithmetic. Alongside it sits the Teams index (FR-39): the same question asked of the other twenty-nine teams, which is how a manager decides whether a player is worth chasing at all. Realizes UJ-1, UJ-2, UJ-3.

**On what is public.** Every figure in this feature is visible to every Manager. There is no partial-information layer and no fog of war: the auction is an open ascending market whose entire premise is that the arithmetic is checkable, and a figure that some managers can derive and others cannot is worse than one nobody can hide. This is stated once here and governs FR-23 – FR-25 and FR-39 alike.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-23: View the Bid Board

Any Manager can view all open Auctions with live state.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Each Auction shows Player identity, current price, Leading Bidder team name, Auction state, and time remaining.
- Auctions are sortable by time remaining, price, and Player name, and filterable to those the viewer's Team is leading or contending in.
- The board updates without a manual refresh; a change is reflected within 5 seconds of the originating action.
- Countdown timers are accurate to the second and derived from a server-authoritative close time, not from client clock arithmetic against page load.
- The viewer's Maximum Bid is persistently visible on the board, not only on individual Auction pages.

#### FR-24: View an auction in detail

Any Manager can open a single Auction and see its complete history.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Full Bid history in chronological order with Team name, acting Manager name, amount, and timestamp — visible to all Managers, with no anonymity at any point.
- Minimum-Bid Contention Auctions show the live Contender list and state in words, not just iconography, that the clock does not reset.
- The bid control pre-fills the minimum legal Bid and offers quick-increment options; it never offers an amount exceeding the viewer's Maximum Bid for this Auction.
- **A close time within ninety seconds of the leading Bid is provisional and says so**, because a retraction restores the Clock the Bid displaced and can move the close *backwards* by up to 24 hours (FR-15, §10 example 48). The Auction shows that the leading Bid is still inside its window rather than presenting a close time that may not survive the next minute.
- **The retract control appears only while the viewer's Team may actually use it** (FR-15), and it **counts down**. It shows the time remaining on the window, disappears the instant the window closes — whether by expiry or by the displaced Team's figures moving — and is never shown disabled. A control that sits there and fails on submit is worse than no control, because it is offered at the one moment a Manager has already made an expensive mistake.
- **It states its consequences before it commits**, in words and not in iconography: which Team the Auction will return to, what the Auction Clock will become (including that the Auction may close immediately, §10 example 48), and that the League Clock will recompute and may shorten the Auction Phase. This is FR-43's reason-sheet posture applied to the one other act in the product that moves a figure a Manager did not expect it to move.
- **A refused retraction names what closed the window and when** — the displaced Team and the act — so the Manager reads a rule rather than a malfunction.
- The viewer's own bid affordances are disabled with a stated reason when their Team is already leading, their Maximum Bid is insufficient, or Roster Capacity is exhausted (FR-37) — and the three reasons are worded distinctly, since "you have no money" and "you have no roster slot" call for different responses.

#### FR-25: View team state

Any Manager can view any Team's roster, cap position, and auction activity.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Every Team's Cap Space, Committed Bids, Available Cap Space, Roster Count by slot kind, Free Active/Bench Slots, Free Minor League Slots, and Nomination Slot status are visible to **all** Managers, for every Team. *(Minors Exposure was on this list until 2026-09-18 and is now permanently zero; publishing a column of zeroes would be worse than dropping it.)*
- The viewer's own Team view additionally shows Maximum Bid with components broken out and the list of Auctions they lead or contend in. **Maximum Bid remains the one figure shown for the viewer's Team only**, because it is per-Auction rather than per-Team (FR-12) and a team-level rendering of it would be a number that authorises nothing.
- Won Players appear on the Team's roster immediately at Auction Close, in their assigned Roster Slot, before contract length is assigned.
- Every one of the above figures must be reproducible by hand from figures already on the Bid Board, and none may be presented as privileged information.

*Amended 2026-08-18.* This requirement previously reserved Free Minor League Slots and Minors Exposure to the viewer's own Team. **That split was not enforceable and has been removed.** Both were derivable from figures FR-25 already published: Free Minor League Slots is `3 − minor-league occupancy`, and occupancy is part of "Roster Count by slot kind"; Minors Exposure was the difference between Committed Bids and the sum of a Team's non-eligible leading amounts, every one of which is public on the Bid Board under FR-23. Any manager willing to do the subtraction had both figures already, and FR-39's index would have handed all thirty of them to everyone at a glance. Concealment that survives only until someone bothers is worse than none, because it misleads the managers who trust it. See §12. *(Minors Exposure was retired on 2026-09-18; the argument is retained because it is what settled the question for **Free Minor League Slots**, which is still published.)*

#### FR-39: View the Teams index

*(Numbered after FR-38 because it was added on 2026-08-18, when the commissioner identified the surface as missing; it belongs to this feature.)*

Any Manager can see all 30 Teams in one list, each with its remaining roster slots and cap position, ranked against a League Median.

**Consequences (testable):**

*Rows*
- The index lists all 30 Teams, never a subset and never paginated. The viewer's own Team is marked but is **not** pinned, reordered, or otherwise exempted from the sort — it sits wherever the figures put it, which is the point of the screen.
- Each row carries: Team name and Manager(s), **Free Active/Bench Slots** as `Roster Count of 12`, **Free Minor League Slots** as `occupied of 3`, **Cap Space**, **Committed Bids**, **Available Cap Space**, and **Nomination Slot** status (free, or the Player holding it).
- Each row also carries the Team's **outstanding commitments against its allowance** — leading Bids held versus `Free Active/Bench Slots + 1` — and its count of open lottery entries. Since FR-37 a Team's remaining Slots no longer answer "can they bid on this"; `Roster 9 of 12` alone now understates a Team that has already spent its allowance and overstates one at Roster Count 12. Publishing the Slots without the commitments would make the index quietly wrong about the one question it exists to answer.
- Injury Reserve occupancy is shown, and is visibly excluded from the twelve — this is the arithmetic managers most often get wrong by hand (§10 example 23).
- Every figure is one FR-25 publishes for every Team. The index introduces no new visibility, only new adjacency.

*The League Median line*
- One comparison line reports the **League Median** of Free Active/Bench Slots and of Available Cap Space, labelled *median*, never *average*.
- It is the lower of the two middle values across the 30 Teams, so it always sits on the $500,000 grid and renders losslessly at one decimal. §10 example 28 covers this.
- The median is stated as a fact and nothing more. **No Team is coloured, badged, ranked, or annotated by its position relative to it**, and no copy characterises a Team as ahead, behind, rich, poor, or under pressure. The app has no opinion about whether a manager should spend, and a comparison figure is the single most tempting place to acquire one — see §6 "not a valuation engine" and SM-C1.

*Sorting*
- Sortable by Available Cap Space, Free Active/Bench Slots, Cap Space, and Team name; the default sort is Team name, which is the ordering that expresses no editorial view.
- Sorting is a view state and never changes a figure.

*Phase behaviour*
- **Auction Phase** — as above.
- **Contract Assignment Phase** — Roster Count reads 12 for every Team by definition (FR-30 blocks the export otherwise), so slot columns lose their information value and are replaced by **remaining Year Allotment** (4-year, 3-year, and both 2-year deals shown as used or unused) and assignment completion. Cap columns remain and now reflect settled contracts rather than open exposure.
- **Archived** — the final frozen state of the Contract Assignment columns, read-only, consistent with FR-31.
- The index is not offered in Setup. The equivalent surface there is FR-1's per-Team import preview, which is a different screen with a different job: confirming an import rather than reading a market.

*Correctness and freshness*
- Every figure is computed from committed state on read and none is stored, exactly as FR-12 requires — the index is thirty renderings of figures the app already computes, never a second source for them.
- A Team's figures on the index and on its FR-25 detail view are produced by the same computation and cannot disagree.
- The index authorises no action: it contains no bid control and no nomination control. Under the freshness rules it therefore **carries the age of its figures rather than disabling anything**, since there is no control to disable.
- The index updates on the same terms as the Bid Board — within 5 seconds of the originating action, without a manual refresh.

*Presentation*
- At 375px the index is a single-column list of rows, not a horizontally scrolling table. Money and slot figures must be readable without lateral scrolling, because a table that requires it is a table nobody reads on the surface where most of this league lives.
- On desktop it becomes a genuine table, which is the shape 30 rows of comparable figures actually want.

### 4.7 Notifications

**Description:** A rolling 24-hour clock without notifications is a trap. Every alert goes to the league Discord channel, which doubles as a public, permanent record of the auction; a notice aimed at a particular manager carries an `@mention`, which is what turns that public record into a personal push alert on their phone. Discord is the only outbound channel — this system sends no email. That concentration is deliberate and cheap, but it makes a Discord outage total, so FR-4 carries a Commissioner sign-in that does not depend on it. Realizes UJ-1, UJ-3.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-26: Broadcast auction events to Discord

The system posts league-wide auction events to a configured Discord channel.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Nominations, Bids, **Bid Retractions (FR-15)**, **Bid Cancellations and the restorations they cause (FR-40)**, Auction Closes, Randomizer draws (including seed and Contender list), Auction Phase start, and Auction Phase end are each posted.
- A retraction post names the retracting Team, the amount withdrawn, and where the Auction went — the Team restored, or that it returned to Awaiting Opening Bid. *(Retraction added 2026-09-16 with FR-15. **Cancellation and restoration were an omission**: FR-40 has required this since 2026-09-08 and said so in its own Record clause, but this list was never amended to match. Corrected here rather than left to be discovered.)*
- Each post names the Team, the acting Manager, the Player, the amount, and the new close time where applicable.
- A Discord delivery failure never blocks or reverses the underlying auction action; failures are logged and surfaced to the Commissioner.
- Posts are emitted at most once per event even if delivery is retried.

#### FR-27: Notify managers by Discord mention

The system alerts Managers about events affecting their Team by `@mention` in the league Discord channel.

**Consequences (testable):**
- A Manager is mentioned when their Team is **outbid**, when their Team **wins an Auction**, when a **Minimum-Bid Contention their Team contended in is drawn**, and when the **Contract Assignment Phase opens**.
- **A Close mentions the winner and nobody else.** They are the party to it twice over — they led the Auction into its Close, and theirs is the Nomination Slot it releases (FR-9) — so the mention states both facts on one line, and states the second only when that Team was actually holding a Slot. The **nominating** Team is not mentioned on a Close they did not win: losing does not release their Slot, so nothing happened to them that the Close is about. *(Corrected 2026-09-17. Until then a Close mentioned the nominator and told them their Slot had been released, which FR-9's 2026-09-14 amendment made false.)*
- **Contenders are told on the draw, not on the Close.** A Minimum-Bid Contention is resolved without a further Bid from anyone, so the draw is the only place its outcome is announced to the Teams it involved.
- A Manager is also mentioned when their Team **becomes the Leading Bidder again without acting** — whether by a Restored Leading Bidder under FR-40 or by the retraction of the Bid that displaced them under FR-15 — and when a Bid of theirs is **cancelled** under FR-40. These are the events a Manager cannot possibly be watching for, since nothing they did caused them. *(The retraction case was added 2026-09-16 with FR-15; the cancellation and restoration cases were an omission from the 2026-09-08 amendment, corrected here.)*
- **An outbid mention and a retraction mention can arrive within a minute of each other**, and the second must read as a correction of the first rather than as a fresh event. A Manager told they were outbid at 14:00 and restored at 14:01 has not had two things happen to them.
- Both Managers of a co-managed Team are mentioned on every Team-affecting event, individually — a single post naming only the Team does not satisfy this.
- Outbid mentions are posted within 60 seconds of the outbidding Bid.
- Each post links directly to the relevant Auction.
- **No category can be muted.** The settings surface lists every category with the reason it carries no control, and says so outright rather than presenting an absence a Manager has to interpret. *(Amended 2026-09-17. The first pass named three mutable categories and shipped one of them; all three are gone, and none for the same reason — see §12.)*
  - **Outbid** — muting it would undermine the fairness premise of a 24/7 clock.
  - **Won an Auction** — the Close is where a Contract is decided. It reports an outcome the Team is bound by, and there is no later surface that announces it. This is also the notice that reports a **Nomination Slot coming back**, which is why the Slot release stopped being separately mutable when it stopped being a separate notice.
  - **Contender in a draw** — the draw is resolved without a further Bid from any Contender, so the mention is the only place it is announced to them.
  - **Contract Assignment opened** — announced once, to every Team at once, and it opens the window each Team must act in.
- Every outbound post declares explicitly which mentions are permitted, so no message can mass-ping the league by accident.
- The channel used for these mentions is the same one carrying the FR-26 broadcast, so a Manager's alert and the public record are one artifact rather than two that can disagree.

### 4.8 Contract Assignment

**Description:** Once the auction ends, every team assigns contract lengths to the players it won, spending from a fixed annual allotment: one 4-year, one 3-year, two 2-year, and unlimited 1-year deals. This is where the dynasty decisions get made, and it is the last step before the results can leave the app. Realizes UJ-4.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-28: Assign contract lengths

A Manager can assign a contract length to each Player their Team won, drawing from the Team's Year Allotment.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Available lengths are 1, 2, 3, and 4 years; 4-year and 3-year may each be used once, 2-year twice, 1-year without limit.
- The remaining allotment is displayed as the Manager assigns, and an assignment exceeding it is refused.
- Assignments may be changed freely until the Manager submits their Team as final.
- Unused allotment expires at the end of the phase and does not carry to a future offseason.
- Year Allotment applies only to Auction Contracts; Existing Contracts cannot be extended or altered here.
- The winning amount is the annual Cap Hit; the app records amount and length only and computes no future-year escalation.

#### FR-29: Track and enforce phase completion

The system tracks per-Team completion of contract assignment and enforces a deadline.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Commissioner sees a live roster of which Teams have submitted and which have not.
- Managers with unassigned Players are reminded by Discord mention at a Commissioner-configured interval before the deadline.
- **The deadline does not resolve anything by itself.** No contract length is ever assigned by default. At the deadline the affected Teams and the Commissioner are notified, unassigned Players stay unassigned, and **the export stays blocked until every Auction Contract has a length** (FR-30).
- A Team that will not or cannot respond is resolved by the Commissioner assigning lengths on its behalf under FR-32, with a reason recorded like any other override. This is the only path past an unresponsive Team, and it is deliberately a visible act rather than a silent default.
- The Commissioner can extend the deadline, and the extension is logged.

### 4.9 Export to Fantrax

**Description:** The final deliverable: a file the Commissioner uploads through Fantrax commissioner controls to write the auction results into the league of record. Because Fantrax offers no supported write API, this is deliberately a human-in-the-loop handoff — the Commissioner reviews on screen before anything leaves the app. Realizes UJ-4.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-30: Review and export results

Commissioner can review the complete auction outcome and download it in Fantrax's expected import format.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The review screen lists every Auction Contract with Team, Player, Fantrax player ID, winning amount, contract length, and Slot Placement, plus each Team's resulting total salary and Cap Space.
- Export is blocked, with the offending Teams named, if any Team's post-auction salary exceeds $165,000,000, any Team's Roster Count is other than exactly 12 (below it leaves a hole; above it breaches FR-37's ceiling), or **any Auction Contract still lacks a contract length** (FR-29).
- Free Agents not won require no export treatment. They simply remain free agents in Fantrax, and the export omits them entirely.
- The exported file carries the stable Fantrax player IDs from the FR-2 import so rows match on upload without name-based reconciliation.
- Export can be re-downloaded any number of times and is recorded in the Audit Log each time.
- Exporting does not itself write to Fantrax; the app states plainly that upload is a manual Commissioner step.

#### FR-36: Export full post-auction rosters

*(Numbered after FR-35 because it was added during finalize; it belongs to this feature.)*

Commissioner can additionally export each Team's complete post-auction roster, not only the contracts created by this auction.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The roster export lists every Player on every Team — Existing Contracts and Auction Contracts alike — with Fantrax team ID, Fantrax player ID, Cap Hit, contract length, and Roster Slot.
- Existing Contracts are reproduced exactly as imported in FR-1, unless altered by a Commissioner override, in which case the current value is exported and the override is traceable in the Audit Log.
- The roster export and the contract export (FR-30) are separate downloads, because Fantrax's commissioner import may accept them through different paths.
- Both exports reconcile: every Auction Contract in the FR-30 export appears in the roster export at the same amount and slot.

#### FR-31: Archive the auction

Commissioner can mark the auction archived once results are in Fantrax.

**Consequences (testable):**
- An archived auction is read-only: no Bids, Nominations, assignments, or overrides are accepted.
- The full Bid Board, Audit Log, and export remain viewable by all Managers indefinitely.

### 4.10 Commissioner Controls and Audit

**Description:** Rules engines meet reality. A manager gets locked out at a critical moment; an import turns out to have a wrong salary; a bid is placed by the wrong co-manager. The Commissioner needs to be able to fix things — and every fix needs to be visible to the whole league, because an unlogged override in a money auction is how a five-year league ends. Realizes UJ-3, UJ-4.

**Functional Requirements:**

#### FR-32: Commissioner overrides

Commissioner can void a Bid, adjust a Team's Cap Space, terminate an Auction, **reverse an Auction Close**, release a Nomination Slot, extend or expire any Clock, and assign a contract length on a Team's behalf.

**Consequences (testable):**
- Every override requires a free-text reason before it commits.
- Voiding a Bid restores the Auction to its state before that Bid, including the prior Leading Bidder and the prior Auction Clock value.
- **The Team it restores is re-validated against the money and capacity gates at that instant, and skipped in favour of the next Bid down if it fails** — the same walk FR-40 runs and FR-15 reuses. *(Stated explicitly 2026-09-16. It was always the intent — a void that restores a Team into a breach of its own cap is the one outcome none of the three withdrawal paths may produce — but FR-32 had never said so, which left FR-40's claim that all three share one selector resting on nothing in this document.)*
- **Voiding a Bid also removes that Bid's League Clock reset.** The League Clock is recomputed from the remaining reset events (FR-22) as though the voided Bid had never landed, which can shorten the Auction Phase.
- Where that recomputation lands the League Clock in the past, the Auction Phase ends at the **next** clock evaluation and takes effect **prospectively only**: Nominations and Bids accepted between the recomputed expiry and the void stand, and nothing is retroactively invalidated. A void produces a *sooner* phase end, never a rewritten history.
- **Reversing an Auction Close terminates the Auction after the fact.** *(Added 2026-09-23 — a Team won a Player it had room for only because a Contract sat in Injury Reserve against the league's free-agency rule.)* The winning Team's Auction Contract is removed, taking its Cap Hit and its Roster Count contribution with it, and the Player returns to the Free Agent pool, nominatable by anyone. The Auction ends with **no winner** and every Bid on it is discarded. It is shown as **Reversed** with the Commissioner and the reason — never as a close that did not happen.
- A reversal **undoes nothing else.** Bid Cancellations the Close caused under FR-40 stand, and so do their restorations. The League Clock is untouched, because the Bids earned their resets under the rules then enforced. A Nomination Slot the Close released is **re-held only if the Team has not used it since**; a Nomination made with it stands. §10 example 58.
- A reversal is **refused** when the won Contract has since been traded (FR-41) or dropped (FR-43), and names that act. A Contract moved within its own Team (FR-44) does not block it.
- During the Contract Assignment Phase a reversal is still permitted, but the reason sheet states that the Team falls below twelve and cannot export (FR-30), and that the Player cannot be nominated again because the Auction Phase has ended. A length already assigned returns to the Year Allotment by arithmetic.
- Overrides are permitted during the Auction Phase and Contract Assignment Phase, and refused once archived.
- Every override is written to the Audit Log with actor, timestamp, before-state, after-state, and reason.

#### FR-33: League-visible audit log

Any Manager can read the complete Audit Log.

**Consequences (testable):**
- The Log is append-only; no entry can be edited or deleted by any role, Commissioner included.
- Entries cover every Nomination, Bid, **Bid Retraction (FR-15)**, **Bid Cancellation and restoration (FR-40)**, Auction Close, **Close Reversal (FR-32, added 2026-09-23)**, Randomizer draw with seed, Commissioner override, import, and export. *(The first was added 2026-09-16; the second was an omission from the 2026-09-08 amendment, corrected the same day.)*
- The three ways a Bid stops leading other than being outbid are **distinguishable at a glance**, because conflating them is how a league loses the thread of what happened: a **void** shows the Commissioner and a reason, a **cancellation** shows the Auction Close that caused it, and a **retraction** shows the Manager and no reason at all.
- The Log is filterable by Team, Player, and event type, and is exportable.

#### FR-34: Pause the auction

Commissioner can pause and resume the entire auction.

**Consequences (testable):**
- While paused, all Auction Clocks and the League Clock stop advancing, and Nominations and Bids are refused with the pause stated as the reason.
- On resume, every Clock continues with exactly the remaining time it held at pause.
- Pause and resume are announced to all Managers and posted to Discord.
- `[ASSUMPTION: pause included as the escape hatch for outages and disputes even though the league runs clocks 24/7 by rule. It is an administrative tool, not a scheduled freeze.]`

#### FR-41: Record a Roster Trade

*(Numbered after FR-40 because it was added on 2026-09-10, when the commissioner recorded that managers trade during the auction. It belongs to this feature: it is a Commissioner control, and it is the first one that changes the world rather than the auction's reading of it. **Renamed from "Record a Roster Move" on 2026-09-12**: what this requirement describes is a trade, and the name "Roster Move" was needed for the within-Team act FR-44 now defines. The requirement's substance is unchanged — no gate, figure or refusal in it moves — and the persisted event type remains `RosterMoveRecorded`, because AD-4 forbids mutating the log.)*

Commissioner can record that one or more Contracts have changed hands between two Teams, in one act, and the system recomputes both Teams' positions from it.

**Consequences (testable):**

*What moves*
- A Roster Trade names **two Teams** and moves Contracts **in both directions in one act**. This is not a convenience: a trade sending three Players for two is a single event, and applying its halves in sequence would transiently push the receiving Team over a ceiling it never actually breaches — refusing a legal trade on a state that never existed.
- **Either direction may be empty.** A salary dump is a Roster Trade; the requirement is that a Trade is one act, not that it is balanced.
- **Existing Contracts and Auction Contracts may both move.** A Trade may mix them freely; the two are distinguishable in the record but not in what the Commissioner may do with them.
- A Contract travels **unchanged in value and in years**. A Roster Trade is not a restructure, an extension, or a buyout, and no control on it edits an amount.
- **Nothing else moves.** A Trade never transfers Committed Bids, a Minimum-Bid Contention entry, a Nomination Slot, or Cap Space. A Trade naming any of them is refused, not silently narrowed.
- **Only a settled Contract moves.** A Trade names a Player who is currently the subject of a **settled Contract** — Existing or Auction. A Player **contested in an open Auction is not eligible**, because he is neither kind yet: no Team holds him, and the Contract that would receive the transfer does not exist until that Auction closes. This is a **third refusal ground alongside the money and slots gates**, checked first, because the other two are meaningless for a Player nobody holds. The refusal is returned by the rules core, not left to the admin screen to filter.

*Placement and Cap Hit*
- **Slot Placement is re-evaluated for every moved Player against the receiving Team's occupancy** at the instant of the Trade: a Minor League Eligible Player takes a Free Minor League Slot if one exists, and an Active/Bench Slot otherwise. **This rule is unchanged and is deliberately not FR-21's.** *(Stated separately from 2026-09-18, having cited FR-21 until then. An Auction win now always lands in Active/Bench, because a Team must be able to fit a Player it is **winning** onto its active roster first. A Contract arriving by Trade is in a different position: it is **already settled** — bought, priced and rostered — and is being re-placed against a new occupancy rather than won onto a roster, so the automatic placement is right for it and is kept. See §12.)*
- A receiving Team unhappy with where an arriving Contract landed has a remedy that did not exist before 2026-09-12: it may rearrange its own Slots under **FR-44**. The Trade's own placement rule is **unchanged** — it is still FR-21's automatic rule against the receiving Team's occupancy at the instant of the Trade, and it is still applied without asking anybody.
- **Cap Hit follows placement** (§3 **Slot Placement**, and the winning-amount/Cap-Hit distinction of FR-21). A Player stashed at a $0 Cap Hit who lands in an Active/Bench Slot charges his **full amount** there. His winning amount is untouched, and no expression reads one from the other.
- The reason sheet states this consequence **in words, before commit**, wherever a moved Player's Cap Hit changes. A Cap Hit rising from $0 to $14,000,000 by the act of moving is the least obvious thing in this requirement.
- **The knock-on to the sending Team runs through Cap Space**, and it is still a knock-on: sending a charging Player away releases what he charged, which moves that Team's Maximum Bid on every Auction it leads without anybody touching those Auctions. Sending a **stashed** Player away releases nothing, because he was charging $0. *(Amended 2026-09-18. This bullet read that the sending Team's freed Minor League Slot raised its **M** and recomputed **Minors Exposure** across every eligible Auction it still led. The freed Slot is real and it now buys that Team nothing but room to stash somebody else under FR-44.)*

*The refusal*
- After the **whole** Trade is applied — both directions, every Player — **both** Teams are re-evaluated against the money gate (FR-13) on every Auction they lead and the slots gate (FR-37). **If either Team fails either gate, the entire Trade is refused** and nothing is written.
- The Trade is also refused if it would breach a slot ceiling on either side: more than 12 Active/Bench, more than 2 Injury Reserve, or more than 3 Minor League Players — the same ceilings FR-1 holds on import.
- **A refusal can be caused by the sending Team, not only the receiving one.** Sending a Player away raises Available Cap Space by his Cap Hit but raises Roster Reserve by $1,000,000, so a Team that sends away a Player cheaper than $1,000,000 while leading an Auction at exactly its Maximum Bid is pushed over it. This is not an edge case to be discovered in production.
- The refusal **names what must clear first** — the Team, the gate, the Auction, and the arithmetic — so the Commissioner can void a Bid or wait for a Close and retry. It never offers to cancel a Bid. **Cancellation is triggered only by an Auction Close (FR-40), and a Roster Trade does not extend that list.**
- The same re-evaluation governs a **Drop** under FR-43.

*Phase and the Year Allotment*
- Permitted during the **Auction Phase** and the **Contract Assignment Phase**; refused once **Archived**, like every other override.
- Moving an Auction Contract that already carries an assigned contract length **clears that length**. The year returns to the sending Team's Year Allotment and the receiving Team assigns from its own under FR-28. The receiving Team can always satisfy this, because 1-year deals are unlimited.
- A cleared length re-blocks the export under FR-30 until it is reassigned, and that is the intended behaviour rather than a side effect.

*The record*
- Every Trade requires a free-text **reason** before it commits, like every override under FR-32.
- The Trade is written to the Audit Log with actor, timestamp, **before-state and after-state for both Teams** — Cap Space, Roster Count, and occupancy of all three Slot kinds — and the reason. One entry, not two.
- **A Roster Trade is not broadcast to Discord**, and is the only Commissioner act that is not. It is recorded in the league-visible Audit Log (FR-33), where it is filterable by Team like any other entry. `[ASSUMPTION: the commissioner chose audit-log-only on 2026-09-10. The accepted cost is that a Manager can see a rival's Cap Space and Maximum Bid change with no announcement explaining why, and must open the Log to learn the cause.]`

*Atomicity*
- A Trade is **one transaction under the global write lock** (§5 concurrency), so it cannot interleave with an Auction Close. Either the whole Trade lands or none of it does; a Trade that moved three Players of five is never a reachable state.

*A Manager's own within-Team rearrangement is **FR-44**, in §4.4 — it is not a Commissioner control and is filed with the cap arithmetic it exists to change. The Commissioner's on-behalf half of it **is** a control of this class and obeys every rule in this section, including the reason sheet.*

#### FR-42: Detect a Roster Divergence from Fantrax

*(Numbered after FR-41, added the same day. It is the reconciliation half of FR-41 and unreadable apart from it.)*

The system periodically reads Team membership from Fantrax and raises any difference from its own rosters to the Commissioner as a proposed Roster Trade or Drop.

**The contingency is discharged.** `GET /fxea/general/getTeamRosters?leagueId=…&period=…` was called against the real BBSL league on **2026-09-10** and answered **unauthenticated**, returning all 30 Teams and 303 roster rows in one call. `status` distinguishes all four Slot kinds — `ACTIVE`, `RESERVE`, `MINORS`, `INJURED_RESERVE` — and every Team passed FR-1's slot ceilings when checked against the live payload. FR-42 is viable and no longer conditional.

**Consequences (testable):**

*The read*
- The system reads `getTeamRosters` for the League **at most once per hour**, not on every tick. A Bid is time-critical to the second; a trade is not, and polling an undocumented third-party endpoint every minute for three weeks invites a rate limit or a block on the one integration that cannot be renegotiated.
- **The read is never trusted for money — and that is now a choice rather than a limitation.** The endpoint **does** return `salary`, a `contract` designation and a per-Team `salaryCap`; the earlier statement that it carried none of these was taken from an incomplete community wrapper and is corrected here. **Membership is still the only fact taken from Fantrax**, for two reasons: the app is authoritative for money while the auction runs and already holds every Cap Hit from the FR-1 import, and Fantrax's own figures are **less trustworthy than the app's** — see the float rule below.
- **Money arrives as a float, and must be rounded, never truncated.** Salaries are emitted in scientific-notation floats carrying representation error: the live payload contains `23499999.999999993`, `46499999.999999985` and `28499999.999999993`. Truncating those yields a figure **off by a dollar and off the $500,000 grid** the entire money rendering depends on. Any code reading this endpoint **rounds to the nearest dollar and then asserts the $500,000 grid**, refusing the row if it fails. Rounding was verified clean on all 303 live rows; truncation corrupted 4. This rule binds even though FR-42 takes no money today, because the moment anything reads `salary` the hazard is live.
- **Fantrax `status` maps to all four Roster Slot kinds** — `ACTIVE` and `RESERVE` to Active/Bench, `MINORS` to Minor League, `INJURED_RESERVE` to Injury Reserve. It is carried into the proposal as **advisory** and shown for confirmation, never taken as authoritative. Note the underscore: `INJURED_RESERVE` does not match the CSV importer's existing `injured reserve` alias, and the two mappings are separate.
- **Player ids must be normalised at the boundary, or nothing matches.** The endpoint returns a bare id (`01eon`); the FR-1 CSV importer stores the asterisk-wrapped form exactly as the export writes it (`*04ewu*`). Compared unnormalised, **every one of the 303 rows reads as a departure and an unknown arrival at once.** Normalisation happens inside the adapter (AD-24) and applies to both directions. The same applies to Teams: the endpoint carries a Fantrax team id the CSV never had, and the app's `teams` table stores none, so the Team mapping must be established explicitly rather than by name — AD-24 forbids name matching.
- **A read never changes app state.** No projection, no reference table and no event is written by the reading of Fantrax. The only thing a read can produce is a proposal.

*What is compared*
- The comparison **excludes every Player won in this auction.** Auction Contracts do not exist in Fantrax until the FR-30 export is uploaded, so including them would report every win as a departure.
- A divergence is classified as one of three kinds:
  - **Paired** — a Player the app holds on Team A appears on Team B, and the reverse or a matching set exists. Raised as a **proposed Roster Trade** (FR-41), pre-filled with both Teams, every affected Player, and the resulting before → after figures. The Commissioner confirms with a reason, or dismisses.
  - **Unpaired departure** — a Player the app holds is on no Fantrax roster. This is a **Drop**, and it is raised as a proposed Drop under FR-43. It is a distinct act from a Trade: one Team, one direction, and **no cap relief at all**.
  - **Unknown arrival** — a Player on a Fantrax roster whom the app does not hold and who is not in its Free Agent pool. **Reported as an error, prominently.** Every acquisition during the auction window goes through the auction itself, so this cannot legitimately occur; it signals a mis-set league id, a wrong period, or a genuine out-of-band change. It is surfaced as a fault rather than listed as a state needing a remedy. Note that the app *could* now price him — the endpoint carries his salary — and deliberately does not: an arrival that cannot legitimately happen is a signal something is wrong, not an invitation to create a Contract nobody agreed to.

- **A placement difference is not a divergence.** The detector takes **membership only**, and from FR-44 onward that is a positive decision rather than a limitation: the app **decides** placement and Fantrax **receives** it through the export, so a Contract the app holds in a Minor League Slot and Fantrax holds in Active/Bench is the expected state between a Move and the next export — not drift. Raising it would train the Commissioner to dismiss the detector. The live payload's `status` field does distinguish all four Slot kinds, so this is a choice and not a data limitation; revisit only if the export stops being the reconciliation point.

*Behaviour*
- A dismissed divergence is **suppressed until the underlying difference changes**, so a Trade or Drop recorded in the app before it is executed in Fantrax — or the reverse — does not re-raise the same notice every hour.
- Once recorded, the corresponding divergence resolves on the next read with no further action.
- Divergences appear on the Commissioner's operational surface only. A divergence is an unconfirmed reading of a third-party system, and is not league-visible until it becomes a Trade or a Drop.

*The plausibility guard*
- **A well-formed response can still be wrong, and the ordinary failure rules do not catch it.** An HTTP 200 carrying three Teams instead of thirty — a transient upstream fault, a wrong `period`, a deploy mid-flight — parses cleanly and would otherwise read as twenty-seven Teams having released every player they hold. The guard exists because that reading is *arithmetically* valid and *obviously* absurd.
- **Structural check:** a read raises nothing unless the payload carries **all 30 Teams** and **no Team's roster is empty**. A League of exactly 30 Teams is hard-wired (§2.2), and no real Team holds zero Players mid-auction.
- **Volume check:** a read whose divergences would affect **more than a quarter of the League in one pass** is treated as a fault, not as news. Trades arrive a few at a time; a single read proposing changes across eight or more Teams is far likelier to be a bad payload than a busy afternoon. *[The fraction is a starting value, tunable by the Commissioner without a code change.]*
- **A tripped guard renders as *stopped*, never as *no divergences*** — the same rule the failure clause below applies, and for the same reason: the dangerous state is the one that looks like health.
- **The Commissioner can acknowledge a tripped guard and see the proposals anyway**, because a genuinely busy day must not be unreconcilable. The guard **never clears itself**: it is dismissed by a person who has looked at it, so a real mass change costs one extra confirmation and a bad payload costs nothing at all.

*Failure*
- A Fantrax read that fails — unreachable, unauthorised, rate-limited, malformed, or shaped differently than expected — **never blocks or reverses any auction action**, exactly as a Discord delivery failure does not (FR-26). It is logged and surfaced to the Commissioner.
- **Repeated failure degrades to FR-41 alone** and says so plainly on the Commissioner's surface, rather than presenting a silent all-clear. An integration that has stopped answering must not read as "no trades have happened."

#### FR-43: Record a Drop, and carry Dead Money

*(Numbered after FR-42, added the same day. It exists because FR-42 can detect a Drop, and a Drop the app records wrongly is worse than one it does not record.)*

Commissioner can record that a Team has dropped a Player, and the system carries the released Contract's Cap Hit as Dead Money.

**Consequences (testable):**

*The governing rule*
- A Drop names **one Team** and one or more Players. Unlike a Roster Trade it has one direction and no counterparty.
- **A Drop converts the Player's *charged* Cap Hit into Dead Money, at the same amount** — not the amount his Contract states. All three Slot kinds fall out of this one rule and none is a special case:

| dropped from | was charging | Dead Money carried | Roster Count | net effect on Maximum Bid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Active/Bench | full | full | **falls** | **lower**, by the $1,000,000 reserve on the freed hole |
| Injury Reserve | full | full | unchanged | unchanged |
| Minor League | **$0** | **none — the row is removed** | unchanged | **unchanged** |

- **A Drop from Active/Bench lowers the Team's Maximum Bid.** Roster Reserve rises $1,000,000 for the freed hole with no offsetting release. The reason sheet states this before commit, because every manager's intuition says the opposite.
- **A Drop from a Minor League Slot moves it nowhere.** He was charging $0, so nothing is carried and Cap Space is unchanged; Roster Count is untouched, because Minor League Players never counted toward the 12; and the freed Minor League Slot buys no bidding power, because nothing a Team is bidding on can ever land in one (§3 **Slot Placement**). **Every figure stands still**, which is the sharpest form of this section's point: the effect of a Drop is derived from what the Player was **charging**, and a Player charging nothing releases nothing. *(Amended 2026-09-18. It read "moves it the other way", raising **M**, lowering Overflow Count and recomputing **Minors Exposure** to release committed capital. That release never existed — the leads it was crediting were committed in full all along.)*
- Dead Money charges for the remaining term of the Contract as established at import. **Within this auction that term computes nothing** — the charge is simply the charge — and the years are carried for the export only. Future-year cap projection stays out of scope (§6).

*There is no exception, and the absence is a rule* *(2026-09-16 — this section previously carved out a second-round rookie Contract of the current draft class, which cleared entirely.)*
- **Every Contract this product releases carries what it was charging.** There is no Contract kind, no designation and no remaining term that waives Dead Money at a Drop.
- **The waiver exists, and it happens somewhere else.** The league runs an **amnesty period before the auction opens**, settled in Fantrax. Whatever it forgives is already forgiven by the time the FR-1 import reads the rosters, so the app imports the post-amnesty truth and has nothing left to waive. **No amnesty capability is specified here** — the correct app-side behaviour is to carry the charge, and it already does.
- **A Minor League Drop still leaves nothing behind, for the reason the table gives:** it was charging **$0**. That is placement arithmetic (§3 **Slot Placement**, FR-44), not relief, and it is the only route by which a Drop returns anything.
- **The `2RK` designation survives the import and decides nothing.** The adapter captures the draft round, and it is staged, persisted and written into the Drop's record. It is retained because it is a fact about the Contract and because the events already in the log carry it — not because any rule reads it. *(The gap this section once recorded — that `CONTRACT_END_YEAR` matched the `NRK` prefix in a non-capturing group, making `2RK31` and `2031` indistinguishable — was closed by Story 7.6 and is no longer true.)*
- **Consequence: an Active/Bench Drop can never *raise* Maximum Bid.** Nothing returns to Cap Space on any release, so the table above is exhaustive in direction as well as in Slot kind — Active/Bench lowers, Injury Reserve leaves unchanged, Minor League raises. The reason sheet therefore states **one** direction for an Active/Bench Drop rather than computing which way it moved.

*Visibility*
- Dead Money is shown on the Team view (FR-25) and the Teams index (FR-39), **labelled and separate from the roster**, so a Manager can reconcile a Team's Cap Space against the players visibly on it. An unexplained gap between the two is precisely the arithmetic-doubt this product exists to remove.
- Dead Money counts toward **no** slot ceiling and is never a Slot Placement destination.

*Boundaries and the record*
- A Drop is re-evaluated against both gates exactly as FR-41 requires, and **refused** if it leaves the Team failing one — naming the Auction.
- **The import does not accept Dead Money in v1** (FR-1). No Team carries any today, and the shape Fantrax exports it in has never been seen. An unrecognised `Status` already refuses the file at content altitude, so this fails loudly on a future setup day rather than silently.
- Dead Money is **excluded from both exports** (FR-30, FR-31). The Drop happened in Fantrax, which already carries its own accounting for it; exporting it back would double it.
- Permitted during the Auction Phase and Contract Assignment Phase; refused once Archived.
- Recorded in the Audit Log with actor, timestamp, before/after and reason, and **not broadcast to Discord**, consistent with FR-41.

## 5. Cross-Cutting NFRs

- **Rule correctness is the product.** Every bid-validity, clock, and cap computation must be deterministic and covered by automated tests, with the §10 worked examples as executable cases. A rules bug is a worse failure than an outage.
- **Concurrency safety.** State mutation must be serialized **globally**, not merely per Auction. Two Bids arriving within the same instant must produce one winner and one clear rejection — never two accepted Bids, a lost Bid, or a Committed Bids figure that double-counts. Per-Auction serialization is insufficient and was corrected here: Maximum Bid depends on the bidding Team's leading positions in *other* Auctions through Committed Bids, so two Managers of one co-managed Team bidding simultaneously on two *different* Auctions each pass their own per-Auction check and jointly breach the cap. No Auction is raced — the Team is. At this scale global serialization costs nothing measurable. *(The argument originally rested on Committed Bids **and Minors Exposure under FR-35**. Losing the second term weakens it not at all: Committed Bids alone is a cross-Auction figure, and since 2026-09-18 **every** lead feeds it, where before an eligible one might not have. The race is if anything wider.)*
- **Server-authoritative time.** All clock arithmetic derives from server time. A client with a skewed clock must not see a different close time or be able to bid after expiry.
- **Timer reliability.** Auction Close must fire within 60 seconds of nominal expiry with no user present, and must survive process restarts — a missed close during a deploy silently extends an auction.
- **Availability — a posture, not a number.** The app should be reachable 24/7 through the Auction Phase, but this runs on stacked free tiers with no SLA between them and with Discord on the sign-in path, so no uptime percentage is committed to. *(The previous "no more than 1 hour of unplanned unavailability" implied 99.8% across ~504 hours, which nothing in the stack can underwrite. Restated 2026-08-17 per OQ-8.)* What is required instead is that an outage be **survivable rather than decisive**: a stalled close sweep produces late closes and never wrong ones, the Commissioner can pause every clock, and the auction is restorable from outside Supabase. Any outage beyond 15 minutes is a Commissioner pause (FR-34) plus a compensating clock adjustment — not a wait-and-see. **That recovery procedure must be written down before the auction opens, not improvised during one**; it is scheduled work inside the build epics, and auction open is blocked on it.
- **Mobile-first responsive.** Bidding, nominating, and reading the board must work one-handed on a phone at 375px width. Desktop gets density; mobile gets nothing removed.
- **Performance.** Board and auction views interactive within 2 seconds on mobile data; a Bid submission acknowledged within 1 second.
- **Auditability over convenience.** Where a design choice trades away a record for a simpler flow, keep the record.
- **Scale is not a concern.** 31 users, ~30 concurrent auctions, a few thousand events across three weeks. Do not design for load; design for correctness.
- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.1 AA contrast ratios (4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for UI components) and touch targets of at least 44×44px on every bidding and nomination control. Auction state must never be conveyed by color alone — a closing-soon auction and a lottery auction need labels, not just hues.
- **Measurability.** Every Bid, Nomination, Auction Close, and notification dispatch must be recorded with enough context to compute the §8 metrics without retrofitting instrumentation — including, for Bids, the device class the bid was placed from (SM-4) and, for notifications, dispatch and delivery outcome (SM-3). Because the auction record is append-only, these fields must be captured from the first event onward; they cannot be backfilled later.

## 6. Non-Goals (Explicit)

- **Not a Fantrax replacement.** Scoring, lineups, trades, waivers, and in-season roster management stay in Fantrax. This app is live for one offseason phase per year.
- **Not enforcing activation.** Promoting a stashed player out of a Minor League Slot requires the team to absorb his full contract against the cap **in Fantrax, during the season**. This app never models it, never warns about it, and never blocks a bid on account of it. *(Amended 2026-09-18. This non-goal used to carry a second job — it was the argument that made FR-35's unbounded bidding self-correcting. FR-35 is retired and nothing in this app now lets a Team acquire a Player it cannot afford, so the non-goal is back to being only what it says: a boundary, not a safety net. What the app **does** model is the in-app half of the same idea — FR-44 charges a demotion its full Cap Hit the instant it happens.)*
- **Not multi-tenant.** One league, hard-wired rules. No league creation, no rule configuration UI, no other sports.
- **Not writing to Fantrax.** v1 never mutates Fantrax state; the Commissioner uploads the export by hand and executes any trade there by hand. This is a property of the integration and not only a preference — **Fantrax exposes no write endpoint at all.** *(Verified 2026-09-10: every method documented by both community wrappers is a GET; `fantrax.com/developer` remains gated.)* v1 **does** now read from Fantrax on one undocumented endpoint, for reconciliation only (FR-42). A read can raise a divergence for the Commissioner to act on; it can never itself change app state.
- **Not a draft tool.** No rookie draft, no draft pick trading, no draft board.
- **Not a trade broker.** The app hosts no proposal, no negotiation, and no acceptance flow. A trade is agreed in Discord and executed in Fantrax by the Commissioner; the app records the resulting roster state under FR-41 and nothing more. **Committed capital is never exchanged** — a leading Bid and a Minimum-Bid Contention entry cannot move between Teams, and a Bid stays the Bid of the Team that placed it. A Trade is not a route around FR-15: the only Team that can take a Bid back is the one that made it, inside its own ninety seconds. **Cap dollars are not a tradeable asset either:** FR-32's Cap Space adjustment exists to correct a wrong imported figure, and settling a trade through it would record the act as something it is not.
- **Not a valuation engine.** No projections, rankings, ADP, or "suggested bid." The app enforces rules; it does not advise.
- **Not a chat app.** Discussion stays in Discord. The app posts to Discord; it does not host conversation.
- **No public/spectator access.** Authentication required for everything.

## 8. Success Metrics

**Primary**
- **SM-1: Zero disputed outcomes.** No auction result is contested on grounds that the app computed a cap figure, clock, increment, or lottery draw incorrectly. Validates FR-11 – FR-22.
- **SM-2: The commissioner plays their own league.** Commissioner time spent adjudicating the auction drops from hours to effectively zero — measured by **corrective** override count (FR-32), targeting fewer than 5 across the whole auction, none of them correcting an app error. **Roster Trades, Drops and Roster Moves (FR-41, FR-43, FR-44) are excluded from this count.** They record what the league did, not what the app got wrong; an offseason with a dozen trades is a busy league, not a failing product, and a metric that cannot tell those apart would pressure the Commissioner not to record them. Validates FR-1 – FR-3, FR-30, FR-32.
- **SM-3: Nobody loses a player to inattention they weren't warned about.** Every outbid manager received a notification before the auction closed against them. Target: 100%. Validates FR-26, FR-27.

**Secondary**
- **SM-4: Mobile is the primary surface.** A majority of bids are placed from a phone — evidence the one-handed flow actually works. Validates FR-23, FR-24.
- **SM-5: The auction completes without manual intervention.** Auction Phase ends by League Clock expiry, contract assignment completes, and export succeeds first-try with no cap or roster violations. Validates FR-22, FR-28 – FR-30.
- **SM-6: Managers use the audit trail.** The Audit Log and randomizer seeds get looked at at least once by someone other than the Commissioner — evidence that verifiable fairness is real rather than decorative. Validates FR-20, FR-33.
- **SM-7: The app's rosters stay true to the league of record.** No Manager discovers a wrong cap or roster figure caused by a trade or drop the app was never told about. Target: zero. Read alongside the count of divergences FR-42 raised that the Commissioner had **not** already recorded — which is the evidence the detector earns its place rather than duplicating a manual habit. Validates FR-41 – FR-44.

**Counter-metrics (do not optimize)**
- **SM-C1: Total bid volume.** More bidding is not better. A UI that nudges managers toward bidding — countdown urgency, one-tap raises without confirmation, suggested amounts — would raise this while degrading the auction. Counterbalances SM-4.
- **SM-C2: Auction duration.** Do not optimize for a faster auction. The 24h and 48h clocks exist to give people time to think; shortening the effective auction would look like efficiency and feel like pressure. Counterbalances SM-5.
- **SM-C3: Commissioner override count driven to zero by removing the tool.** SM-2 targets few overrides because few are *needed* — not because the escape hatch was made hard to reach. Counterbalances SM-2.
- **SM-C4: Divergence count driven to zero by not looking.** A detector that has silently stopped answering reports no divergences, which is indistinguishable from a league that had none — and is the more dangerous state, because it displaces the manual check it exists to support. SM-7's zero is only meaningful beside a *live* FR-42; a stale one invalidates the metric rather than satisfying it. Counterbalances SM-7.
- **SM-C5: Retractions treated as a feature to encourage.** FR-15 exists for the typo, and the only healthy retraction rate is the one the league's actual mistake rate produces. A rising ratio of retractions to Bids, or a small number of Managers accounting for most of them, means the window has become a negotiating instrument — bid to see what happens, withdraw if it goes badly — which is precisely what it was bounded to prevent. Watch the ratio and the per-Manager distribution, and treat a rise as a signal to tighten the window rather than as engagement. **Do not make retracting easier, faster, or more prominent** to improve any other metric on this list.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

- **A rules bug decides a player.** The one failure the league won't forgive. *Mitigation:* §10 worked examples as an executable test suite; server-side validation with no client-trusted path; append-only audit log making any error reconstructible and correctable via FR-32.
- **Timer misses fire during a deploy or restart.** Silently extends an auction and changes who wins. *Mitigation:* durable scheduled closes reconciled on startup; the 60-second close SLA in §5 tested against a forced restart.
- **Fantrax export format changes between offseasons.** Breaks import at the worst moment — setup day. *Mitigation:* validate and report on import rather than assuming shape; keep column mapping in one place (see `addendum.md`); import is re-runnable pre-open.
- **The minor-league-eligibility flag isn't in the Fantrax export — confirmed, no longer a risk but a design input.** FR-38 makes it commissioner-set. *The residual risk is real and, since 2026-09-18, much smaller:* the flag still depends on one person marking players correctly by hand, but it **no longer has economic weight at the auction**. A Player wrongly marked eligible used to let a capped-out Team win him at any price; now he costs exactly what he costs, and the only consequence of a wrong flag is that a Team can or cannot **stash a Player it already paid for** under FR-44. That is a real cost and it is recoverable by a logged override, where the old one decided who won a Player. *Mitigation:* default is *not* eligible so omission fails safe rather than permissive; FR-3 surfaces the flagged count for explicit confirmation before open; every change is logged, and post-open changes require an override with a reason.
- **A team arrives from import with a full roster and cannot bid at all.** Under FR-37 a Team at Roster Count 12 is locked out of **every** auction for the whole auction, no matter how much cap space it has. The Outstanding Bid Allowance does **not** rescue this Team — the allowance requires at least one free Slot, precisely so that a Team with none cannot win a thirteenth Player — so the risk survives the 2026-09-08 amendment unchanged. **Since 2026-09-18 it is worse**: such a Team used to keep one avenue open, bidding on Minor League Eligible Players into its free Minor League Slots, and that avenue was never real. This is correct, and it will feel broken the first time, and it will feel *arbitrary* as well, since other Teams are visibly bidding beyond their Slots. *Mitigation:* FR-37 requires the reason to be shown on the board rather than discovered at submission, and to distinguish "at your allowance" from "no Slots at all"; FR-1's per-Team preview shows Roster Count before the auction opens, so the condition is visible on setup day; **and a Team in this position has a remedy it did not have in August — a Roster Move under FR-44 demotes nobody but *promotes* an eligible Contract into the minors, which reopens an Active/Bench Slot and puts it back in the auction.**
- **A manager loses a bid they were winning, through no act of their own.** The Outstanding Bid Allowance means a Bid can be cancelled by a Close on an entirely different Auction (FR-40). The manager did nothing wrong, was not outbid, and may have been leading for a day; they will experience it as the app taking something away, and a single lottery win can cancel a whole book of commitments at once. *Mitigation:* the allowance must be visible **before** the bid is placed, not explained after it is cancelled — FR-11 requires the bid control to name which Bid is the allowance Bid and that it is the one at risk; FR-40 requires the cancellation notice to name the win that caused it; §10 example 31 works the case. This is a comprehension risk, not a correctness one, and it is the hardest sentence in the product to explain: *you may chase two players with one open slot, and one of those chases can end without you.*
- **A restored bidder is handed a win they no longer want, or can no longer afford.** FR-40 re-commits a Team's capital on an Auction it had stopped watching, possibly hours before that Auction closes. *Mitigation:* the restored Team is re-validated against the money and capacity gates at the moment of restoration and skipped if it fails, so it can never be restored into a breach; and it is notified rather than left to discover it. What the app does **not** do is let it decline, and FR-15's retraction window does not change that: the window is measured from a Bid's own timestamp, and a Bid old enough to be restored under FR-40 is long past its ninety seconds. A restored Bid is a Bid the Team really placed, and it is still stuck with it.
- **A manager will find the retraction window closed for a reason they cannot see.** FR-15's cut-short depends entirely on the *other* Team's activity, which the retracting Manager has no view of and no control over. They mistype $50,000,000, reach for the retract control forty seconds later, and are refused because a Team they have never interacted with had a Drop recorded against it. Experienced cold, that reads as the app breaking its own promise at the exact moment it mattered most. *Mitigation:* the control must show the **remaining time live and disappear the instant the window closes**, rather than sitting there and failing on submit; and the refusal must name **what closed it and when** — the displaced Team and the act — so the Manager sees a rule rather than a malfunction. This is a comprehension risk of the same family as FR-40's, and it lands on a Manager who has just made an expensive mistake.
- **A retraction can end the Auction Phase.** FR-15 removes the retracted Bid's League Clock reset, which is the Commissioner void's behaviour and is correct — a Bid that never counted did not buy the league another 48 hours. But on a quiet league at hour 47, the retracted Bid may be the only thing holding the Phase open, and taking back a typo ends the auction for everybody. *Mitigation:* the recomputation is **prospective only** (FR-22, §10 example 27), so nothing already accepted is invalidated and the Phase ends at the next evaluation rather than retroactively; and the retract control must state that the League Clock will recompute **before** the act commits, on the same reasoning FR-43's reason sheet states which way Maximum Bid will move. The alternative — letting a retraction keep its reset — would make the League Clock extendable by a Bid the league has agreed never counted.
- **A manager will expect to win a Minor League Eligible Player cheaply, and will be refused.** *(Replaces two risks retired on 2026-09-18 — "Minor League stashing looks like a bug to anyone who did not read FR-35" and "Overflow exposure is the non-obvious half of that rule". Both described the old rule, and both are gone with it.)* The stash is real, the $0 Cap Hit is real, and **neither is available at the moment of the win.** A Manager who knows the league — or who read an earlier draft of this document — will reason that an eligible Player costs him nothing and bid accordingly, and the app will refuse him against his ordinary Maximum Bid. Experienced cold that reads as the app not knowing the league's own rule. *Mitigation:* FR-38's setting screen must state the consequence in words that do not promise a cheaper **win**; FR-13's refusal names the Auctions holding the money, so the Manager sees where it went rather than a bare negative; and FR-44 must be discoverable from a Team's own roster view, because it is the act that delivers what the Manager was expecting, one step later than they expected it.
- **The retired overflow arithmetic will be reintroduced by somebody being helpful.** `Eligible Leading Bids`, `Overflow Count`, `Minors Exposure` and `Active/Bench Overflow` are permanently zero rather than deleted, and they are still computed — through their own arithmetic over an empty set — in the shipped code. A reader who finds a figure that is always zero, or an eligibility flag the bidding gates ignore, will reasonably conclude something is unwired and connect it. That restores a rule the league does not have, silently, on the one path where being wrong decides who gets a Player. *Mitigation:* Minor League Eligibility is **removed from** `bidStateFor` and `teamMoneyStateFor` rather than defaulted, so no caller can reintroduce the dependency by passing a value, and the wire does not carry it to the browser's own rebuild; the §3 Glossary keeps the four names with the reason they are zero; and §10 examples 16–22, 25 and 35 are **retired rather than deleted**, each carrying a regression that fails if the old placement returns.
- **~~The two overflow figures will be implemented as one.~~ RETIRED 2026-09-18** — resolved by subtraction rather than by mitigation. The money side and the slots side were one subtraction apart and could be computed once and used twice by accident; both are now permanently zero, so there is nothing left to conflate. **Projected Active/Bench Additions and Reserve Additions still differ**, by a Team's held Minimum-Bid Contention entries, and that pair carries the same hazard — it is stated in the Glossary at both terms and exercised by §10 example 34.
- **A dead nomination locks a manager out for the whole auction.** Accepted by league decision, but it will feel bad the first time it happens. *Mitigation:* FR-10 makes it loud and early rather than a discovery; FR-32 lets the Commissioner release the slot if the league decides to.
- **Discord is a single point of failure for both sign-in and alerting.** Since FR-4 authenticates through Discord and FR-27 notifies through it, a Discord outage locks every Manager out *and* silences every alert at the same moment, while the Auction Clocks keep running. This is the cost of the zero-budget, single-channel design and it is accepted knowingly. *Mitigation:* sessions persist 30 days so an outage does not log anyone out; FR-4 provides a Commissioner sign-in independent of Discord so FR-34's pause stays reachable; delivery is decoupled from auction state so a failed post never reverses a Bid; and the operator's own liveness alerting must not route through Discord, since it may need to report that Discord is down. An outage beyond the recovery threshold is a pause, not a wait-and-see.
- **Every Manager must have a Discord account.** FR-4 makes this load-bearing for *access*, not merely for convenience. *Mitigation:* confirm all 31 before setup day, not on it.
- **Solo-builder bus factor.** One person builds and operates this, live, for 31 people. *Mitigation:* Commissioner pause (FR-34) as the universal escape hatch; keep the deployment boring.

## 10. Rule Resolution Examples

*These exist to remove interpretation from the implementer. Each should become an automated test.*

*Nine examples — 16–22, 25 and 35 — were **retired on 2026-09-18**, when the league corrected the rule that a Free Agent could be won straight into the minors. Each is kept in place with a record of what it said and why it is gone, and each has a regression in `tests/examples/` that fails if the old rule returns; 38, 43 and 45 are rewritten, and 45's conclusion inverts. Note also that examples 26–34 still qualify their Players as “non-eligible” and still specify a Team's free Minor League Slots. Those are **setup details that no longer carry weight** — they were written to keep those examples clear of FR-35 — and they are left as written rather than swept, because nothing in them is wrong. **No example distinguishes an eligible Player from any other any more**, and one that appeared to would be a defect.*

1. **Ordinary raise.** Player at $8,000,000 held by Team A. Team B bids $8,500,000 — valid (exactly +$500k). Clock resets to 24h. A's $8,000,000 commitment releases; B's $8,500,000 commits.
2. **Insufficient increment, and off-grid.** Same auction. Team B bids $8,400,000 — refused on **both** grounds: it is below `current high + $500,000` and it is not a whole multiple of $500,000. Board and clock unchanged. Note that under $500,000 granularity these two grounds cannot be separated in Standard Contention — the next grid value above $8,000,000 is $8,500,000, which is exactly one increment — so no test can exercise a sub-increment bid that is on-grid. The granularity check earns its keep in Minimum-Bid Contention instead (example 10).
3. **Roster reserve bites.** Team C: Cap Space $12,000,000, Roster Count 9, no leading bids. Maximum Bid = $12,000,000 − ($1,000,000 × (12 − 10)) = $10,000,000. A $10,500,000 bid is refused with the arithmetic shown.
4. **Reserve clears as commitments accumulate.** Same Team C, now leading two auctions at $3,000,000 and $2,000,000. Available Cap Space $7,000,000; reserve = $1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − (9 + 2 + 1)) = $0. Maximum Bid $7,000,000.
5. **Outbid frees capital immediately.** Team C is outbid on the $3,000,000 auction. Available Cap Space returns to $10,000,000; Projected Active/Bench Additions drops to 2, so reserve becomes $1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − 11) = $1,000,000; Maximum Bid $9,000,000.
6. **Lottery opens.** Player nominated by Team D, opened by Team E at exactly $1,000,000 at 09:00 Monday. State = Minimum-Bid Contention, close fixed at 09:00 Tuesday. E commits $1,000,000.
7. **Lottery grows, clock unmoved.** Teams F, G, H each bid exactly $1,000,000 at 14:00, 20:00, and 08:55 Tuesday. All join; each commits $1,000,000; close time remains 09:00 Tuesday.
8. **Lottery draws.** At 09:00 Tuesday the Randomizer draws from [E, F, G, H]. Seed, ordered list, and selection are recorded and displayed. Winner holds a $1,000,000 Auction Contract; the other three release their commitments. Team D's Nomination Slot releases.
9. **Lottery dissolves.** Same auction, but Team I bids $1,500,000 at 20:30 Monday. Contenders E, F, G release immediately; I becomes Leading Bidder; state = Standard Contention; close resets to 20:30 Tuesday. The next valid bid is $2,000,000.
10. **The dead zone.** In a Minimum-Bid Contention, a bid of $1,200,000 is refused — too high to join the lottery, too low to convert it, and off-grid besides. Under $500,000 granularity the dead zone contains **no** legal amount at all: between $1,000,000 (join) and $1,500,000 (convert) there is no whole multiple of $500,000. The zone is therefore reachable only by an off-grid submission, which is exactly the case the granularity check exists to catch.
11. **Single-contender lottery.** Only Team E ever bids $1,000,000. At expiry, E wins outright; the draw is recorded with a one-team list.
12. **Nomination slot held by an unbid player.** Team J nominates a player at 10:00 Monday. Nobody bids. At 10:00 Tuesday the board flags it and J is notified. J still cannot nominate again. The player remains until the Auction Phase ends, then returns to the pool unclaimed.
13. **League clock.** Last bid league-wide lands 12:00 Friday; last nomination 09:00 Friday. An auction closes 12:00 Saturday — this does *not* reset the League Clock. With no further nomination or bid, the Auction Phase ends 12:00 Sunday.
14. **Allotment exhaustion.** Team K won five players and has already used its 4-year, 3-year, and both 2-year deals. Every remaining player must be assigned 1 year; longer options are unavailable.
15. **Co-manager race.** Both of Team L's Managers submit a bid on the same auction within the same second. Exactly one is accepted; the other is refused because the price moved, and the Audit Log names which Manager placed the accepted bid.
16. **Minors placement. — RETIRED 2026-09-18.** *(Retired, not deleted: the number stays spent so no future example reuses it, and the test file that **was** this example is now the regression that keeps it retired — `tests/examples/example-16-retired-the-win-that-cannot-land-in-minors.test.ts`. Examples 16–22, 25 and 35 are retired together, by one rule correction.)* It read: Team M holds two players in Minor League Slots and wins a Minor League Eligible player at $4,000,000; he takes the third Minor League Slot at a **$0 Cap Hit** and Roster Count is unchanged. **A Team cannot win a Free Agent straight into its minors.** The same close now lands him in an **Active/Bench Slot** at the full **$4,000,000**, Roster Count goes 9 → 10, and Team M's Minor League occupancy stays at two. Every figure the example asserted is inverted, which is what makes the file fail if the old placement ever returns. **What has *not* changed is `slotPlacementFor`**: the same function still answers `minor_league` for a Contract arriving by **Trade** (FR-41), because a Contract that arrives by trade is already settled rather than being won onto a roster. The retired file asserts both facts side by side — a close that no longer consults it, beside a trade that still does — and the two together are the whole of what this retirement changed. **AD-23 survives and is still the point:** winning amount and Cap Hit remain two persisted fields rather than one figure read twice. They simply agree on this close now.
17. **Minors overflow. — RETIRED 2026-09-18.** It read: same Team M immediately wins a second eligible player at $3,000,000; all three Minor League Slots now being occupied, he takes an Active/Bench Slot at a $3,000,000 Cap Hit and Roster Count increases by one. **There is no overflow any more, because there is no stashing at a close.** The second win reaches the destination the example predicted — Active/Bench, full Cap Hit — but by the **ordinary rule** rather than by exhausting three Minor League Slots, and Team M arrives at this close with one Minor League Slot still **free** and takes an Active/Bench Slot anyway. **The example's answer survived its reason**, which is exactly why it had to be retired rather than kept: a file that still passed for the wrong reason would be worse than one that failed. **AD-11 survives untouched and is still worth the file** — each close's effect must be committed to the state the next close is evaluated against, and folding an overdue set against one loaded snapshot is still a defect. What changed is which figure demonstrates it: **Roster Count** now carries the sequencing (9 → 10 → 11) where Minor League occupancy used to.
18. **Stashing beats the cap, on purpose. — RETIRED 2026-09-18.** It read: Team P, $2,000,000 under the cap with one Free Minor League Slot (`M = 1`) and Roster Count 11, bids **$30,000,000** on a Minor League Eligible player; Minors Exposure $0, Roster Capacity passing at `11 + 0`, the bid **permitted**, and the auction page showing *"no cap limit"* rather than a figure. **Team P is now refused**, against a Maximum Bid of $2,000,000. The $0 Cap Hit that made the amount unbounded arrives only **after** the Manager moves him down under FR-44, and until the Auction closes he cannot be moved at all — so the money is held in full for the Auction's whole life, exactly as any other lead's is. Everything the example turned on goes with it: the `unbounded` branch is unreachable, *"no cap limit"* is never rendered, Minors Exposure is a permanent $0 over an empty set, and the win projects an Active/Bench addition like any other. **The Roster Reserve half survives on its own terms** — *"unbounded is not a waiver"* was always the claim that `Available Cap Space − Roster Reserve ≥ 0` still decides, and it still does, as the ordinary money gate with nothing special about an eligible Player.
19. **Overflow refuses the cheap bid, not the expensive one. — RETIRED 2026-09-18.** It read: Team P, still holding that $30,000,000 lead, bids **$1,000,000** on a second eligible player and is refused by $30,000,000 of Minors Exposure, the message naming the $30,000,000 auction as the cause. **The lesson survives; the mechanism does not.** The $30,000,000 lead commits its full amount from the moment it is placed, so Team P is $28,000,000 underwater before it types anything and the cheap Bid is refused for the plainest possible reason. That keeps the example's actual point, which was never really about minors: **a *later* Bid is refused, whatever its size, and nothing reaches back to invalidate an accepted one** — the opposite of the intuition a Manager arrives with. **Roster Capacity still passes and still isolates the money**, now through the Outstanding Bid Allowance: two projected additions against one Free Active/Bench Slot is exactly the allowance, so the slots gate passes while reporting that Roster Count *would be* 13 of 12, and FR-40 cancels the surplus the moment a win leaves it nowhere to land. **One capability was genuinely lost here**, and it is recorded rather than quietly dropped: the refusal named the specific earlier Auction creating the exposure, because FR-35 required it and `exposingBids` carried the set. Committed Bids had no equivalent itemisation, so the refusal read only *"$1.5M exceeds your Maximum Bid of −$28.0M"*. **FR-13 now requires that naming back**, from the `leading` list that held it all along.
20. **A resolved win stops being exposure. — RETIRED 2026-09-18, and renamed on the way out: *a resolved win stops being a commitment*.** It read: the $30,000,000 auction closes, the player takes Team P's last Free Minor League Slot at a **$0 Cap Hit**, he stops being an Eligible Leading Bid, and the exposure he created disappears — *"an open leading bid creates exposure while a closed one does not."* **The lesson survives in a stronger form; the arithmetic does not.** The close produces a **$30,000,000 Cap Hit in an Active/Bench Slot**, not a $0 one. The $30,000,000 still stops being a commitment, and for exactly the reason the example gave — the fold stops returning the Auction and the amount stops appearing — but it **reappears as a Cap Hit instead of vanishing**. That makes the point cleaner than it was: money does not disappear at a close, it **moves**, out of Committed Bids and into the Cap Space the Contract now charges, with Available Cap Space landing in the same place either way. The old $0 Cap Hit hid that conservation. **The example still needs no code at all, and that is still the finding** (AD-7): nothing sweeps the closed Auction out and nothing recomputes a stored figure. **Team P's figures are re-founded — Cap Space $32,000,000, Roster Count 10 — and that is not a fudge**: a Team holding a $30,000,000 lead against $2,000,000 of Cap Space is a position the gates now make unreachable, and a fixture built on it would assert against a state that cannot exist.
21. **A lottery on an eligible player commits nothing. — RETIRED 2026-09-18, and the sentence it turned on now reads the other way.** It read: Team Q, capped out at $0 of Available Cap Space but with all three Minor League Slots free, joins a $1,000,000 contention on a Minor League Eligible fringe player — `N = 1` against `M = 3`, Minors Exposure $0, the join **permitted despite no cap space at all** — and closed with *"had the same lottery been on a player who is not Minor League Eligible, the flat $1,000,000 of FR-14 would apply and Team Q could not join."* **Both halves of that sentence now read the same way, and the second one won.** A Contender who wins an eligible lottery lands in Active/Bench and pays, exactly as a Contender in a non-eligible one does, so FR-14's flat $1,000,000 applies to **every** contention and Team Q — with $0 of Available Cap Space — can join none of them. The two states the retired file builds are now literally the same object, and that indistinguishability **is** the regression: if eligibility ever re-enters the gates, they stop matching and the file fails. **This is the FR-14-versus-FR-35 contradiction resolving the other way** (§12). **What survives untouched is FR-18's *capacity* exemption:** an entry still contributes nothing to Projected Active/Bench Additions and still does not consume the Outstanding Bid Allowance, because that rule rests on its own reasoning — the expected outcome of a lottery is losing — and on FR-40 cancelling entries before the draw. It was always a capacity exemption and never a cap one; it is simply no longer entangled with minors.
22. **A lottery that overflows does commit. — RETIRED 2026-09-18.** It read: the same Team Q, now leading three eligible auctions at $5,000,000, $4,000,000 and $3,000,000 against its three free slots, tries to join a fourth eligible contention — `N = 4` against `M = 3`, Overflow Count 1, Minors Exposure $5,000,000 — and is **refused**, the message naming the $5,000,000 auction. **The pair was the point, and the pair is gone:** 21 and 22 differed only in whether a fourth eligible win had a Free Minor League Slot to land in, and no win lands in one. All three standing leads commit their full amounts from the moment they are placed, and the fourth join is refused for the same reason every other over-commitment is. **The refusal survives, and so does "the money is the sole ground"** — Team Q still has $0 of Available Cap Space and the capacity gate still passes, Roster Count 11 leaving the one Free Active/Bench Slot that is FR-18's landing test. Only the term holding the money changed. **Cap Space is re-founded from $5,000,000 to $12,000,000, and that is not a fudge:** the old figure reached "$0 Available Cap Space" only because $12,000,000 of leads were charging $5,000,000 of exposure, and a Team holding $12,000,000 of commitments against $5,000,000 of space is a position the gates could never have let it reach. **The refusal no longer names the Auction holding the money** — see example 19's retirement, and FR-13, which requires the naming back.
23. **IR does not fill the twelve.** Team N holds 11 players in Active/Bench Slots and 1 in an Injury Reserve Slot. Roster Count is 11, not 12, so with no leading bids its Projected Active/Bench Additions on a new bid is 1 and Roster Reserve is `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − 12) = $0` — the bid itself fills the last hole. Capacity passes with room to spare: Free Active/Bench Slots is 1, so `1 ≤ 1 + 1` and Team N could hold a second outstanding bid as well. Adding a second IR player would not change either figure. *(Read “on a new **non-eligible** bid” until 2026-09-18. There is one kind of bid now.)*
24. **A full roster ends bidding, money or not — and the allowance does not rescue it.** Team R holds 12 players in Active/Bench Slots, has $40,000,000 of Cap Space, and leads nothing. It bids $5,000,000 on any Player at all. Projected Active/Bench Additions is 1 — as it is for every Bid, since every win lands in Active/Bench — and Free Active/Bench Slots is **0**, so the rule fails its precondition before its arithmetic is even reached. The bid is **refused** — with the capacity arithmetic, not a cap figure, since Maximum Bid here is a healthy $40,000,000. Note carefully that `Free Active/Bench Slots + 1 = 1` would have *admitted* this bid had the precondition not been there: Team R would then win a thirteenth player, with no other close available to cancel the surplus first. The precondition is what makes the allowance safe, and this is the example that proves it. Team R's controls are disabled on **every** auction on the board with the reason shown, not only at submission. This state is reachable straight from import. *(Amended 2026-09-18: the headline read “ends **non-eligible** bidding” and the Player was specified as not Minor League Eligible, because a Team in this position could supposedly still bid into its free Minor League Slots. It cannot, and never could — retired example 25 is that half of the case, now indistinguishable from this one.)*
25. **The same full roster can still stash, until it overflows. — RETIRED 2026-09-18.** It read: Team R, at Roster Count 12 with all three Minor League Slots free, bids $9,000,000 on a Minor League Eligible player — Projected Active/Bench Additions **0**, the `P = 0` branch, which needed no free Active/Bench Slot and was therefore untouched by the allowance's precondition — **permitted**, Maximum Bid unbounded; it leads all three eligible auctions within its three slots, and is refused only on a fourth. **A Team at Roster Capacity can now bid on nobody at all.** It cannot win a Free Agent straight into its minors: it has to fit him on its active roster first, and Team R has room for nobody. **The three Free Minor League Slots are real and they are useless** — they can only ever receive a Player the Team **already holds**, moved down under FR-44. So the **first** bid is refused, not the fourth, and "until it overflows" has nothing left to describe; `P = 0` is unreachable for an ordinary Bid, because every win projects an Active/Bench addition. **This is example 24's Team reaching example 24's outcome, and the pair is now a single case** — which is what makes the retired file a regression: it runs an eligible Bid and a non-eligible one through the same gates and asserts they are indistinguishable. **FR-37's own property survives untouched:** money makes no difference in either direction, and Team R is refused at $0, $40,000,000 and $400,000,000 of Cap Space alike, because a capacity refusal quotes counts and never a dollar.
26. **Off-grid amounts are refused everywhere.** A player stands at $6,000,000 in Standard Contention. A bid of $6,750,000 is refused as off-grid, though it clears the increment. In a separate Minimum-Bid Contention, a bid of $1,000,001 is refused as off-grid, though it is neither a valid join nor a conversion either. The granularity check runs independently of contention state.
27. **A voided bid shortens the League Clock.** The last two league-wide events are a Nomination at 09:00 Monday and a Bid at 15:00 Monday, so the League Clock expires 15:00 Wednesday. The Commissioner voids that Bid at 18:00 Monday with a reason. The League Clock is recomputed from the surviving reset events and now expires **09:00 Wednesday**, six hours earlier. The auction the voided bid was on returns to its prior Leading Bidder and prior Auction Clock value. Nothing that happened between 15:00 and 18:00 Monday is invalidated. Had the recomputed expiry already passed, the Auction Phase would end at the next clock evaluation rather than retroactively.

28. **The median lands between two grid values.** The 30 Teams' Available Cap Space figures are sorted ascending; the 15th is $4,000,000 and the 16th is $4,500,000. The conventional median — the mean of the two middle values — is **$4,250,000**, which is *not* a whole multiple of $500,000 and cannot be rendered losslessly at one decimal: `$4.3M` is wrong by $50,000, and `$4.25M` breaks the one-decimal rule the entire money rendering depends on. The **League Median is therefore $4,000,000**, the lower middle value, which renders as `$4.0M` and is a figure a real Team actually holds. The same rule applies to slots: with a 15th value of 2 Free Active/Bench Slots and a 16th of 3, the League Median is **2**, not 2.5 — half a roster slot is not a thing that exists. The lesson is that the $500,000 grid is a product-wide invariant, not a bidding rule: **any new derived figure must land on it, and an aggregate is the easiest place to fall off it.**

*Added 2026-09-08 with the Outstanding Bid Allowance (FR-37) and Bid Cancellation (FR-40):*

29. **The allowance, in the ordinary case.** Team S has Roster Count 11, $20,000,000 of Cap Space, no minor-league involvement, and already leads one non-eligible auction at $6,000,000. Free Active/Bench Slots is 1. It bids on a second non-eligible player: Projected Active/Bench Additions is `1 (the existing lead) + 1 (this bid) = 2`, and `2 ≤ 1 + 1`, so capacity **passes**. Roster Reserve is `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − (11 + 2)) = $0` — the clamp doing real work for the first time — Available Cap Space is $14,000,000, and Maximum Bid is $14,000,000. **Under the pre-2026-09-08 rule this bid was refused** at `11 + 2 = 13 > 12`; the allowance is exactly the difference. A *third* outstanding bid gives `P = 3 > 2` and is **refused on capacity**, with the message stating that the allowance is already spent rather than implying the Team is out of slots.
30. **The allowance needs a slot to extend.** Team T has Roster Count 12, no free Minor League Slots, $40,000,000 of Cap Space, and leads nothing. It bids $5,000,000 on a non-eligible player. Projected Active/Bench Additions is 1 and Free Active/Bench Slots is 0, so the precondition fails. **Refused on capacity.** Read the arithmetic that *would* have applied: `Free Active/Bench Slots + 1 = 0 + 1 = 1`, and `P = 1 ≤ 1` — the bid would have been admitted, Team T would have won a thirteenth player, and no other close would have existed to cancel it. This is example 24's lesson stated as arithmetic, and it is the reason the rule is not a flat ceiling of 13.
31. **The cascade fires, and only as far as it must.** Team U has Roster Count 10, so Free Active/Bench Slots is 2 and it may hold 3 outstanding bids. It leads three non-eligible auctions: Anderson at $4,000,000 (sequence 100), Brooks at $3,000,000 (sequence 140), Carter at $2,000,000 (sequence 190). `P = 3 ≤ 2 + 1` — permitted. **Anderson closes.** Team U wins, Roster Count becomes 11, Free Active/Bench Slots becomes 1, and it now holds 2 surviving commitments against an allowance of `1 + 1 = 2`. It is within capacity, so **nothing is cancelled** — the cascade is conditional, not a reflex. **Brooks then closes.** Roster Count becomes 12, Free Active/Bench Slots becomes 0, and the single surviving commitment on Carter has `P = 1 ≠ 0` with no free slot. **Carter's bid is cancelled** — it is the most recent survivor at sequence 190 — Team U's $2,000,000 is released, and Team U is notified naming *the Brooks win* as the cause. On Carter's auction the next-highest surviving bid is Team V's $1,500,000; Team V passes both gates, becomes Leading Bidder, has $1,500,000 re-committed, and is notified that it is winning again. **Carter's Auction Clock is untouched** and still expires when it always would have. The League Clock does not move, and Team U's original bid stays visible in Carter's history.
32. **A restoration that is skipped, not undone.** Carter's history below Team U reads: Team V at $1,500,000 (sequence 150), Team W at $1,000,000 (sequence 120). By the time Team U's bid is cancelled, Team V has itself reached Roster Count 12. Team V is tried first and **fails the capacity gate**, so it is skipped — it is never restored and then cancelled, because a restoration must not trigger a cascade. Team W is tried next, passes both gates, and becomes Leading Bidder at $1,000,000. The auction's price falls from $2,000,000 to $1,000,000, which is correct: $1,000,000 is what the highest team still able to win him actually offered.
33. **A restoration with nothing to restore.** Same cancellation, but Team U's $2,000,000 on Carter was the **Opening Bid** and nobody else ever bid. There is no next-highest bid, so the auction returns to **Awaiting Opening Bid**, and its Auction Clock is **cleared** rather than left running — an auction with no bid has no clock, exactly as a nomination nobody has bid on has none. Carter stays on the Bid Board and the nominating Team's Nomination Slot stays held (§10 example 12). Any team may open him again at $1,000,000 or more, starting a fresh 24 hours; if nobody does, he returns to the Free Agent pool unclaimed when the Auction Phase ends. Note what must **not** happen: the auction does not close at Team U's old expiry with no winner, and the nomination slot is not released early.
34. **Unlimited lotteries, and the one win that ends them.** Team X has Roster Count 11 (Free Active/Bench Slots 1), no free Minor League Slots, and $9,000,000 of Available Cap Space. It joins six Minimum-Bid Contentions on non-eligible players, committing $1,000,000 to each. **Capacity is never consulted:** entries contribute nothing to Projected Active/Bench Additions and do not consume the allowance, so the only question asked of the sixth is whether $6,000,000 fits in $9,000,000. A seventh, eighth and ninth would also be permitted; a **tenth is refused on money** at $10,000,000 against $9,000,000 — cap space is the throttle, and the only one. Three of the six expire in the same sweep. The first is drawn and **Team X wins**: Roster Count 12, Free Active/Bench Slots 0. Before the second is drawn, the cascade cancels Team X's five remaining entries most-recent-first, releasing $5,000,000. The second lottery is therefore drawn from a Contender list that **does not include Team X**, and its recorded list contains exactly the teams that were eligible to win it. One of the five had Team X as its **only** Contender: it closes with no winner and that player returns to the pool. The sequential close ordering is what makes this safe — folding all three draws against one snapshot would have let Team X win two of them.
35. **The trigger is a free slot, not a roster count. — RETIRED 2026-09-18.** It read: Team Y, at Roster Count 12 with one Free Minor League Slot, is a Contender in three Minimum-Bid Contentions on **Minor League Eligible** players, all three permitted under FR-18's `M ≥ 1` branch despite no Active/Bench room; the first is drawn, Team Y wins into the last Minor League Slot at a $0 Cap Hit with **Roster Count still 12**, and the close fires the cascade anyway **because it reduced a free slot** — where a trigger phrased as *"a close that increases Roster Count"* would not have fired, and Team Y would have finished at Roster Count 14. **Both halves are gone, and for one reason.** FR-18's `M ≥ 1` branch is removed: the only Slot that can receive a lottery win is an Active/Bench one, so a Team with no Active/Bench room may enter **no** contention at all and never reaches the state the second half describes. **The trigger distinction this example existed to protect has lost its witness, and that is the finding worth recording.** Every Auction win now lands in Active/Bench and therefore raises Roster Count, so the two phrasings fire identically on **every close**; this example was the one case that told them apart. **FR-40's wording should still not be narrowed to Roster Count** — a Roster Move or a Trade can still free or fill a Minor League Slot without touching the twelve — but a **Close** can no longer demonstrate it, and the retired file says so rather than pretending otherwise. **The cascade itself survives** and is kept verbatim: the close still fires it, still cancels both remaining entries, still names the win as the cause, and still takes Team Y out of both Contender lists before either draws. Roster Count is re-founded at **11**, because at 12 Team Y could not have entered the three lotteries it is holding.

*Added 2026-09-10 with Roster Trades (FR-41), Roster Divergence (FR-42) and Dead Money (FR-43):*

36. **The trade that clears the room.** Team A has Roster Count 11, Cap Space $3,000,000, and leads nothing. It wants a $12,000,000 free agent and cannot come close. It sends Curry — Active/Bench, Cap Hit $14,000,000, 2 years — to Team B and receives nothing back. **An empty direction is legal:** a salary dump is a Roster Trade with Contracts moving one way, and FR-41's bidirectionality permits a side to be empty, not merely to be balanced. Team A afterwards: Roster Count 10, Cap Space $17,000,000; bidding once, Projected Additions 1, Roster Reserve `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − 11) = $1,000,000`, **Maximum Bid $16,000,000**. Team B afterwards: Roster Count 9, Cap Space $16,000,000. Both pass both gates, so the Trade commits. This is the case the requirement exists for.
37. **The Team pushed over by giving something away.** Team C has Roster Count 11, Cap Space $6,700,000, and leads one Auction at $6,500,000. Today: Projected Additions 1, Roster Reserve `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − 12) = $0`, and it covers its own leading Bid with $200,000 to spare. It now sends away a Player whose Cap Hit is $500,000. Afterwards Cap Space is $7,200,000 — but Roster Count is 10, so Roster Reserve becomes `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − 11) = $1,000,000`, and `$7,200,000 − $6,500,000 − $1,000,000 = −$300,000`. **The Trade is refused**, naming the Auction Team C leads and the $300,000 shortfall. 38. **The stash that becomes expensive by moving.** Team D holds Ellis, Minor League Eligible, won at $18,000,000 — landed in an **Active/Bench Slot** like every win, and **moved down into a Minor League Slot by Team D itself under FR-44**, where he charges a **$0 Cap Hit**. *(That first step is new since 2026-09-18: a win cannot arrive in the minors, so every stash in this league got there by an act or by the import.)* It trades him to Team E, which already holds three Minor League Players and has no Free Minor League Slot. *Added 2026-09-16 with the retraction window (FR-15). Examples 1 – 46 are unchanged. Note that 47 – 57 turn on **seconds**: the window is ninety of them, and an example written in whole minutes would not fit inside it — and **57 turns on milliseconds**, because it is the boundary instant itself that is in question:*

47. **The typo the window exists for.** Team A has Cap Space $60,000,000, Roster Count 9 and leads nothing, so bidding once its Roster Reserve is `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − 10) = $2,000,000` and its **Maximum Bid is $58,000,000**. Player X is held by Team B at $4,500,000. A means $5,000,000 and sends **$50,000,000**. B is released and notified it was outbid; the Auction Clock resets to 24 hours; the League Clock resets to 48. **Forty seconds later A retracts.** B is restored at $4,500,000 and re-committed, A's $50,000,000 releases, the Auction Clock returns to the instant **B's** Bid had set rather than to 24 hours from A's, and A's League Clock reset is removed. A then bids **$5,000,000**, which is legal on every ground and which **no floor forbids**. The Audit Log carries three lines — the $50,000,000, the retraction, the $5,000,000 — and the price ends where A meant it to be.
48. **The retraction that closes the Auction immediately.** Team B has led Player Y at $4,500,000 since 09:00 Monday, so the Auction Clock stands at **09:00 Tuesday**. At **08:59:20 Tuesday** Team A bids $5,000,000 and the Clock resets to 08:59:20 Wednesday. At **09:00:30 Tuesday** — seventy seconds later, inside the window, and B has done nothing at all — A retracts. The Auction Clock is **restored to 09:00 Tuesday, thirty seconds in the past.** The Auction closes at the next sweep and **B wins at $4,500,000**. This is correct rather than a race: it is precisely what would have happened had A never bid, and it is the "late, not wrong" posture FR-21's sweep already runs on. An implementation that left the Clock at 08:59:20 Wednesday would hand B an extra day for nothing, and one that refused the retraction because the restored instant is past would leave A stuck with a Bid the rule says it may take back.
49. **The change that cuts the window short, and the change that does not.** Team A outbids Team B on Player X at **14:00:00**. Two continuations, thirty seconds apart, opposite outcomes. **(a)** At 14:00:30 **B bids on Player Y.** B has committed capital it did not have committed at 14:00, so restoring it on X could now fail: A's window is **over**, and a retraction at 14:01:00 is **refused, naming B's Bid on Y**. **(b)** Instead, at 14:00:30 **Team C outbids B on Player Z.** B's committed capital **falls** — it is strictly better able to take X back than it was at 14:00 — so **A's window is untouched** and a retraction at 14:01:00 **succeeds**. The two differ only in the direction B moved, which is the whole of the rule. A cut-short written as "has anything about B changed?" would refuse both, and would refuse (b) to protect B from an outcome that helps it.
50. **The Drop that closes the window by freeing a Slot.** Team A outbids Team B on Player X at **14:00:00**. At **14:00:40** the Commissioner records a **Drop** for Team B — an Active/Bench Player charging $2,000,000. B's Roster Count falls from 10 to 9 and its Cap Space is **unchanged**, because the $2,000,000 becomes Dead Money (FR-43); but Roster Reserve rises from `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − 11) = $1,000,000` to `$1,000,000 × max(0, 12 − 10) = $2,000,000`, so B's Maximum Bid **falls by $1,000,000** and it may no longer cover the Bid it would be restored to. A's retraction at 14:01:10 is **refused**. Read what happened: **B became harder to restore by an act that gave it a Roster Slot**, which is why the cut-short is tested against the four restoration figures rather than against a list of bid-shaped acts. This is example 40's arithmetic reached from the other side.
51. **Leaving a lottery, and emptying one.** Player Y is in Minimum-Bid Contention, opened by Team E at 09:00 Monday and joined by Teams F and G; the Auction Clock stands at 09:00 Tuesday and does not move (FR-18). G joined at **11:00:00**, and at **11:01:10** — seventy seconds later — **Team G retracts**. The Contender list becomes **E, F**, G's $1,000,000 releases, and the **Auction Clock is untouched in both directions**: G's join never reset it, so the retraction has no clock value to restore. **No seed is revealed and nothing is dissolved** — a shrinking list is not FR-19's conversion, and the draw opens the envelope at expiry as it always would. **Now the other shape.** Player Z was opened at exactly $1,000,000 by Team H and nobody has joined. H retracts eighty seconds later. The history is **exhausted**, so the Auction returns to **Awaiting Opening Bid** with its Clock **cleared** — the Opening Bid was what started it (FR-17) — and Player Z stays on the Board with the Nomination Slot still held (FR-9).
52. **The chain that terminates without a depth limit.** Team C bids $6,000,000 on Player X at **13:00:00**. Team B bids $6,500,000 at **17:00:00**. Team A bids $7,000,000 at **17:00:30**. At **17:01:00** A retracts — its Bid is thirty seconds old, and B has not moved — so **B is restored** at $6,500,000. B's *own* Bid is now sixty seconds old, still inside **its** window, which the restoration did **not** refresh: it runs from 17:00:00 and closes at 17:01:30, not from 17:01:00. C has not moved either, so at **17:01:20 B may retract as well**, and C is restored at $6,000,000. **There the chain stops.** C's Bid is four hours old and its window closed at 13:01:30. Each step down the history is anchored to a strictly older Bid, so the chain shortens by construction and terminates with no depth limit anywhere in the rule — the same argument FR-40 makes for its cascade, reached differently. Note also that the three windows are independent: A's was governed by B's figures, B's by C's, and C's by whoever C outbid.
53. **The window that outlived the Auction.** Player Y is in Minimum-Bid Contention with Teams E and F, and the Auction Clock stands at **09:00 Tuesday**. At **08:59:30** Team G joins at exactly $1,000,000. The Clock does **not** move — a join never resets it (FR-18) — so at 09:00 the Auction expires with the Contender list **E, F, G**, the Randomizer draws, and the seed and the ordered list are recorded permanently (FR-20). At **09:00:40** Team G is still inside the ninety seconds its entry earned, which do not run out until 09:01:00. **It may not retract.** The draw closed the window, and closed it identically whether G won or lost: a retraction accepted at 09:00:40 would unwind a result already published with the seed that produced it, which every Manager can reproduce. **This is the only place in the product where an Auction can close inside a Bid's own retraction window**, and it exists solely because a contention join does not reset the Clock. In Standard Contention the case is unreachable — a Bid resets the Clock to 24 hours, so the Auction cannot close for a day.
54. **The stall the League Clock rule closes.** The league has gone quiet and the latest surviving League Clock reset is 46 hours old, so the Auction Phase has **two hours left**. Team A wants more time. It joins an open $1,000,000 Minimum-Bid Contention at **20:00:00**, which resets the League Clock to 48 hours (FR-18). At **20:01:10**, inside its ninety seconds and with nobody displaced to cut it short, A **retracts** — and the reset goes with the Bid, so the Clock falls back to **two hours remaining**. A rejoins at **20:01:20**. **That Bid resets nothing**, because A retracted a Bid less than ninety seconds earlier (FR-15), and the Clock still reads two hours. A may repeat this until the Auction Phase ends and will never gain a minute from it. What A *can* do is join once and **keep** the entry: that resets the Clock honestly, and costs A $1,000,000 of committed capital and a real chance of winning a Player it did not want. **That trade is what the League Clock has always asked for**, and the rule exists so that retraction cannot buy the extension without paying it.
55. **The anchor the stall rule hangs on.** Continues example 54's setup — the Auction Phase has **two hours left** on a League Clock whose latest surviving reset is 46 hours old. Team A joins an open $1,000,000 Minimum-Bid Contention at **20:00:00**, resetting the League Clock to 48 hours (FR-18). At **20:01:25** — eighty-five seconds later, still inside its window — A **retracts**, and the reset goes with the Bid: the Clock falls back to **two hours remaining**. A re-joins at **20:01:31**. **That Bid resets nothing.** It is six seconds after A's retraction, which is what the rule measures, even though it is **ninety-one seconds** after the Bid that was retracted. Read the other way the Bid would earn a full 48 hours, and the stall example 54 closes would reopen underneath it: a Team retracts at `T+89s`, re-bids at `T+91s`, and collects a fresh reset every ninety-one seconds for nothing, because each re-bid is outside the *previous Bid's* window while being inside its own. **Example 54 cannot catch this** — it re-joins ten seconds after retracting, so both readings suppress and both pass. This example exists only to separate them, which is why its clock arithmetic is otherwise identical to 54's and its timestamps are not.
56. **The window that stops with every other clock.** Team B has led Player X at $4,500,000 since Monday. At **14:00:00** Team A bids $5,000,000; B is released and notified, the Auction Clock resets to 24 hours, and A's window runs to 14:01:30. At **14:00:30** the Commissioner **pauses** the auction (FR-34) — A's window holds **sixty seconds**. At **14:00:45** A tries to retract and is **refused**: a retraction is a write, and every write is refused while paused, exactly as a Bid is. The pause lasts just under three and a half hours. On **resume at 17:30:00** A's window holds sixty seconds still, so a retraction at **17:30:30** is **accepted** — B is restored at $4,500,000, and the Auction Clock returns to the remaining duration B's Bid had set, not to 24 hours from A's. A retraction at **17:31:05** would be refused: sixty seconds is all the window had, and resuming does not round it up. **A naive implementation refuses the 17:30:30 retraction**, because it subtracts 14:00:00 from 17:30:00 and reads four and a half hours — taking a remedy away from a Manager who was locked out of using it for all but the first thirty seconds, for a reason wholly outside their control. The window is a fold over the log like every other clock here, and a pause is in the log.
57. **Exactly ninety seconds, both halves.** Two boundaries, one convention. **(a)** Team A bids on Player X at **14:00:00.000**. A retraction arriving at **14:01:29.999** is **accepted**; one arriving at **14:01:30.000** is **refused** as outside the window. *Within* ninety seconds means fewer than ninety, and the instant the window ends belongs to the refusal — the same way an Auction at its close instant is closed rather than closing (FR-21). **(b)** The identical rule governs the League Clock suppression, read the other way round. A retracts at **14:00:40.000**; an otherwise valid Bid of A's at **14:02:09.999** earns **no** reset, and one at **14:02:10.000** — exactly ninety seconds later — **does**. The server's clock decides both comparisons, never the client's (FR-15, FR-16). **One convention, two rules, no second definition to drift**: an implementation that made the window inclusive and the suppression exclusive would be self-consistent on every other example in this section and wrong on this one.

58. **A Close reversed, and what it leaves alone.** *(Added 2026-09-23, FR-32.)* Team R holds 11 Active/Bench Contracts and one Contract in an Injury Reserve Slot the league rules say it may not hold during free agency. Team R wins Player X at $4,000,000 into Active/Bench, reaching Roster Count 12. That Close cancels Team R's leading Bid on Player Y under FR-40 and restores Team S there. The Commissioner reverses the Close → Team R's Roster Count is **11**, its Cap Space is **$4,000,000 higher**, Player X is **nominatable** by any Team, Team S **still leads** Player Y, and the League Clock's expiry is **unchanged**. The Commissioner then Roster Moves the IR Contract to Active/Bench, and Team R is at **12** — the position it should have held all along. - **§3 Glossary, FR-14, FR-18, FR-21, FR-35, FR-37 vs the league — a Free Agent could be won straight into the minors. REVERSED 2026-09-18, and it is the largest correction this document has taken.** *(Found by the league, not by a reviewer here. The correction **shipped first**, deliberately — commit `7bb7a26` on `pilot` — because the old gates were permitting bids mid-auction that should have been refused, and a wrong gate in a live auction costs a Player.   **The league's rule, stated:** *"You cannot win a Free Agent straight into your minors. You have to be able to fit them on your active roster first, and then you can move them into your minors to reopen the space."*
  **What the PRD said instead.** From 2026-08-16 it held that **Slot Placement** sent a Minor League Eligible winner to a free Minor League Slot at a **$0 Cap Hit**, and six requirements were sized for it: an eligible lead committed nothing (FR-14), projected no roster addition (FR-37), read as **unbounded** (FR-12, FR-35), let a Team at Roster Count 12 keep bidding (FR-37), and let a Team with no Active/Bench room enter lotteries on the strength of a Free Minor League Slot (FR-18).

- **§4.4 / FR-13 — Committed Bids must name the Auctions holding the money, and the requirement is FR-13's rather than FR-14's.** This is a **restoration** of a capability the correction removed. FR-35 required an Overflow refusal to name the specific earlier Auction creating the exposure, and `exposingBids` carried exactly that set; when the overflow arithmetic went, the naming went with it and a refusal became a bare pair of numbers — *"$1.5M exceeds your Maximum Bid of −$28.0M"*, with no way for a Manager to see which of their own leads spent it. Nothing was lost but the requirement to print it: the Team's `leading` list still holds every Player name and amount. **It is filed at FR-13, which governs what a refusal says, rather than at FR-14, which defines what the figure is.** The alternative reading — make itemisation a property of Committed Bids itself — would propagate it to FR-12's display surfaces as well, which is a larger change nobody asked for; the capability that was lost was a *refusal's*, and it is at the refusal that a Manager needs it. **No confirmation needed**; flagged because it is the one thing this amendment **adds** rather than removes.
- **§4.10 / FR-41 — a Roster Trade keeps automatic Slot Placement, and the rule is now stated in FR-41 rather than borrowed from FR-21.** A Contract arriving by Trade is still re-placed against the receiving Team's occupancy — a Minor League Eligible Player takes a Free Minor League Slot if one exists, an Active/Bench Slot otherwise — and `slotPlacementFor` survives in the code serving this act alone. **The two acts genuinely differ**, which is why the correction does not reach here: a won Player is being **acquired**, and the league's rule is that a Team must be able to fit an acquisition on its active roster before it can have him; a traded Contract is **already settled** — bought, priced, rostered — and is being re-placed rather than won onto a roster. The cross-reference to FR-21 was the only thing that had to change, because FR-21 stopped saying what FR-41 was citing it for. **No confirmation needed**; flagged because a reader who knows the new rule will expect it to apply here and must be told why it does not.
- **§10 — the retired examples are kept in place with their numbers spent.** Nine of them (16–22, 25, 35) plus the earlier 41 now sit in §10 as records rather than as cases. The alternative — delete them and renumber — was not taken, for the reason the numbering has always been kept stable: every retired example is cited from the §0 callouts, the §12 index, the architecture spine and its own test file, and a renumber silently rewrites the meaning of every one of those citations. The cost is that §10 is now a third longer than the set of examples it actually asserts. **No confirmation needed**; flagged because the section reads oddly on first encounter and the oddity is deliberate.
- **The code led this amendment, and that is a fact about this entry rather than a policy.** Commit `7bb7a26` shipped before a word of this was written, because the gates in production were permitting bids the league's rule forbids and a wrong gate in a live auction decides who gets a Player. The PRD has led four of its ten passes and followed six; what matters is that the document says so each time, so a reader can tell a specification from a record. **This one is a record.**

*Added 2026-09-23 with the Close Reversal (eleventh pass). Four entries, each confirmed by the Commissioner on approval of `sprint-change-proposal-2026-09-23.md`:*

- **§4.9 / FR-32 — a reversal leaves the League Clock alone.** A reversal is a termination, not a void: the Bids were accepted under the rules the app enforced, and removing their resets now could land the Phase's expiry in the past for the whole league.
