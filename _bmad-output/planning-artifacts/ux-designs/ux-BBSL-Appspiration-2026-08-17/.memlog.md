- (decision) Working mode: coaching path -- walk decisions one at a time, creative tools (colour themes, design directions, Excalidraw wireframes) woven in.
- (decision) Inherited from SPEC (binding, not up for redesign): mobile-first at 375px; WCAG 2.1 AA contrast; touch targets >=44x44px on every bidding and nomination control; Auction state never conveyed by colour alone.
- (decision) Inherited from SPEC: NO URGENCY DESIGN. Countdown pressure, one-tap raises without confirmation, and suggested bid amounts are rejected outright. Posture is referee, not croupier. This is the single strongest shaping force on the UX.
- (decision) Inherited from SPEC: auditability over convenience is the tiebreaker when two designs are otherwise equal. Refusals must show their arithmetic (Cap Space, Committed Bids, Minors Exposure, Roster Reserve, resulting Maximum Bid). Money refusal (CAP-4/6) and slot refusal (CAP-19) must read as visibly distinct grounds.
- (decision) Inherited from SPEC: unbounded Maximum Bid renders as words ('no cap limit'), never as a number. Bid control pre-fills minimum legal Bid and never offers an amount above viewer's Maximum Bid. Bid history carries no anonymity at any point -- Team and acting Manager always shown.
- (decision) Inherited from SPEC -- perceived-performance floors the UX must not violate: board/Auction interactive <2s on mobile data, Bid acknowledged <1s, Maximum Bid recomputed <1s, board change reflected <5s. Countdowns derive from server-authoritative absolute close time, shown alongside viewer-local absolute time.
- (decision) BRAND CONCEPT (user, verbatim intent): the visual identity is an eco-friendly banking startup -- deliberately evoking Aspiration Inc, the sustainable-banking company at the centre of the alleged Clippers/Kawhi Leonard cap-circumvention scheme. The product name 'Appspiration' is that pun. The irony is load-bearing: the app dresses as the most famous alleged cap-circumvention vehicle in recent memory while being structurally incapable of permitting one.
- (decision) AESTHETIC: clean and functional, but unafraid of information density where convenient -- the audience is fantasy managers fluent in dense stat tables and acronyms. Density is a feature, not a compromise. Explicitly requested: player real-life info (NBA team, position, existing contract salary and length) shown under the player name on the Bid Board.
- (decision) Dark mode is the default theme.
- (decision) Stakes: internal (31 known users, no public surface, no regulator) with an elevated reputational edge -- the builder is simultaneously the Commissioner and a competing manager, which is why the SPEC makes auditability structural.
- (decision) Money renders ABBREVIATED EVERYWHERE ($14.5M), including the Auction view, refusal arithmetic and Audit Log -- not only on the board. User chose this over a board-abbreviated/detail-full hybrid. Density wins consistently; one money format across the whole app.
- (decision) GREEN IS BRAND ONLY. The eco-fintech green owns identity, chrome and surface. It never signals Auction state. Auction states (Awaiting Opening Bid / Standard Contention / Minimum-Bid Contention / leading / outbid / closed) are carried by label, shape and position with a non-brand accent -- which also satisfies the SPEC rule that state is never conveyed by colour alone, and dodges the green/red colourblind collapse across 31 managers.
- (decision) DARK MODE ONLY. No light theme, now or later -- committed fully rather than dual-ready. Single token set, single AA contrast audit, palette free to be opinionated. Fits a one-person zero-budget build.
- (decision) VOICE (derived from the Aspiration brand pun, to be confirmed at Finalize): sincere, quietly institutional, faintly overpolished -- the register of a bank that wants you to know about its tree-planting programme. A refusal declines you the way a bank declines a transfer: unbothered, precise, receipts attached. Never apologetic, never alarmed, never exclamatory.
- (decision) MONEY RENDERING RESOLVED, LOSSLESS. Existing Contract salaries from Fantrax sit on the same $500,000 grid as Bids. Therefore every money value in the app -- bids, existing salaries, and all figures derived from them (Available Cap Space, Committed Bids, Minors Exposure, Roster Reserve, Maximum Bid) -- is a whole multiple of $500,000 and is exactly representable at one decimal place. Abbreviated-everywhere ($14.5M) is a faithful rendering, not a rounding, and the refusal arithmetic column sums correctly as displayed. Format: always exactly one decimal place, never dropped ($12.0M, not $12M), so the money column aligns on the decimal in tabular numerals.
- (decision) FORM FACTOR: single responsive web app, not split surfaces. Manager surfaces (board, Auction, Team, Audit Log) designed at 375px first and must work one-handed. Commissioner surfaces (import + 30-team preview, Minor League Eligibility setting, override console, pause/resume, contract-assignment monitoring, export) get layouts that expand properly on desktop but stay fully operable on a phone -- because a pause during an outage happens wherever the Commissioner physically is, and the SPEC requires the pause to be reachable.

- (decision) Worked example fixed across the study, and it sums: Salary Cap $165.0M - Existing Contracts $142.0M - Committed Bids $6.0M - Minors Exposure $1.0M = Available Cap Space $16.0M; less Roster Reserve $2.0M = Maximum Bid $14.0M. Roster Count 9 of 12. Reuse these figures in EXPERIENCE.md so every example in the spec is one coherent scenario rather than unrelated numbers.
- (decision) Amber is the single attention colour (alongside brand green). Not red -- red reads as alarm and the SPEC forbids urgency design; amber is the register of a bank declining a transfer. Every state additionally carries a word and an icon shape, so colour is never load-bearing.
- (decision) REFUSAL SCREEN PATTERN (drafted, pending user confirmation): headline 'This bid was not placed.'; one-sentence delta naming the excess; explicit reassurance that nothing was committed and the Auction is unchanged; BOTH gates reported side by side -- Cap refused / Slots passed with the 10-of-12 figure -- so the two independent grounds of CAP-4 and CAP-19 are visibly distinct; the full arithmetic timestamped 'your figures at <time>'; and a DISABLED bid control stating the reason (next legal bid $15.0M vs Maximum Bid $14.0M) per the SPEC requirement that controls disable with a stated reason rather than fail at submission.
- (decision) VISUAL DIRECTION SETTLED -- a deliberate remix, not one of the three whole: A's fonts, B's density and card style, C's deep green. Recorded as House.dc.html on the canvas, first artboard. The three source directions are kept on the canvas so the reasoning stays legible.
- (decision) TYPE PAIRING (from Direction A): Georgia (fallback Palatino Linotype, then serif) for Player names, the wordmark, and the two headline labels -- 'Maximum Bid' and the refusal headline. system-ui / Segoe UI for every label, number, control and body string, always with font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so money columns align on the decimal. Two families only. Explicitly NOT monospace -- Direction B's uppercase mono was fatiguing in prose and fought the banking voice.
- (decision) SURFACE AND DENSITY (from Direction B): flat bordered panels -- 1px border, 3px radius, 12px padding, 7px internal row gap, 8px between cards. No drop shadows, no elevation, no large radii. Direction C's 14px radii, pill chips and card shadows were rejected as costing vertical room the 375px board cannot spare.
- (decision) PALETTE (ground and brand from Direction C): ground #0D1712 (deep green-black), surface #15211B, border #24332A, strong rule #46584D. Text #E8F0E9 primary / #C3D0C6 body prose / #93A69A secondary / #67796E tertiary. Brand mint #6FD3A0 -- used only on the wordmark mark, links, and the 'Maximum Bid' label. Attention amber #E8B366. Lottery periwinkle #8B98E0, carried as a 3px left border plus label and icon. Every colour needs an AA contrast pass against #15211B at Finalize.
- (decision) BOARD CARD STATES: three distinct treatments drafted -- Open (neutral label, money large, countdown + absolute local close time), Minimum-Bid Contention/Lottery (periwinkle left border + icon + label, contender count, and the words 'The clock does not reset on a join'), and Awaiting Opening Bid (no clock at all, money slot reads 'No opening bid' in tertiary text, and states that it holds the nominator's Nomination Slot with hours-unbid). Awaiting Bid was added specifically because it is the state with no clock, and it must look different without looking broken.
- (decision) REFUSAL REPORTS BOTH GATES, ALWAYS. 'Cap -- Refused' and 'Slots -- Passed, Roster Count would be 10 of 12' appear together on every refusal, neither collapsed behind a disclosure. Goes beyond the SPEC, which only requires the two grounds be distinguishable. Rationale: it proves every check ran and this is the only obstacle, which forecloses 'what else is it not telling me' -- and auditability over convenience is the SPEC's stated tiebreaker. Costs one line per refusal, accepted.
- (decision) AWAITING OPENING BID card keeps the sting: the public board shows the nominating Team and hours-unbid, not just a neutral 'awaiting opening bid'. A Nomination Slot sitting dead is visible to the whole league rather than only to its owner. Consistent with CAP-3 ('flagged on the board and to its owner at 24 hours') and with the SPEC assumption that the app surfaces the held-slot consequence rather than mitigating it.
- (decision) NAVIGATION: redundant, two routes to one set of destinations. (1) The persistent Maximum Bid strip at the bottom of the thumb zone doubles as the primary navigation -- it shows Maximum Bid and Roster Count at all times per CAP-10, and tapping it opens a destination sheet. (2) An expandable nav menu in the header offers the same destinations. Deliberate redundancy: the thumb strip is the mobile primary, the header menu is what scales to the desktop layout where a bottom strip reads wrong, and it gives discoverability the sheet alone would not. BINDING RULE: both affordances render from ONE destination list, filtered by phase and role -- they can never offer different sets, and neither may show a destination that is not live in the current phase.
- (decision) COMMISSIONER TOOLING IS IN PLACE, not walled off. Override controls appear on the object being acted on -- the Auction, the Team, the Bid, the Nomination Slot -- visible only to the Commissioner, so an override is performed with the full context on screen and the SPEC-required reason is written against something visible. Genuinely global administrative acts (import + 30-team preview, Minor League Eligibility, auction-open gate, pause/resume, assignment monitoring, export and archive, operational health) get their own admin destination. Note for EXPERIENCE.md: because the Commissioner is also a competing manager, every in-place Commissioner control needs a visual treatment that is unmistakably distinct from ordinary Manager controls -- the act must never be reachable by muscle memory while playing.
- (decision) PHASE-AWARE IA. The app changes shape across four phases -- Setup, Auction, Contract Assignment, Archived. Destinations are filtered by phase: the Bid Board does not exist before open and is frozen-but-readable once archived; Contract Assignment does not exist until the League Clock expires; the Audit Log and Team views persist across every phase. A Manager must never be offered a destination that is not live.
- (decision) TEAM IDENTITY: every Team in the BBSL is named after a real NBA franchise -- managers are 'Los Angeles Lakers' / 'LAL'. Consequence for the board card: the player's real-life NBA team and the bidding fantasy Team occupy the same card in the same three-letter form, and can literally be the same string. This is a legibility defect in the drafted card and must be resolved by a naming rule, not by styling alone.
- (change) IMPORT IS 31 CSV FILES, NOT TWO. One free-agent pool export plus one export per Team, thirty of them. The SPEC's CAP-1 wording -- 'the auction refuses to open unless BOTH imports are committed' -- assumes a two-file import and does not survive contact with the real Fantrax workflow. Flagged to the user; needs reconciling in SPEC/ARCHITECTURE, and it materially changes the import surface (batch upload, per-file parse status, missing-Team detection, single-Team re-upload before open).
- (decision) AUCTION OPENS Sept 14, 12:00pm PST. The league is California-centred with members across the US plus contingents in the EU and Australia. Timezone spread is structural, not incidental: the auction runs as a relay of timezone clusters -- flurries when a region wakes, long quiet stretches between. This is why absolute close time in viewer-local alongside the countdown (CAP-7/CAP-10) is load-bearing rather than a nicety, and why the 48-hour League Clock is a plausible end condition rather than a formality.
- (decision) THE DOMINANT SESSION IS THE WAKE-UP, and it has a fixed shape the UX must serve in order: Discord notification arrives first (the app is entered FROM Discord, not opened cold) -> check my active bids: did I win, or was I outbid while asleep -> if outbid, check Available Cap Space to see whether I can continue -> scan the board for auctions on comparable players, both as alternatives and to price a bid that makes those alternatives more tempting to rivals -> if a Nomination Slot is free, nominate a comparable Free Agent as a decoy to pull bidding away from the preferred target. Every step of that chain must be reachable without hunting.
- (decision) OPENING NIGHT SHAPE: at 12:00pm PST the Bid Board is EMPTY and every awake manager is on the nomination surface hunting for a first target. Early auctions open low and get pushed up toward market value, so the opening hours carry a disproportionate share of $1.0M openings and therefore of Minimum-Bid Contention lotteries. The board's empty state at auction open is a real designed screen, not an edge case, and it should point at nomination.
- (decision) COMPARISON SHOPPING IS A REAL NEED WITH A HARD LIMIT. Managers compare candidates positionally and by production. The app may therefore filter and sort the board and the Free Agent pool by position and by the imported real-life contract figures -- but the SPEC's 'Not a valuation engine' non-goal forbids projections, rankings, ADP, similarity scoring or any suggested bid. The line: the app arranges what the league already knows; it never offers an opinion about who is comparable to whom.
- (decision) NAMING RULE, APP-WIDE AND ABSOLUTE: a three-letter capitalised abbreviation ALWAYS means a player's real-life NBA team and never anything else. A fantasy Team is always rendered spelled out as its nickname plus an em dash plus the acting Manager -- 'Lakers -- Meakel'. The rule holds even when both are the same franchise, which is precisely the case it exists for. Applies to the Bid Board, Auction detail, bid history, Audit Log, Team views, Discord posts and both exports.
- (decision) LANDING SURFACE: opening the app mid-auction lands on Your Positions -- what you lead, what you have been outbid on, and the Minimum-Bid Contentions you are a Contender in -- with the full Bid Board one tap away. Not a filter on the board and not a pinned group: a destination of its own, because the dominant session is the wake-up and nobody opens this app to browse. CAP-10's sortable/filterable full board remains, one tap away and unfiltered by default so a filtered view can never be mistaken for the whole board.
- (decision) IMPORT SURFACE: one drop zone taking all thirty-one files at once. A thirty-one row status list resolves each file to a Team by name, flags unmatched or malformed files naming the offending row per CAP-1, and names any Team still missing. A single file can be re-dropped to replace one row without redoing the others. The SPEC's per-Team preview of Roster Count and Cap Space still gates the commit, and re-import stays permitted before open and refused after.

- (decision) CONNECTION AND FRESHNESS (closes the rubric's only critical finding). Three states, always exactly one: Live (nothing said -- announcing 'live' constantly is noise), Reconnecting (strip states figures may be stale and gives the age of the last update), Stale (Bid and Nomination controls disable with the reason stated; Maximum Bid renders as a last-known figure explicitly labelled as such). Money in a non-Live state is never displayed as though current -- either it carries its age or the control it would authorise is disabled. Countdowns never freeze on disconnect because they derive from an absolute close time the client already holds. Rationale: a stale board that still looks live is the worst failure mode in this product -- the SPEC settles it server-side but the guarantee has to be visible on the client, not merely true underneath it.
- (decision) SIGN-IN STATES: signed out (a single Discord action, no email field anywhere), not registered (states plainly that the Commissioner must add the account, discloses nothing further, offers no retry loop), session expired (states it expired rather than presenting as a fresh sign-out, returns to the surface the manager was on), and the Commissioner fallback path independent of Discord -- unadvertised on the Manager screen but reachable, because a Commissioner locked out during a Discord outage cannot pause the auction and a reachable pause is the SPEC's core outage mitigation. Every sign-in surface renders the current League phase.

- (decision) THE OBLIGATION IS DISCHARGED BEFORE THE EVENT, NOT AFTER IT. FR-40 is the only thing in this product the app takes away from a manager who did nothing wrong, and the instinct is to write a beautiful apology notice. Rejected. The bid control states the trade before the manager confirms -- 'This is your 2nd of 2 permitted bids. If you win a player before this auction closes, this bid is cancelled and the next highest bid leads' -- once, at the moment of the decision, not repeated on every later view. New Key Flow 6 puts its climax at that step deliberately: a product that explains the rule only after it fires has already failed, and by the time the notice arrives the only remaining question is whether the manager was ambushed.
- (decision) THREE PARTIES, THREE NOTICES, ONE EVENT (new State Pattern 'Cancelled and restored'). The cancelled manager gets cause-before-effect, because the effect alone is indistinguishable from a defect -- no apology (concedes error), no alarm styling (says it louder), no congratulation wrapped around it (pairing a win with a confiscation cheerfully is worse than either alone). The RESTORED manager stopped watching days ago and needs context re-established first, then the two things they must act on: what it now costs their cap, and how long they have -- the clock did not reset, so it may be minutes. A restored manager discovering at expiry that they won a player they forgot bidding on is the failure this notice prevents. The league gets one line in the existing register.
- (decision) SLOTS GATE FIGURE CARRIES TWO NUMBERS AND THREE DISTINCT REFUSALS. Roster count alone stopped explaining the gate: a manager at 'Roster 10 of 12' may be permitted two more bids or none. Passed reads 'your 2nd of 2 permitted bids; Roster Count would be 10 of 12'; refused on the allowance reads 'this would be your 3rd outstanding bid; 1 free slot permits 2'; refused on the precondition reads 'no free Active/Bench slot, so no bid is permitted'. The last two must not collapse into one string -- 'at your allowance' resolves itself at the next close, 'no room at all' lasts the whole auction, and a manager who cannot tell them apart either waits for a close that fixes nothing or abandons a wait that would have worked. DESIGN.md gate row now wraps rather than truncates; a truncated gate sentence is a gate that did not report.
