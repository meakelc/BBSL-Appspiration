# BBSL Offseason Free Agent Auction

**Vocabulary is fixed.** Every capitalised domain term below — Salary Cap, Cap Hit, Committed Bids, Available Cap Space, Free Minor League Slots, Free Active/Bench Slots, Eligible Leading Bids, Overflow Count, Minors Exposure, Roster Count, Roster Reserve, Roster Capacity, Maximum Bid, League Median, Slot Placement, Minor League Eligible, Nomination Slot, Minimum-Bid Contention, Contender, Randomizer, Auction Clock, League Clock, Year Allotment — is defined in PRD §3 and is used here with exactly that meaning. Identifier names in code are these terms verbatim. A synonym is a defect.

**Four numbers to internalise before reading further,** each easy to carry a stale copy of: a Bid is a whole multiple of **$500,000** (not $100,000, changed 2026-08-17); a Team has exactly **12** Active/Bench Slots — a ceiling, not just a floor (2026-08-17); a Team may hold **one outstanding Bid beyond its free Slots** (2026-09-08), which is a bidding permission and never a thirteenth Slot — the surplus is cancelled at Close by CAP-21; and a Bid may be taken back by the Team that placed it for **ninety seconds** (2026-09-16, CAP-24). **The fourth reverses a rule this contract asserted from its first draft until 2026-09-16** — that no Bid could ever be withdrawn by its own Team — so a stale copy of it is not a wrong number but an inverted rule.

## Why

A vision to realize, forced by a pain. The BBSL is a 30-team dynasty fantasy basketball league entering its sixth season, and for five offseasons it has run its free agent auction by hand. The rules are good — open ascending bidding on a rolling 24-hour clock, nomination slots that pace the market, a $1M minimum-salary lottery, a contract-length allotment that forces real dynasty tradeoffs — and running them manually is a part-time job for one person. Someone has to notice a clock expired, check whether a team can afford the bid it just posted, remember that a team already spent its 4-year deal. When that person gets it wrong at 1am, thirty people find out in the morning. This app runs the auction instead: it knows every Team's Maximum Bid to the dollar, refuses illegal bids at submission rather than reversing them afterward, runs its own clocks, draws the lottery with a seed anyone can verify, ends itself on 48 hours of league silence, and hands the Commissioner a file to upload back into Fantrax. The point is that the Commissioner gets to play in their own league again, and that when a team loses a player by $500k at 4am, nobody has to wonder whether the app got it right. It is built and operated by one person who is also the Commissioner and a competing manager — which is why auditability is structural here rather than decorative.

## Capabilities

- **CAP-1 — Import the league and open the auction**
  - **intent:** Commissioner can load all 30 Teams' Existing Contracts, Roster Slots and the Free Agent pool from Fantrax CSV exports — **thirty-one files: one Free Agent pool export plus one roster export per Team** — and transition the League into the Auction Phase.
  - **success:** All thirty-one files are supplied in a single batch and each is resolved independently: a Team file that matches none of the 30 Teams, or a Team already supplied, is refused naming the file. A malformed or contradictory file is refused naming the offending row — including a starting state that breaches a slot ceiling (more than 12 Active/Bench, 2 Injury Reserve, or 3 Minor League players). Parse status is reported per file and every Team still outstanding is **named, not counted**. A single Team's file may be re-supplied to replace that Team's rows without re-supplying the other twenty-nine. A per-Team preview of Roster Count and Cap Space across all 30 Teams is confirmed before commit, and commit is all-or-nothing — a partially imported League is never a reachable state. The auction refuses to open unless the pool import and all thirty Team imports are committed and every Team has a bound Manager. Re-import is permitted before open and refused after. Realizes FR-1 – FR-3.

- **CAP-18 — Set Minor League Eligibility by hand**
  - **intent:** Commissioner can mark which pooled Players are Minor League Eligible, because the Fantrax export does not carry the flag.
  - **success:** Every imported Player defaults to **not** eligible, so an unset flag can never grant CAP-6's unbounded bidding by omission; the flag is settable individually and in bulk until the auction opens, locked afterward except by override, and every change is recorded as an event with actor, Player and before/after values — so a state rebuild reproduces the flag as it stood rather than as it is now. The setting screen states the consequence in words ("this Player can be stashed at a $0 Cap Hit") rather than showing a bare checkbox. Realizes FR-38.

- **CAP-2 — Authenticate Managers and bind them to Teams**
  - **intent:** A Manager can reach their Team's auction view through Discord OAuth without a password, acting on behalf of exactly one Team, with the Commissioner holding administrative rights on top of ordinary Manager rights over their own Team.
  - **success:** A Discord account the Commissioner has not pre-registered cannot obtain a session, and is refused without enumerating the league — the refusal states only that the Commissioner must add the account, never whether that identity or any Team exists, and offers no retry loop to probe with. A session that expired reads as expired, not as a fresh sign-out. Sessions persist ≥30 days; every Nomination and Bid records the acting Manager alongside the Team and both surface in public displays; Commissioner-only capabilities are unreachable by a non-Commissioner by UI and by direct request alike; the Commissioner's own Team is subject to every ordinary rule; a Commissioner sign-in path exists that does not depend on Discord. No email is sent by this system for any purpose. Realizes FR-4 – FR-6.

- **CAP-3 — Nominate Free Agents onto the Bid Board**
  - **intent:** A Manager can put any Free Agent on the Bid Board while their Team's Nomination Slot is unused, without obligation to bid on him.
  - **success:** The Player appears in Awaiting Opening Bid with no Auction Clock; the Slot is marked used and released only at that Player's Auction Close, regardless of who won or whether the nominator ever bid; a Team may nominate a Player it could not afford; two concurrent Nominations of the same Player produce exactly one; an unbid Nomination is flagged on the board and to its owner at 24 hours and never auto-expires. Realizes FR-7 – FR-10.

- **CAP-4 — Compute and display Maximum Bid per Auction**
  - **intent:** A Manager can see, on the board and on every Auction, the largest legal Bid their Team may place on that specific Auction right now, with the arithmetic broken out rather than as a bare number.
  - **success:** For a non-eligible Player, `Maximum Bid = Available Cap Space − Roster Reserve`; for a Minor League Eligible Player the Team's Free Minor League Slots can absorb, the figure reads as unbounded in words rather than as a number; the components — Cap Space, Committed Bids, Minors Exposure, Roster Reserve — are each shown; the figure recomputes for all affected Teams and Auctions within one second of any Bid, Auction Close, or override; controls disable with a stated reason where the figure is below the minimum legal Bid. Maximum Bid governs **money only** — CAP-19 is a separate gate an unbounded figure does not exempt a Bid from. Realizes FR-12, FR-25.

- **CAP-5 — Place a Bid, or be refused with the arithmetic**
  - **intent:** A Manager can bid on any Auction their Team is not leading, and an illegal Bid is refused at submission rather than accepted and reversed.
  - **success:** In Standard Contention a Bid is valid only at ≥ current high + $500,000 and as a whole multiple of **$500,000** — the two coincide, so no grid-valid Bid can be sub-increment and the granularity check earns its keep in Minimum-Bid Contention, where the increment rule does not apply; a Team cannot bid against itself; a Bid over Maximum Bid never appears on the Bid Board or in the Audit Log as valid, and its refusal shows Cap Space, Committed Bids, Minors Exposure, Roster Reserve and the resulting Maximum Bid, **and reports both gates — the one that refused and the one that passed, with its figure**; validation runs server-side against committed state at submission, so a Bid valid when composed but stale on arrival is refused with current figures. **No control to EDIT or LOWER an accepted Bid exists**, and that is unchanged — retract-and-rebid is two separately recorded acts and nothing amends a Bid in place. **THREE things can end a Bid's leadership other than being outbid**: a Commissioner void (CAP-14), a system cancellation (CAP-21), and **the Team's own retraction inside ninety seconds (CAP-24)** — the last being the only one the bidding Team itself invokes. *(Amended 2026-09-16. This capability previously stated that a Bid once accepted could not be withdrawn **by the Team that placed it**, and that "two things outside the Team" could end it. The emphasis was added 2026-09-08 to keep the Team locked out while CAP-21 let the system act; FR-15 reversed it. The count is now three, and the framing failed as well as the number, because the third is the Team itself.)* Realizes FR-11, FR-13.

- **CAP-19 — Enforce Roster Capacity**
  - **intent:** A Team has exactly 12 Active/Bench Slots — 8 active plus 4 bench — and cannot commit itself to more players than it has Slots for.
  - **success:** A Bid passes when `Projected Active/Bench Additions = 0`, **or** when the Team holds at least one Free Active/Bench Slot and `Projected Active/Bench Additions ≤ Free Active/Bench Slots + 1` — computed on the same post-bid basis as Roster Reserve. The `+ 1` is the **Outstanding Bid Allowance**: a Manager may chase one more Player than they have room for rather than idling a day for a close, and the surplus is taken back by CAP-21 rather than permitted to land. It is **not a thirteenth Slot** — a Team reaches Roster Count 12 and never 13. The **free-slot precondition is load-bearing**: a Team at Roster Count 12 holds no Active/Bench-bound commitment at all, because a Bid it could win would breach the ceiling with no other close available to cancel it first. This is a **second refusal ground independent of Maximum Bid** — a Team can fail it with unlimited cap space and pass it with none, neither check subsumes the other, and both run on every Bid; the two refusals carry distinct machine-readable reasons and distinct arithmetic, and reporting a capacity refusal as a cap refusal is a defect. A Team at Roster Count 12 can still bid on a Minor League Eligible Player a Free Minor League Slot would absorb, on the `Projected = 0` branch. An eligible Bid creating Active/Bench Overflow with nowhere to land is refused, naming the earlier Auction causing it — there is **no carve-out** for automatic Slot Placement. Minimum-Bid Contention entries are exempt from this capability entirely (CAP-8). The refusal states Roster Count, Projected Additions, Free Active/Bench Slots **and the allowance**, and distinguishes *"at your allowance"* — which resolves itself at the next close — from *"no free Slot at all"*, which lasts the whole auction; both are shown on the board rather than discovered at submission. Roster Reserve's `max(0, …)` clamp is now reachable in ordinary play. PRD §10 examples 23, 24, 25, 29, 30 pass. Realizes FR-37.

- **CAP-6 — Commit and release capital, including Minors Exposure**
  - **intent:** The system holds a Team's leading money against its cap for the life of an Auction, and treats a Minor League Eligible Player its Free Minor League Slots can absorb as committing nothing.
  - **success:** Committed Bids include leading amounts on non-eligible Auctions, $1,000,000 per Contender position in a Minimum-Bid Contention on a non-eligible Player, and Minors Exposure — the sum of the Overflow Count largest Eligible Leading Bids, zero when `N ≤ M`. Capital releases the instant a Team stops leading **for any reason — outbid, cancelled under CAP-21, or retracted by the Team itself under CAP-24** — or loses a draw, and is **re-committed for a Restored Leading Bidder** at the amount of the Bid restored. A later cheap eligible Bid can be refused because it exposes an earlier expensive one, and the refusal names the Auction causing the exposure; no **arithmetic** ever reaches back and invalidates an accepted Bid. CAP-21's cancellation is not an exception: it does not find the Bid invalid, it stands a valid Bid down because a Slot it needed has since been filled. PRD §10 examples 16–22 pass. Realizes FR-14, FR-35.

- **CAP-7 — Run the Auction Clock and close Auctions**
  - **intent:** Each open Auction runs a 24-hour clock that closes it and awards the Player with automatic Slot Placement, with no user present.
  - **success:** Every valid Bid in Standard Contention sets the Clock to exactly 24 hours from that Bid; the Clock never pauses for hours-of-day; absolute close time is displayed in the viewer's local timezone alongside the countdown; close fires within 60 seconds of nominal expiry and survives a process restart; a Minor League Eligible Player takes a free Minor League Slot at a $0 Cap Hit or an Active/Bench Slot at the winning amount otherwise; Roster Count increases only on an Active/Bench placement; the nominator's Slot releases; Auction Close does not reset the League Clock. The Close then evaluates the winning Team’s remaining commitments and applies CAP-21, **inside** the sequential ordering — committed to the state the next Close is evaluated against, which is what stops two Auctions placing Players into the same last Slot. 

- **CAP-21 — Cancel a surplus commitment and restore the Auction**
  - **intent:** When an Auction Close leaves a Team holding commitments with no Roster Slot to receive them, the system stands those commitments down and returns each affected Auction to its next-highest bidder — without a Commissioner acting, and without the cancelled Team having done anything wrong.
  - **success:** Cancellation is triggered **only** by a Close that reduces the winning Team's free Slots, **Active/Bench or Minor League** — a trigger reading only "Roster Count increased" misses a Team whose last Minor League Slot has just filled, which can then win two more eligible lotteries and finish at Roster Count 14. Commitments are cancelled **one at a time, most recent first by log sequence**, re-testing CAP-19 and CAP-8 after each and stopping the moment the Team is within both; an eligible Bid a Free Minor League Slot can still absorb is untouched. Each cancellation appends a compensating event that **never deletes or mutates the original Bid** — it stays a visible history line, struck through and labelled, with the causing win named — releases the capital, and notifies the Team with the **cause stated before the effect**. On an Auction in Standard Contention the next-highest surviving Bid becomes leading and that Team's capital is re-committed, after re-validation against the money and capacity gates at that instant; a candidate that fails is **skipped**, never restored-then-cancelled, and the next below is tried. Where no Bid survives, the Auction returns to Awaiting Opening Bid with its **Auction Clock cleared** — an Auction with no Bid has no Clock — holding its nominator's Slot until someone opens it or the Phase ends. Neither Clock is reset and **no prior reset is removed**, which is the opposite of a Commissioner void and the distinction most likely to be built wrong. A restoration never itself triggers a cancellation, which is what bounds the cascade — an argument about re-entrancy inside one Close, which is **not** the argument bounding CAP-24's chain. **The restorer has three callers, not two**: a cancellation (here), a Commissioner void (CAP-14) and a retraction (CAP-24) all hand the Auction to the next-highest surviving Bid by **one** selector under **one** skip rule, differing only on three axes — whether the withdrawn Bid is erased or retained, whether the Auction Clock is restored or left, and whether the League Clock reset is removed or kept. **This capability's trigger is unchanged and must not be widened: cancellation is caused by an Auction Close and by nothing else, and a retraction calls this restorer without being a cancellation and without causing one.** PRD §10 examples 31, 32, 33, 34, 35 pass. Realizes FR-40.

- **CAP-24 — Retract a Bid inside ninety seconds**
  - **intent:** A Team can take back its own most recent Bid, at no cost, within ninety seconds of placing it — while that Bid still stands, and while the Team it displaced is no harder to restore than when it was displaced. The window exists for the **typo**: one irreversible keystroke against a six-figure sum, typed on a phone, where $5,000,000 and $50,000,000 differ by one character.
  - **success:** The window is **ninety seconds from the retracted Bid's own timestamp** and from nothing later, is **derived and never stored**, and is **half-open** — open below ninety seconds, closed at exactly `90.000`, the same posture CAP-7 takes on an Auction at its close instant. Being **restored** to leadership never grants a fresh window, which is what bounds a chain of retractions **with no depth limit anywhere in the rule**: every step down the history is anchored to a strictly older Bid, so a chain shortens by construction. A Team may retract **only its own most recent Bid on an Auction and only while it still stands** — Leading Bid in Standard Contention, still a Contender in a lottery — and a Team since outbid is refused on **that** ground rather than on the window. Retraction is **free**: no fee, no cooldown, and **no floor on what the Team may bid next**, including below what it just retracted, because the Bid Board is open outcry and the figure it would bid against was already public. **The window ends early the moment the displaced Team's restoration figures move in a direction that could make restoring it fail**, tested over exactly four — **Cap Space, Committed Bids, Roster Count, Minor League occupancy** — and the test is **directional**, so a change that can only make restoration *easier* (capital released by being outbid elsewhere, or by losing a draw) does not close it. **The test is stated over those figures and never over a list of acts**; the acts are a reader's checklist only, because that list was found incomplete three times before any code existed. A **Drop** is the case worth naming: it *frees* a Roster Slot and still **lowers** Maximum Bid by $1,000,000 of Roster Reserve, so a Team becomes harder to restore by an act that looks like relief. A retraction takes the **Commissioner void's** treatment on all three axes and not the cancellation's: the Bid is **erased from the Auction's standing** though it remains in the Audit Log as placed and retracted, the **Auction Clock is restored** to the value it displaced — and if that instant is already past the Auction closes at the next sweep, late rather than wrong — and the **League Clock reset it earned is removed**, so the Auction Phase recomputes shorter, prospectively only. The retracting Team's capital is **released** (CAP-6). The Auction goes to the **next-highest surviving Bid by CAP-21's selector**, and **the skip is still evaluated** even though the directional cut-short means the displaced Team is always restorable: the two rules are independent, one is a test a future requirement can silently outgrow, and the failure mode of trusting it is restoring a Team into a breach of its own cap — so a retraction that reaches a Team **other** than the one it displaced is a defect to investigate, not an outcome to accept. In a **Minimum-Bid Contention** a retracted entry leaves the Contender list and releases its $1,000,000 with **no restoration** (a lottery has no next-highest Bid) and **no seed revealed** (a shrinking list is not a dissolution); the **Auction Clock is untouched** while any Contender remains, whoever retracts, the Opening Bid's Team included, because a join never reset it; a list reduced to **one** resolves to that Contender; and a list **emptied** by retraction returns the Auction to Awaiting Opening Bid with its Clock **cleared**, which is deliberately not what a cancellation-emptied list does (CAP-8). **The Bid that DISSOLVES a contention cannot be retracted at all and the control is absent rather than refused** — dissolution releases every Contender's $1,000,000 irreversibly, so a converting Bid is final on submission and a void is the only remedy. A retraction is **refused while paused**, and the window **stops with every other Clock** (CAP-14). Retraction exists **only during the Auction Phase**, and a Phase ending inside an open window ends it. Every retraction is written to the Audit Log and **broadcast to the league**, and the restored Team is told it leads again (CAP-11). PRD §10 examples 47–57 pass. Realizes FR-15.

- **CAP-8 — Run the Minimum-Bid Contention lottery**
  - **intent:** An Auction opened at exactly $1,000,000 becomes a lottery any Team can join at exactly $1,000,000, dissolves back to ordinary ascending rules on a Bid of $1,500,000 or more, and otherwise resolves by an auditable random draw at expiry.
  - **success:** The Clock is fixed from the Opening Bid and does not reset on a join; the state is visually distinct and states in words that the clock will not reset; a Bid strictly between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000 is refused as neither a valid entry nor a valid raise; dissolution discards the Contender list, releases every commitment, and resets the Clock to 24 hours regardless of Contender count; the draw is uniform at `1/n`, resolves a single-Contender lottery to that Contender, and records seed, ordered Contender list as it stood at expiry, and selection permanently on the closed Auction and in the Audit Log — sufficient for any Manager to reproduce the result independently. A Team holding at least one Free Active/Bench Slot — or, for an eligible Player, a Free Minor League Slot — may be a Contender in **any number** of open Contentions at once: entries contribute nothing to Projected Active/Bench Additions, do not consume the Outstanding Bid Allowance, and are limited only by **cap space**, since the expected outcome of a lottery is losing and a capacity rule sized as though a Team would win them all would throttle the mechanism. That is safe only because CAP-21 removes a Team from its remaining lotteries when its Slots fill, **before** those lotteries draw — which AD-11's sequential closing guarantees. The recorded Contender list is therefore the list **after any cancellations (CAP-21) and any retractions (CAP-24)**, and neither a cancelled nor a retracted Contender can be drawn. **An emptied list resolves two ways, and the act that emptied it decides** — emptied by **cancellation** the Clock keeps running and the Contention closes at expiry with no winner, the Player returning to the pool; emptied by **retraction** the Clock is **cleared** and the Auction returns to Awaiting Opening Bid. A cancelled Bid stood and its Clock stands with it; a retracted Bid never counted, so the Clock it started never counted either. **The Opening Bid's Team holds no special status in this** — once others have joined the Clock belongs to the Contention, so a Contention opened by a Team that later retracts and is *then* emptied by a cancellation **keeps its Clock**. A retraction also reveals **no seed**, because a shrinking list is not a dissolution and only a conversion dissolves anything. PRD §10 examples 6–11, 34, 35, 51 pass. Realizes FR-17 – FR-20.

- **CAP-9 — Run the League Clock and end the Auction Phase**
  - **intent:** The auction ends itself when the league goes quiet for 48 hours.
  - **success:** The League Clock starts when the auction opens and is reset by a Nomination and by an accepted Bid (including a lottery join) and by nothing else — not by an Auction Close, draw, dissolution, void, override, or pause/resume; the start is not a reset, and no void recomputes the Clock back past it; on expiry Nomination and bidding are disabled league-wide, Auctions still in Awaiting Opening Bid terminate with no winner and their Players return to the pool, the League moves to Contract Assignment Phase, and all Managers are notified. The Clock is **derived from the surviving reset events, never stored as a countdown**: voiding a Bid removes its reset and the Clock recomputes shorter, prospectively only. **A retraction removes its Bid's reset the same way** — the void's treatment, not the cancellation's, whose reset stands because the Bid was valid when placed. **And a Bid placed by a Team within ninety seconds of that same Team's own retraction earns no reset at all, measured from the RETRACTION rather than from the retracted Bid.** That rule is **not a rate limit and must not be built as one**: it is the only bound on an otherwise free and indefinite extension of the Auction Phase, since a lottery join resets this Clock, this Clock is the sole terminator, and a retraction removes only its own reset — so join-retract-rejoin would renew the one thing that ends the auction at no cost. Anchored to the retracted Bid instead, the stall returns intact. PRD §10 examples 13, 27, 54, 55 pass. Realizes FR-22.

- **CAP-10 — Read the board, an Auction, and any Team**
  - **intent:** Any Manager can see every open Auction with live state, any single Auction's complete history, and any Team's roster and cap position — one-handed on a phone.
  - **success:** Opening the app mid-auction lands on what the viewer leads, has been outbid on, and is a Contender in — the full board is one step away and opens unfiltered, so a filtered view can never be mistaken for the whole board. The board shows identity, price, Leading Bidder, state and time remaining per Auction, is sortable and filterable to what the viewer leads or contends in, updates within 5 seconds of the originating action without a manual refresh, and keeps the viewer's Maximum Bid persistently visible; when the view cannot be known to be current it says so and disables the controls it would otherwise authorise, rather than presenting a stale figure as live; countdowns derive from a server-authoritative absolute close time; **the retract control (CAP-24) appears only while the viewer's Team may actually use it and counts down, and is never shown disabled** — a control that sits there and fails on submit is worse than none, because it is offered at the one moment a Manager has already made an expensive mistake — so where the view cannot be trusted as current that control **hides** where every other control disables, and **its countdown is the one in this product that must not keep running through a disconnect**, because the window can end early for a reason only the server knows; **a close time within ninety seconds of the leading Bid is shown as provisional**, since a retraction can move it backwards by up to 24 hours; full Bid history shows Team, acting Manager, amount and timestamp with no anonymity at any point; the bid control pre-fills the minimum legal Bid, never offers an amount above the viewer's Maximum Bid, and says "no cap limit" in words where it is unbounded; every Team's Cap Space, Committed Bids, Available Cap Space, Roster Count by Slot kind, Free Active/Bench Slots, Free Minor League Slots, Minors Exposure and Nomination Slot status are visible to **all** Managers for **every** Team — there is no partial-information layer — with Maximum Bid alone remaining a viewer's-own-Team figure, because it is per-Auction and a team-level rendering of it would authorise nothing. Realizes FR-23 – FR-25.

- **CAP-20 — Read the whole league in one list**
  - **intent:** Any Manager can see all 30 Teams at once — remaining roster slots, cap position, Nomination Slot — with a League Median to compare against, so the question "can that Team actually chase this player" is answered by reading rather than by arithmetic.
  - **success:** All 30 Teams are listed, never a subset and never paginated; the viewer's own Team is marked but takes no privileged position in the sort. Each row carries Free Active/Bench Slots as `Roster Count of 12`, **outstanding Bids against the allowance as `2 of 4 bids` with open lottery entries counted separately** — since CAP-19 the roster figure alone answers this screen's own question wrongly in both directions — Free Minor League Slots as `occupied of 3`, Injury Reserve occupancy shown as visibly outside the twelve, Cap Space, Committed Bids, Available Cap Space and Nomination Slot status — every one of them a figure CAP-10 already publishes for every Team, so the index adds adjacency and no new visibility. One line reports the **League Median** of Free Active/Bench Slots and Available Cap Space, labelled *median* and never *average*, taken as the **lower** of the two middle values so the figure stays on the $500,000 grid and renders losslessly at one decimal. **No Team is coloured, badged, ranked or annotated by its position relative to the median** — the comparison is stated as a fact and the app acquires no opinion from it. Sortable by Available Cap Space, Free Active/Bench Slots, Cap Space and Team name, defaulting to Team name; sorting never changes a figure. Columns shift by phase: slot columns give way to remaining Year Allotment and assignment completion in Contract Assignment, frozen in Archived, and the index is not offered in Setup, where CAP-1's import preview does a different job. The index authorises no action, so under the freshness rules it carries the age of its figures rather than disabling a control it does not have. A Team's figures here and on its CAP-10 detail view come from one computation and cannot disagree. At 375px it is a single-column list, not a horizontally scrolling table; on desktop it becomes a real table. PRD §10 example 28 passes. Realizes FR-39.

- **CAP-11 — Notify the league and its Managers through Discord**
  - **intent:** Every auction event is posted to the league Discord channel, and a notice aimed at a particular Manager carries an `@mention` that turns that public record into a personal push alert.
  - **success:** Nominations, Bids, **Bid Retractions (CAP-24)**, **cancellations and the restorations they cause (CAP-21)**, Auction Closes, draws (with seed and Contender list), and phase transitions are each posted at most once per event even under retry; a retraction post names the retracting Team, the amount withdrawn, and where the Auction went — the Team restored, or that it returned to Awaiting Opening Bid; outbid mentions land within 60 seconds of the outbidding Bid; both Managers of a co-managed Team are mentioned individually on every Team-affecting event; the notification categories are `outbid`, `contract_assignment` and `slot_release`, and muting suppresses the mention while the post still appears so the public record stays complete; `outbid` and `contract_assignment` are **never mutable**, refused server-side with stated wording rather than silently ignored. The **cancellation and restoration mentions of CAP-21, and the restoration a retraction causes under CAP-24, are all carried by `outbid`** — **no fourth category is added** and the mutable set stays exactly `slot_release`. The category therefore names four things, three of which are not literally an outbid: being outbid, having a Bid cancelled, being restored by a cancellation, and being restored because the Bid that displaced you was retracted. That is deliberate — it is the *"your position in an Auction changed without you"* class, and a Manager must not be able to mute the notice telling them they lost a Player through no act of their own. every payload declares its permitted mentions so nothing can mass-ping the league; a delivery failure never blocks or reverses the underlying auction action and is surfaced to the Commissioner. Realizes FR-26, FR-27.

- **CAP-12 — Assign contract lengths against the Year Allotment**
  - **intent:** After the Auction Phase, a Manager can assign a contract length to each Player their Team won, spending from one 4-year, one 3-year, two 2-year, and unlimited 1-year deals.
  - **success:** Remaining allotment is displayed as assignment proceeds and an assignment exceeding it is refused; assignments are freely changeable until the Team is submitted final; the Commissioner sees a live per-Team completion roster; Managers with unassigned Players are reminded by Discord mention at a Commissioner-configured interval. **No length is ever assigned by default.** The deadline notifies but resolves nothing; the export stays blocked until every Auction Contract has a length, and an unresponsive Team is resolved by the Commissioner assigning on its behalf under CAP-14 with a recorded reason — a visible act rather than a silent fallback. The Commissioner can extend the deadline and the extension is logged; Existing Contracts cannot be altered here. PRD §10 example 14 passes. Realizes FR-28, FR-29.

- **CAP-13 — Export the result to Fantrax and archive the auction**
  - **intent:** The Commissioner can review the complete outcome on screen and download it in Fantrax's import shape — both the Auction Contracts and every Team's full post-auction roster — then mark the auction archived.
  - **success:** Export is blocked, naming the offending Teams, if any Team's post-auction salary exceeds $165,000,000, any Team's Roster Count is other than exactly 12 (below leaves a hole, above breaches CAP-19's ceiling), or any Auction Contract still lacks a contract length; Free Agents not won need no export treatment and are omitted entirely — they simply remain free agents in Fantrax; both exports carry stable Fantrax player and team IDs so rows match without name reconciliation, and emit winning amount and Cap Hit as distinct values; the two exports reconcile — every Auction Contract appears in the roster export at the same amount and Slot; export is re-downloadable and logged each time; the app states plainly that upload to Fantrax is a manual Commissioner step; an archived auction accepts no further Bids, Nominations, assignments or overrides while its board, Audit Log and export stay viewable by all Managers. Realizes FR-30, FR-31, FR-36.

- **CAP-14 — Override, pause, and account for it**
  - **intent:** The Commissioner can void a Bid, adjust Cap Space, terminate an Auction, release a Nomination Slot, extend or expire any Clock, assign a contract length on a Team's behalf, and pause and resume the whole auction — with every one of those acts visible to the league.
  - **success:** Every override requires a free-text reason before it commits and is written with actor, timestamp, before-state, after-state and reason; voiding a Bid restores the Auction to its prior Leading Bidder and prior Auction Clock value **and recomputes the League Clock without that Bid's reset**, **re-validating the Team it restores against the money and capacity gates at that instant and skipping it in favour of the next Bid down if it fails** — the same walk CAP-21 runs and CAP-24 reuses, stated here 2026-09-16 because FR-32 had never said it and CAP-21's claim that all three withdrawals share one selector rested on nothing upstream, which can end the Auction Phase sooner — prospectively only, so nothing accepted in the interim is invalidated; overrides are refused once archived; while paused every Clock stops advancing and Bids, Nominations, **retractions (CAP-24)** and Roster Moves (CAP-25) are refused with the pause stated, and on resume each Clock continues with exactly the remaining time it held — **including the Retraction Window, which is derived from a timestamp rather than stored and would otherwise drain while the Manager was locked out of acting on it**; pause and resume are announced and posted to Discord; the Audit Log is readable by any Manager, filterable by Team, Player and event type, exportable, and cannot be edited or deleted by any role including the Commissioner. Realizes FR-32 – FR-34.

- **CAP-22 — Record a Roster Trade or a Drop**
  - **intent:** When managers trade or drop players in Fantrax during the auction, the Commissioner records the resulting roster state — a **Roster Trade** (two Teams) or a **Drop** — and every affected Team's cap and slot position recomputes from it — so the app's arithmetic never silently diverges from the league of record.
  - **success:** A Roster **Trade** names two Teams and moves Contracts **in both directions as one act**, with one gate evaluation at the end — a side may be empty, and applying the halves in sequence would refuse a legal trade on a state that never existed. Existing and Auction Contracts both move; a Contract travels unchanged in value and years; Committed Bids, contention entries, Nomination Slots and Cap Space never move. Slot Placement is re-evaluated against the receiving Team's occupancy, so **Cap Hit follows placement** and a stashed Player can arrive charging his full amount while his winning amount stands untouched; the sending Team's freed Minor League Slot recomputes its Minors Exposure across Auctions it still leads. A Drop frees the Slot and **converts the Player's *charged* Cap Hit into Dead Money at the same amount** — from Active/Bench it charges in full and Roster Count falls, so Maximum Bid drops by the $1,000,000 reserve on the freed hole; from Injury Reserve nothing else moves; from a Minor League Slot he was charging $0, so nothing is carried and Maximum Bid *rises* via Minors Exposure. **There is no exception, and the absence is a rule.** *(Amended 2026-09-16. This capability previously carved out a second-round rookie Contract of the current draft class — `2RK` plus a full unelapsed term — as removed rather than reclassified, releasing its Cap Hit. **The League deleted that carve-out**: Dead Money is waived only in an amnesty period before the auction opens. The Dead Money a release carries is the **charged** Cap Hit in one expression, and **row removal is derived from the charge having been $0, never decided** — nothing branches on Slot kind, on the rookie round, or on remaining term. The way back, should the League want a waiver again, is a **new rule**, never a designation test reintroduced into the one expression that decides the amount. While it stood, the exception fired once in production and released $1,000,000 a Team should have kept charging.)* Dead Money charges the Cap, occupies no Slot, counts toward no ceiling, is shown labelled and separate from the roster, is not importable in v1, and is excluded from both exports. Both acts are **refused outright** if either Team would fail the money or slots gate afterwards, naming the Team, gate, Auction and arithmetic — and a refusal can be caused by the **sending** Team, since a freed Slot costs $1,000,000 of reserve against a Cap Hit that may be worth less. **Neither ever cancels a Bid**, because cancellation is triggered only by a Close (CAP-21). Moving an assigned Auction Contract clears its length, returns the year to the sending Team's allotment and re-blocks the export until reassigned. Every act carries a mandatory reason, is refused once archived, is one transaction under the global write lock, and is recorded in the Audit Log with before/after for both Teams **but not broadcast to Discord**. PRD §10 examples 36–40, 42 and 43 pass, and **retired example 41 still pins that `2RK31` and `2031` stay distinguishable through the import** even though the designation now decides nothing. Realizes FR-41, FR-43.

- **CAP-25 — Rearrange a Roster's Slot Placements**
  - **intent:** A Manager can move their own Team's Contracts between an Active/Bench Slot and a Minor League Slot as one act, and the Commissioner can do the same on any Team's behalf — so a Contract placed automatically at a Close or on arrival from a Trade is not stuck there forever.
  - **success:** A Roster **Move** names **one Team** and one or more of its own Contracts, each with the Slot it is to occupy: **no Contract changes hands**, there is no counterparty, and no amount is edited. It is **one act with one evaluation at the end** — a swap applied a leg at a time transiently breaches a ceiling the act itself never breaches, which is CAP-22's lesson reached by a different act. **Only Active/Bench and Minor League Slots participate**: **Injury Reserve is not rearrangeable**, because IR is a Fantrax fact about a player's health rather than a placement this app assigns and moving a player out of it would be inventing a medical opinion, and **Dead Money is not a Slot** and never participates. **Only a Minor League Eligible Contract may occupy a Minor League Slot, and eligibility is remembered rather than read from the present** — the eligible set is every Contract the live pool flags **union every Contract the app has ever observed in a Minor League Slot**, from the import snapshot or any recorded act since. Without that second half, demoting an imported stash would destroy the only fact the app held saying he may be stashed, and the act would be irreversible. **Demotion needs no eligibility at all.** An imported Contract the app has **never** seen in a Minor League Slot and that was never in the pool **cannot be promoted**, and the refusal says *the app has never been told he is eligible* rather than asserting he is ineligible, which is something the app does not know. **Only a settled Contract moves** — a Player contested in an open Auction is on no roster and cannot be named, refused from the rules core before the money and slots gates. **Cap Hit follows placement**: promoted into a Minor League Slot a Contract charges **$0**, demoted to Active/Bench it charges its **full** amount, and its value never changes. Minors Exposure recomputes across every eligible Auction the Team leads and Roster Count moves with Active/Bench occupancy, so **a Move is a Maximum Bid lever in both directions** — and one direction is counterintuitive: *demoting* a stash frees a Minor League Slot and can **raise** Maximum Bid by cutting Minors Exposure while simultaneously **lowering** Cap Space by charging in full. **No assigned contract length is cleared** and the Year Allotment is untouched, because the Contract does not change hands. The **existing** money and slots gates are evaluated once over post-Move state — this capability writes no affordability check of its own — and where either fails **the whole Move is refused and nothing is written**, naming the gate, the Auction and the arithmetic. **It never cancels a Bid** (CAP-21). A **Manager** acts on **their own Team only**, resolved from the session and never from a form field, behind a confirmation sheet and with **no reason** — it is an ordinary strategic decision, not a referee intervention; the **Commissioner** may act on any Team through the override sheet with a mandatory reason. Permitted in the Auction and Contract Assignment Phases, refused in Setup, once archived, and **while paused** (CAP-14). **Unlimited**, since a Move is reversible and costs nothing to undo; the accepted cost is Audit Log volume. One transaction under the global write lock, mutating the Slot kind for Existing Contracts and appending **one** event carrying the **whole delta** — every Contract, both placements, both Cap Hits, and the Team's figures before and after — with Auction Contracts moving by the event alone under latest-placement-wins. Recorded in the Audit Log and **not broadcast to Discord**, consistent with CAP-22. **From the first Move the app is authoritative over placement**, so a placement difference against Fantrax before the export is **expected and is not a divergence** (CAP-23). PRD §10 examples 44, 45, 46 pass. Realizes FR-44.

- **CAP-23 — Detect a Roster Divergence from Fantrax**
  - **intent:** The app notices a trade or drop nobody told it about, rather than computing against a roster that stopped being true.
  - **success:** `getTeamRosters` is read at most hourly — not per tick — and compared against the app's rosters, **excluding Players won in this auction**, who are not yet in Fantrax. **Membership is the only fact taken from Fantrax** — a choice, not a limitation. The endpoint **does** carry `salary`, a contract designation and `salaryCap` (verified against the live league 2026-09-10; the earlier "carries none of these" came from an incomplete community wrapper). It is not used, because the app is authoritative for money while the auction runs and Fantrax's figures are the less trustworthy: **salaries arrive as floats carrying representation error**, and anything reading them must round to the nearest dollar and assert the $500,000 grid — truncation put 4 of 303 live rows off-grid. **Player ids must be normalised at the adapter boundary** (bare `01eon` here, asterisk-wrapped `*04ewu*` in the CSV the importer stores), or every row reads as a departure and an unknown arrival at once. `status` maps to all four Slot kinds and is advisory only. A Slot kind, where present, is advisory and confirmed by the Commissioner. **A placement difference alone is expected and is not a divergence**: from the first Roster Move onward the app is authoritative over placement and the export carries its decision out (CAP-25), so Fantrax disagreeing about which Slot a Player sits in — before that export lands — is the app being ahead rather than wrong, and raising it would train the Commissioner to dismiss this detector. A **paired** difference proposes a Roster **Trade**; an **unpaired departure** proposes a Drop; an **unknown arrival** is reported as an **error, prominently** — every acquisition in the window goes through the auction, so it cannot legitimately occur and signals a mis-set league id, a wrong period, or a genuine out-of-band change. **A read never writes**: no event, no reference row, no projection, and the only thing it can produce is a proposal. **A plausibility guard precedes every raise**, because a well-formed payload can still be wrong in a way the failure rules miss: nothing is raised unless all 30 Teams are present and none is empty, and a read whose divergences would touch more than a quarter of the League in one pass is a fault rather than news — an HTTP 200 carrying three Teams parses cleanly and would otherwise read as twenty-seven Teams releasing every Player. A tripped guard renders as **stopped**, never as *no divergences*, and never clears itself; the Commissioner can acknowledge it and see the proposals, so a genuinely busy day costs one confirmation and a bad payload costs nothing. A dismissed divergence stays suppressed until the underlying difference changes. Divergences reach the Commissioner's surface only, never a Manager's. Failure never blocks or reverses an auction action, and **repeated failure renders as stopped, never as "no divergences"** — an integration that has silently stopped must not read as a clean bill of health. Realizes FR-42.
  - **contingency discharged 2026-09-10:** the endpoint was called against the real BBSL league and answered unauthenticated with all 30 Teams, 303 rows, and `status` distinguishing all four Slot kinds. CAP-23 is viable and no longer conditional. It remains an undocumented endpoint that can change between offseasons, which is what its stated failure mode is for.

- **CAP-15 — Replay the auction against a synthetic clock**
  - **intent:** The whole auction can be run against an injected clock at arbitrary speed, so a time-compressed rehearsal against a fake 30-team league is possible before the real one opens.
  - **success:** A rehearsal drives a full auction — nomination, bidding, lottery, closes, phase end, assignment, export — end to end with no wall-clock waiting and no code path special-cased for the rehearsal; a close sweep running late produces exactly the outcome an on-time sweep would have, because `now` for each Auction is that Auction's own nominal expiry. Realizes AD-3.

- **CAP-16 — Detect that the tick has stopped**
  - **intent:** The operator is alerted when the close sweep stops running, when the outbox backs up, when a projection disagrees with a rebuild, or when free-tier quota is heading for exhaustion.
  - **success:** Every tick writes a heartbeat; a detector outside both Netlify and Supabase, sharing no component with the outbox path and not routed through Discord, alerts on a stale heartbeat, a growing backlog, and a projection-integrity mismatch; Netlify credit burn and Supabase Edge invocation burn are alerted on well before their ceilings; the alert reaches a sleeping operator. Realizes AD-19.

- **CAP-17 — Reconstruct the auction from outside Supabase**
  - **intent:** The auction can be restored from a copy held in a third failure domain, because the chosen free tier has no automatic backups, no point-in-time recovery, and no SLA.
  - **success:** A scheduled export of the event log plus the reference data a fold needs runs for the duration of the Auction Phase to storage outside Supabase and Netlify; a restore is rehearsed at least once before the auction opens and reproduces state that reconciles against the Discord record. A Discord incoming webhook is write-only and is not a restore path. Realizes AD-21.

## Constraints

- **Rule correctness is the product.** Determinism, reproducibility and auditability are bought at the direct expense of throughput. A rules bug is a worse failure than an outage. Scale is not a concern — 31 users, ~30 concurrent Auctions, a few thousand events.
- **Functional core / imperative shell, event-sourced auction domain.** Every rule lives in a pure core that reads no clock, no database and no random source, and imports nothing outside the TypeScript standard library. One core directory, imported by both the Node and Deno runtimes, never forked or re-implemented in SQL. It exposes exactly two entry points: `evaluate(state, command, now) → GateResults`, total, returning **every** gate's outcome with its own arithmetic; and `decide(state, command, now, seed) → Accepted<Event[]> | Rejected<GateResults>`. Server time and lottery seed are arguments. A rejection is a returned value, never a thrown exception. **"Exactly two entry points" governs the rules surface, not the whole core directory:** `core/money.ts` already holds pure money arithmetic reached by neither, and CAP-20's League Median belongs there alongside it. A read-model aggregate authorises nothing and refuses nothing, so it is not a rule and must never be reached through `evaluate()` or `decide()` — but it is money arithmetic, so it lives in the core, uses the branded integer type, and is covered by the §10 suite like any other.
- **One evaluator, two consumers.** `decide()` obtains its gate outcomes by calling `evaluate()`, never by re-deriving them, and the read path calls `evaluate()` directly to disable controls with stated reasons and to render the Maximum Bid breakdown. A control that says a bid is impossible and a refusal that explains why cannot disagree, because they are the same function. The gate set is fixed per command type, so no caller receives a partial record to guess at.
- **State mutation is serialized globally, not per Auction.** Maximum Bid depends on the bidding Team's leading positions in *other* Auctions, so two Managers of one co-managed Team bidding on two different Auctions each pass a per-Auction check and jointly breach the cap. The Team is raced, not the Auction. This supersedes the per-Auction wording the PRD originally carried.
- **The auction log is insert-only for every role, the Commissioner included.** The Audit Log is a read of that log, not a second table. Corrections append compensating events. Measurement fields (device class on Bids and Nominations, dispatch and delivery outcome on notifications) must be captured from the first event onward — an insert-only log cannot be backfilled.
- **Derived money is never stored.** Available Cap Space, Committed Bids, Minors Exposure, Overflow Count, Roster Reserve, Projected Active/Bench Additions and Maximum Bid are computed from committed state at validation time, against the hypothetical state that would exist *if the prospective Bid were accepted*. A displayed figure is a rendering; only a freshly computed figure authorises a Bid.
- **Money is integer dollars, branded at every runtime boundary.** No floats, no decimal library, no cents. Winning amount and Cap Hit are distinct persisted fields — a Minor League placement yields a $0 Cap Hit while the winning amount stands.
- **The abbreviated money rendering never reaches a CSV cell.** Money renders as `$14.5M` — always exactly one decimal, never dropped — in the UI and in Discord payloads, which are a view of the same figures for the same readers. It is lossless only because every BBSL figure sits on the $500,000 grid. Exports emit exact integer dollars; an abbreviated figure in an export would silently corrupt the Fantrax round-trip. **The grid is a product-wide invariant, not a bidding rule: any derived or aggregate figure the app displays must land on it.** Aggregates are where this is easiest to lose — a conventional median across an even number of Teams lands at $250,000 granularity, which is why CAP-20's League Median is defined as the lower middle value. A new derived money figure that cannot be shown to sit on the grid does not ship.
- **The import is thirty-one files, staged, then promoted atomically.** Parsed rows land in staging keyed by Team (and the pool as its own source), never straight into live reference tables. Per-file parse status survives between requests, one Team's file can be re-supplied without touching the other twenty-nine, and promotion is a single all-or-nothing transaction. A partially imported League is never a reachable state.
- **A displayed figure carries its freshness, and a stale one disables what it would authorise.** Every projection read carries one global event-`seq` watermark. The client derives exactly one of Live / Reconnecting / Stale from connection status **and** a positive periodic liveness check; in anything but Live, money either carries its age or the control it would authorise is disabled. **A stalled watermark must never imply staleness** — this league runs as a relay of timezone clusters and six silent hours at 4am Pacific is a healthy auction. The question is "can I still reach the server?", never "has anything changed?". Countdowns are exempt; they derive from absolute close times the client already holds.
- **Phase and role gate the surface from one server-resolved source.** The League phase and the viewer's role resolve server-side per request and produce one destination list, rendered in more than one place but never maintained as more than one list. A destination not live in the current phase is not rendered *and* its route refuses server-side. Phase is a projection folded from the log, never a flag anyone sets by hand.
- **Expiry is authoritative for validation from the instant it passes**, regardless of whether the sweep has recorded the close. A stalled sweep must produce *late* closes, never *wrong* ones.
- **The lottery seed is commit-reveal and unreadable before the draw** by any client-facing role. The builder of this app is also the Commissioner and a competing manager; this is what makes that acceptable.
- **No client write path.** No client-facing database role holds INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE on any table; the browser key is read-only and used only for Realtime. The Manager→Team binding and Commissioner flag are resolved server-side from application tables, never from a JWT claim the client can influence. Client-side validation only disables controls and pre-fills amounts.
- **Discord is both the only identity provider and the only outbound transport, and this is a knowingly accepted single point of failure.** This system sends no email for any purpose. Mitigations are mandatory, not optional: ≥30-day sessions, a Commissioner sign-in independent of Discord, and liveness alerting that does not route through Discord.
- **Zero budget.** Stacked free tiers with no SLA between them, two Supabase projects total (dev and prod, no third environment), a Netlify credit ceiling and a Supabase Edge invocation ceiling that are each silent-outage sources.
- **Availability is a posture, not a percentage.** No uptime figure is committed to, because nothing in this stack can underwrite one. The requirement is that an outage be *survivable rather than decisive*: late closes instead of wrong ones, a reachable pause, and a restorable auction. Any outage beyond 15 minutes is a pause plus a compensating clock adjustment, not a wait-and-see.
- **Mobile-first at 375px**, WCAG 2.1 AA contrast, touch targets ≥44×44px on every bidding and nomination control, and Auction state never conveyed by colour alone — every state carries a word **and** a shape, and a greyscale screenshot of any surface must stay fully readable. `DESIGN.md` holds the measured contrast ratios and the component anatomy; `EXPERIENCE.md` holds the information architecture, state patterns and key flows. Both are companions and both win over any mock.
- **No urgency design.** Countdown pressure, one-tap raises without confirmation, and suggested bid amounts are rejected outright — they would raise engagement and degrade the auction. The app's posture is referee, not croupier.
- **Performance floor:** board and Auction views interactive within 2 seconds on mobile data, a Bid submission acknowledged within 1 second, Maximum Bid recomputed within 1 second of any state change, and a board change reflected within 5 seconds of the originating action. These are floors, not optimisation targets — nothing above trades correctness for them.
- **Auditability over convenience.** Where a design choice trades away a record for a simpler flow, keep the record. This is the tiebreaker whenever two designs are otherwise equal.
- **PRD §10 examples 1–35 are the executable specification.** Each exists as a named test calling the core directly, with no database, HTTP, clock mocking or fixtures beyond a state literal. A rule change altering any example's outcome changes the PRD in the same commit. The suite is green before any production deploy.
- **Money and slots are two independent gates, and neither short-circuits the other.** Every Bid passes both Maximum Bid and Roster Capacity, with distinct refusal reasons and distinct arithmetic. An unbounded Maximum Bid under CAP-6 exempts nothing from CAP-19. Both outcomes are evaluated and returned whether or not the first passed, because a refusal reports the gate that **passed** alongside the one that refused — proving every check ran and this is the only obstacle.
- **The outage recovery procedure must be written before the auction opens** — how a >15-minute outage maps onto a pause plus a compensating clock adjustment. It is scheduled work inside the build epics, not an up-front document, and auction open is blocked on it.
- **Stack is pinned:** SvelteKit 2.70.2 (not 3.x), `@sveltejs/adapter-netlify` 6.0.4 with `edge: false`, TypeScript strict with `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, Node 24 LTS, Deno for the tick, Supabase-managed Postgres ≥15.1.1.61, one Supabase Cron schedule invoking one Edge Function.
- **Two overflow figures, never one.** `Overflow Count` (money side) counts Minimum-Bid Contention entries and feeds Minors Exposure; `Active/Bench Overflow` (slots side) excludes them and is the only overflow reaching Projected Active/Bench Additions. They were a single figure until 2026-09-08 and are one subtraction apart, so computing once and using twice is now wrong in a way no single test announces — it over-counts capacity for a Team in lotteries and refuses Bids the league allows.
- **A world change refuses; it never cascades.** A Roster **Trade**, **Drop** or **Move** (CAP-22, CAP-25) that would leave a Team failing the money or slots gate is refused outright, with nothing written. It never cancels a Bid to make room. This rules out extending CAP-21's cancellation trigger, which is bounded to *an Auction Close and nothing else* precisely to stop cascade re-entrancy, and rules out an administrative act stripping a Player from a Team that took no action. **Neither half of that reaches CAP-24**, which is why a retraction may call the restorer where a Trade may not: a retraction **restores** rather than strips, and the **bidding Team itself** invokes it. It also rules out a second affordability implementation: re-evaluation calls the **existing** pure gates, the same one-implementation-two-callers posture the restorer takes below. The Commissioner's remedies — void, or wait for a Close — both already exist and both already record themselves.
- **One restorer, three callers.** The algorithm that picks a restored Leading Bidder — walk the surviving history down, re-evaluate `{cap, slots}`, skip failures, stop at the first pass — lives in one pure function consumed by CAP-21's cancellation, CAP-14's Commissioner void, **and CAP-24's retraction**. They differ in exactly three parameters: retain-vs-erase the Bid in the fold, leave-vs-restore the Auction Clock, keep-vs-remove the League Clock reset. **A retraction passes the void's triple unchanged — erase, restore, remove — so it adds a caller and not a fourth parameter**, and an implementation reaching for a fourth axis has mis-read the requirement: the one case that would need one, retracting the Bid that dissolves a contention, is **forbidden outright** instead (CAP-24). Two implementations would disagree about the skip rule, and the disagreement would surface only when a Commissioner voided a Bid on an Auction whose under-bidders had since filled their rosters. **The cut-short's baseline is recorded on the displacing Bid, never reconstructed by folding back to its `seq`**, because the read path renders the window as a live countdown and because a reconstruction depends on reference-data replay the spine records as unresolved.
- **A withdrawal records its own restoration, in one of three named cases.** `BidCancelled` and `BidRetracted` each carry the withdrawn `seq`, the cause, and **one of three discriminated outcomes — `Restored` (Team, `seq`, amount, and the Auction Clock instant), `Exhausted` (Awaiting Opening Bid, Clock cleared), or `ContentionContinues` (no restoration, Clock untouched, Contenders remain)**. The projection reads that rather than re-deriving it by re-running gates inside a fold. **The third case must never be inferred from a null restoration**: both it and `Exhausted` carry no restored Team, and a fold that treated them alike would clear the Clock and return a **contested** Player to Awaiting Opening Bid, dissolving a live lottery. `close.ts` is the sole appender **of cancellations** — `AuctionClosed` first, then each `BidCancelled` in cascade order — and restoration is evaluated against post-close state; a retraction has no cascade to order and appends its own event under the same global lock. No migration: `event_type` is a generic `text` column.
- **AD-1 through AD-32 in `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` are binding in full.** The constraints above are the ones that bend decisions before you open the spine; they do not replace it.

## Non-goals

- **Not a Fantrax replacement.** Scoring, lineups, waivers and in-season roster management stay in Fantrax, as does the *execution* of every trade and drop. The app records their effect on rosters (CAP-22) because that effect changes what a Team may bid. This app is live for one offseason phase per year and goes quiet.
- **Not writing to Fantrax.** v1 never mutates Fantrax state; the Commissioner uploads the export and executes every trade by hand. **Fantrax exposes no write path at all** — verified 2026-09-10, every method in both community wrappers is a GET — so this is a property of the integration, not only a scope choice. v1 does read one endpoint, for reconciliation only (CAP-23); a read can raise a proposal and can never change app state.
- **Not enforcing activation.** Promoting a stashed Player out of a Minor League Slot requires absorbing his full contract against the cap. That rule is what makes unbounded eligible bidding self-correcting, and it is enforced in Fantrax in-season. This app never models it, never warns about it, and never blocks a Bid on account of it. A Team $2M under the cap winning a $30M eligible Player is correct behaviour, not a defect.
- **Not multi-tenant.** One League, hard-wired rules. League constants are named values in `core/constants.ts`; no admin UI edits them, no league creation, no other sports.
- **Not a valuation engine.** No projections, rankings, ADP, or suggested bid. The app enforces rules; it does not advise.
- **No proxy or max auto-bidding.** Rejected outright, not deferred — it changes auction dynamics and interacts badly with the lottery rule.
- **No overnight clock freeze and no anti-snipe extension.** Rejected for v1 as rules changes requiring league buy-in, not engineering decisions.
- **Not a draft tool and not a trade broker.** No rookie draft, no pick trading, and no proposal, negotiation or acceptance flow. Trades are agreed in Discord and executed in Fantrax; the app records the resulting rosters (CAP-22) and brokers nothing. **Committed capital never moves** — a leading Bid, a contention entry and Cap Space cannot be traded.
- **Not a chat app.** Discussion stays in Discord. The app posts to Discord; it does not host conversation.
- **No public or spectator access.** Authentication is required for everything; there is no anonymous read-only board.
- **No web push or PWA notifications**, no future-year cap projection, no Existing-Contract extensions or restructures, and no historical archive across offseasons. v1 covers one auction.

## Success signal

The BBSL runs its 2026 offseason auction entirely in this app and finishes with **no result contested on the grounds that the app computed a cap figure, clock, increment, or lottery draw incorrectly** — demonstrable as fewer than five **corrective** Commissioner overrides across the whole auction, none of them correcting an app error, against a complete Audit Log the league can read. **Roster Trades, Drops and Moves (CAP-22, CAP-25) are excluded from that count** — they record what the league did, not what the app got wrong, and a trade-heavy offseason must not read as a failing product. The auction ends on its own League Clock expiry, contract assignment completes, and the Fantrax export passes its cap and roster checks on the first attempt.

## Assumptions

- **When voiding a Bid recomputes the League Clock to an instant already past, the Auction Phase ends at the *next* clock evaluation and takes effect prospectively only** — nothing accepted in the interim is invalidated. Derived from the commissioner's OQ-7 answer rather than stated by it; the alternative, retroactive invalidation, contradicts the append-only log and the "late, not wrong" posture the close sweep runs on. **This is the only live inference in the spec.**
- Minors Exposure is sized to the sum of the *largest* surplus Eligible Leading Bids, because Slot Placement follows close order and no bidder can steer which of its wins lands in minors. A cheaper worst-case assumption would let a Team over-commit.
- Outbid notices cannot be muted, on the grounds that muting them undermines the fairness premise of a 24/7 clock.
- An unbid Nomination holds its Team's Nomination Slot for the remainder of the Auction Phase. Confirmed by the league as the accepted consequence of the rules as written; the app surfaces it rather than mitigating it.
- Verifiable Randomizer fairness — recorded seed and Contender list, independently reproducible — is a hard requirement rather than a nicety.
- The Commissioner pause exists as an outage and dispute escape hatch, despite the league running clocks continuously by rule. It is an administrative tool, not a scheduled freeze.
- Minor League Eligibility defaults to *not* eligible on import, so an unset flag fails safe rather than permissive. The commissioner's answer settled that the flag is hand-set; the direction of the default is this spec's call.
- **League Median takes the lower of the two middle values rather than their mean.** The commissioner asked for a median and did not specify the even-count rule; across 30 Teams the conventional definition lands at $250,000 granularity, off the $500,000 grid and not losslessly renderable at one decimal. The lower middle value is always a figure a real Team holds. Rounding the mean to the grid was rejected — it publishes a number nobody holds and no manager can reproduce — as was a two-decimal rendering, which would make the median the only money figure in the product with its own format.

*Retired 2026-08-17 when the commissioner answered the open questions:* the $100,000 granularity assumption (the real figure is $500,000), the imported-eligibility-flag assumption (the export does not carry it), and the 1-year assignment default (removed — the export blocks instead).

*Retired 2026-08-18, surfaced by UX journey work:* the **two-file import** assumption. CAP-1 previously read "the auction refuses to open unless **both** imports are committed", and FR-1 described "a Fantrax-exported roster/salary file" covering all 30 Teams. The real Fantrax workflow produces **thirty-one** exports — one Free Agent pool plus one per Team. CAP-1, FR-1 and FR-3 are corrected accordingly. This changes the import surface materially (batch supply, per-file parse status, Team resolution by name, outstanding-Team naming, single-Team re-supply) and is the reason `EXPERIENCE.md` specifies an import status list rather than two upload controls.

*Retired 2026-08-18 when the commissioner named the missing Teams index (CAP-20, FR-39):* the **partial-information** assumption. CAP-10 previously reserved Free Minor League Slots and Minors Exposure to the viewer's own Team. Neither reservation was enforceable — Free Minor League Slots is `3 −` an occupancy CAP-10 already publishes, and Minors Exposure is Committed Bids less a Team's non-eligible leading amounts, every one of which is public on the board. A thirty-row index would have handed both figures for the whole league to anyone at a glance. **Every Team figure is now explicitly public**, Maximum Bid excepted on the different ground that it is per-Auction. Concealment that survives only until someone bothers to subtract is worse than none, because it misleads exactly the managers who trust it.
