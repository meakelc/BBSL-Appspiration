# BBSL Offseason Free Agent Auction

**Vocabulary is fixed.** Every capitalised domain term below — Salary Cap, Cap Hit, Committed Bids, Available Cap Space, Free Minor League Slots, Free Active/Bench Slots, Eligible Leading Bids, Overflow Count, Minors Exposure, Roster Count, Roster Reserve, Roster Capacity, Maximum Bid, League Median, Slot Placement, Minor League Eligible, Nomination Slot, Minimum-Bid Contention, Contender, Randomizer, Auction Clock, League Clock, Year Allotment — is defined in PRD §3 and is used here with exactly that meaning. Identifier names in code are these terms verbatim. A synonym is a defect.

**Two numbers to internalise before reading further,** because both changed on 2026-08-17 and both are easy to carry a stale copy of: a Bid is a whole multiple of **$500,000** (not $100,000), and a Team has exactly **12** Active/Bench Slots — a ceiling, not just a floor.

## Why

A vision to realize, forced by a pain. The BBSL is a 30-team dynasty fantasy basketball league entering its sixth season, and for five offseasons it has run its free agent auction by hand. The rules are good — open ascending bidding on a rolling 24-hour clock, nomination slots that pace the market, a $1M minimum-salary lottery, a contract-length allotment that forces real dynasty tradeoffs — and running them manually is a part-time job for one person. Someone has to notice a clock expired, check whether a team can afford the bid it just posted, remember that a team already spent its 4-year deal. When that person gets it wrong at 1am, thirty people find out in the morning. This app runs the auction instead: it knows every Team's Maximum Bid to the dollar, refuses illegal bids at submission rather than reversing them afterward, runs its own clocks, draws the lottery with a seed anyone can verify, ends itself on 48 hours of league silence, and hands the Commissioner a file to upload back into Fantrax. The point is that the Commissioner gets to play in their own league again, and that when a team loses a player by $500k at 4am, nobody has to wonder whether the app got it right. It is built and operated by one person who is also the Commissioner and a competing manager — which is why auditability is structural here rather than decorative.

## Capabilities

- **CAP-1 — Import the league and open the auction**
  - **intent:** Commissioner can load all 30 Teams' Existing Contracts, Roster Slots and the Free Agent pool from Fantrax CSV exports — **thirty-one files: one Free Agent pool export plus one roster export per Team** — and transition the League into the Auction Phase.
  - **success:** All thirty-one files are supplied in a single batch and each is resolved independently: a Team file that matches none of the 30 Teams, or a Team already supplied, is refused naming the file. A malformed or contradictory file is refused naming the offending row — including a starting state that breaches a slot ceiling (more than 12 Active/Bench, 2 Injury Reserve, or 3 Minor League players). Parse status is reported per file and every Team still outstanding is **named, not counted**. A single Team's file may be re-supplied to replace that Team's rows without re-supplying the other twenty-nine. A per-Team preview of Roster Count and Cap Space across all 30 Teams is confirmed before commit, and commit is all-or-nothing — a partially imported League is never a reachable state. The auction refuses to open unless the pool import and all thirty Team imports are committed and every Team has a bound Manager. Re-import is permitted before open and refused after. Realizes FR-1 – FR-3.

- **CAP-18 — Set Minor League Eligibility by hand**
  - **intent:** Commissioner can mark which pooled Players are Minor League Eligible, because the Fantrax export does not carry the flag.
  - **success:** Every imported Player defaults to **not** eligible, so an unset flag can never grant CAP-6's unbounded bidding by omission; the flag is settable individually and in bulk until the auction opens, locked afterward except by override, and every change is recorded as an event with actor, Player and before/after values — so a state rebuild reproduces the flag as it stood rather than as it is now. The setting screen states the consequence in words ("this Player can be stashed at a $0 Cap Hit") rather than showing a bare checkbox. Realizes FR-38.

- **CAP-2 — Authenticate Managers and bind them to Teams**
  - **intent:** A Manager can reach their Team's auction view through Discord OAuth without a password, acting on behalf of exactly one Team, with the Commissioner holding administrative rights on top of ordinary Manager rights over their own Team.
  - **success:** A Discord account the Commissioner has not pre-registered cannot obtain a session, and is refused without enumerating the league — the refusal states only that the Commissioner must add the account, never whether that identity or any Team exists, and offers no retry loop to probe with. A session that expired reads as expired, not as a fresh sign-out. Sessions persist ≥30 days; every Nomination and Bid records the acting Manager alongside the Team and both surface in public displays; Commissioner-only capabilities are unreachable by a non-Commissioner by UI and by direct request alike; the Commissioner's own Team is subject to every ordinary rule; a Commissioner sign-in path exists that does not depend on Discord. No email is sent by this system for any purpose. Realizes FR-4 – FR-6.

- **CAP-3 — Nominate Free Agents onto the Bid Board**
  - **intent:** A Manager can put any Free Agent on the Bid Board while their Team's Nomination Slot is unused, without obligation to bid on him.
  - **success:** The Player appears in Awaiting Opening Bid with no Auction Clock; the Slot is marked used and released only at that Player's Auction Close, regardless of who won or whether the nominator ever bid; a Team may nominate a Player it could not afford; two concurrent Nominations of the same Player produce exactly one; an unbid Nomination is flagged on the board and to its owner at 24 hours and never auto-expires. Realizes FR-7 – FR-10.

- **CAP-4 — Compute and display Maximum Bid per Auction**
  - **intent:** A Manager can see, on the board and on every Auction, the largest legal Bid their Team may place on that specific Auction right now, with the arithmetic broken out rather than as a bare number.
  - **success:** For a non-eligible Player, `Maximum Bid = Available Cap Space − Roster Reserve`; for a Minor League Eligible Player the Team's Free Minor League Slots can absorb, the figure reads as unbounded in words rather than as a number; the components — Cap Space, Committed Bids, Minors Exposure, Roster Reserve — are each shown; the figure recomputes for all affected Teams and Auctions within one second of any Bid, Auction Close, or override; controls disable with a stated reason where the figure is below the minimum legal Bid. Maximum Bid governs **money only** — CAP-19 is a separate gate an unbounded figure does not exempt a Bid from. Realizes FR-12, FR-25.

- **CAP-5 — Place a Bid, or be refused with the arithmetic**
  - **intent:** A Manager can bid on any Auction their Team is not leading, and an illegal Bid is refused at submission rather than accepted and reversed.
  - **success:** In Standard Contention a Bid is valid only at ≥ current high + $500,000 and as a whole multiple of **$500,000** — the two coincide, so no grid-valid Bid can be sub-increment and the granularity check earns its keep in Minimum-Bid Contention, where the increment rule does not apply; a Team cannot bid against itself; a Bid over Maximum Bid never appears on the Bid Board or in the Audit Log as valid, and its refusal shows Cap Space, Committed Bids, Minors Exposure, Roster Reserve and the resulting Maximum Bid, **and reports both gates — the one that refused and the one that passed, with its figure**; validation runs server-side against committed state at submission, so a Bid valid when composed but stale on arrival is refused with current figures; no control to cancel, edit, or lower an accepted Bid exists. Realizes FR-11, FR-13, FR-15.

- **CAP-19 — Enforce Roster Capacity**
  - **intent:** A Team has exactly 12 Active/Bench Slots — 8 active plus 4 bench — and cannot commit itself to more players than it has Slots for.
  - **success:** A Bid is refused when `Roster Count + Projected Active/Bench Additions > 12`, computed on the same post-bid basis as Roster Reserve. This is a **second refusal ground independent of Maximum Bid** — a Team can fail it with unlimited cap space and pass it with none, neither check subsumes the other, and both run on every Bid; the two refusals carry distinct machine-readable reasons and distinct arithmetic, and reporting a capacity refusal as a cap refusal is a defect. A Team at Roster Count 12 cannot bid on any non-eligible Player whatever its Cap Space, but can still bid on a Minor League Eligible Player a Free Minor League Slot would absorb. An eligible Bid creating Overflow with nowhere to land is refused, naming the earlier Auction causing it — there is **no carve-out** for automatic Slot Placement. A Team that reaches the ceiling has its controls disabled across the board with the reason shown, not discovered at submission, because the state is reachable straight from import. PRD §10 examples 24, 25 pass. Realizes FR-37.

- **CAP-6 — Commit and release capital, including Minors Exposure**
  - **intent:** The system holds a Team's leading money against its cap for the life of an Auction, and treats a Minor League Eligible Player its Free Minor League Slots can absorb as committing nothing.
  - **success:** Committed Bids include leading amounts on non-eligible Auctions, $1,000,000 per Contender position in a Minimum-Bid Contention on a non-eligible Player, and Minors Exposure — the sum of the Overflow Count largest Eligible Leading Bids, zero when `N ≤ M`. Capital releases the instant a Team stops leading or loses a draw. A later cheap eligible Bid can be refused because it exposes an earlier expensive one, and the refusal names the Auction causing the exposure; an accepted Bid is never retroactively invalidated. PRD §10 examples 16–22 pass. Realizes FR-14, FR-35.

- **CAP-7 — Run the Auction Clock and close Auctions**
  - **intent:** Each open Auction runs a 24-hour clock that closes it and awards the Player with automatic Slot Placement, with no user present.
  - **success:** Every valid Bid in Standard Contention sets the Clock to exactly 24 hours from that Bid; the Clock never pauses for hours-of-day; absolute close time is displayed in the viewer's local timezone alongside the countdown; close fires within 60 seconds of nominal expiry and survives a process restart; a Minor League Eligible Player takes a free Minor League Slot at a $0 Cap Hit or an Active/Bench Slot at the winning amount otherwise; Roster Count increases only on an Active/Bench placement; the nominator's Slot releases; Auction Close does not reset the League Clock. Realizes FR-16, FR-21.

- **CAP-8 — Run the Minimum-Bid Contention lottery**
  - **intent:** An Auction opened at exactly $1,000,000 becomes a lottery any Team can join at exactly $1,000,000, dissolves back to ordinary ascending rules on a Bid of $1,500,000 or more, and otherwise resolves by an auditable random draw at expiry.
  - **success:** The Clock is fixed from the Opening Bid and does not reset on a join; the state is visually distinct and states in words that the clock will not reset; a Bid strictly between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000 is refused as neither a valid entry nor a valid raise; dissolution discards the Contender list, releases every commitment, and resets the Clock to 24 hours regardless of Contender count; the draw is uniform at `1/n`, resolves a single-Contender lottery to that Contender, and records seed, ordered Contender list as it stood at expiry, and selection permanently on the closed Auction and in the Audit Log — sufficient for any Manager to reproduce the result independently. PRD §10 examples 6–11 pass. Realizes FR-17 – FR-20.

- **CAP-9 — Run the League Clock and end the Auction Phase**
  - **intent:** The auction ends itself when the league goes quiet for 48 hours.
  - **success:** The League Clock is reset by a Nomination and by an accepted Bid (including a lottery join) and by nothing else — not by an Auction Close, draw, dissolution, void, override, or pause/resume; on expiry Nomination and bidding are disabled league-wide, Auctions still in Awaiting Opening Bid terminate with no winner and their Players return to the pool, the League moves to Contract Assignment Phase, and all Managers are notified. The Clock is **derived from the surviving reset events, never stored as a countdown**: voiding a Bid removes its reset and the Clock recomputes shorter, prospectively only. PRD §10 examples 13, 27 pass. Realizes FR-22.

- **CAP-10 — Read the board, an Auction, and any Team**
  - **intent:** Any Manager can see every open Auction with live state, any single Auction's complete history, and any Team's roster and cap position — one-handed on a phone.
  - **success:** Opening the app mid-auction lands on what the viewer leads, has been outbid on, and is a Contender in — the full board is one step away and opens unfiltered, so a filtered view can never be mistaken for the whole board. The board shows identity, price, Leading Bidder, state and time remaining per Auction, is sortable and filterable to what the viewer leads or contends in, updates within 5 seconds of the originating action without a manual refresh, and keeps the viewer's Maximum Bid persistently visible; when the view cannot be known to be current it says so and disables the controls it would otherwise authorise, rather than presenting a stale figure as live; countdowns derive from a server-authoritative absolute close time; full Bid history shows Team, acting Manager, amount and timestamp with no anonymity at any point; the bid control pre-fills the minimum legal Bid, never offers an amount above the viewer's Maximum Bid, and says "no cap limit" in words where it is unbounded; every Team's Cap Space, Committed Bids, Available Cap Space, Roster Count by Slot kind, Free Active/Bench Slots, Free Minor League Slots, Minors Exposure and Nomination Slot status are visible to **all** Managers for **every** Team — there is no partial-information layer — with Maximum Bid alone remaining a viewer's-own-Team figure, because it is per-Auction and a team-level rendering of it would authorise nothing. Realizes FR-23 – FR-25.

- **CAP-20 — Read the whole league in one list**
  - **intent:** Any Manager can see all 30 Teams at once — remaining roster slots, cap position, Nomination Slot — with a League Median to compare against, so the question "can that Team actually chase this player" is answered by reading rather than by arithmetic.
  - **success:** All 30 Teams are listed, never a subset and never paginated; the viewer's own Team is marked but takes no privileged position in the sort. Each row carries Free Active/Bench Slots as `Roster Count of 12`, Free Minor League Slots as `occupied of 3`, Injury Reserve occupancy shown as visibly outside the twelve, Cap Space, Committed Bids, Available Cap Space and Nomination Slot status — every one of them a figure CAP-10 already publishes for every Team, so the index adds adjacency and no new visibility. One line reports the **League Median** of Free Active/Bench Slots and Available Cap Space, labelled *median* and never *average*, taken as the **lower** of the two middle values so the figure stays on the $500,000 grid and renders losslessly at one decimal. **No Team is coloured, badged, ranked or annotated by its position relative to the median** — the comparison is stated as a fact and the app acquires no opinion from it. Sortable by Available Cap Space, Free Active/Bench Slots, Cap Space and Team name, defaulting to Team name; sorting never changes a figure. Columns shift by phase: slot columns give way to remaining Year Allotment and assignment completion in Contract Assignment, frozen in Archived, and the index is not offered in Setup, where CAP-1's import preview does a different job. The index authorises no action, so under the freshness rules it carries the age of its figures rather than disabling a control it does not have. A Team's figures here and on its CAP-10 detail view come from one computation and cannot disagree. At 375px it is a single-column list, not a horizontally scrolling table; on desktop it becomes a real table. PRD §10 example 28 passes. Realizes FR-39.

- **CAP-11 — Notify the league and its Managers through Discord**
  - **intent:** Every auction event is posted to the league Discord channel, and a notice aimed at a particular Manager carries an `@mention` that turns that public record into a personal push alert.
  - **success:** Nominations, Bids, Auction Closes, draws (with seed and Contender list), and phase transitions are each posted at most once per event even under retry; outbid mentions land within 60 seconds of the outbidding Bid; both Managers of a co-managed Team are mentioned individually on every Team-affecting event; exactly three categories are mutable, and muting suppresses the mention while the post still appears so the public record stays complete; outbid and Contract Assignment notices are never mutable; every payload declares its permitted mentions so nothing can mass-ping the league; a delivery failure never blocks or reverses the underlying auction action and is surfaced to the Commissioner. Realizes FR-26, FR-27.

- **CAP-12 — Assign contract lengths against the Year Allotment**
  - **intent:** After the Auction Phase, a Manager can assign a contract length to each Player their Team won, spending from one 4-year, one 3-year, two 2-year, and unlimited 1-year deals.
  - **success:** Remaining allotment is displayed as assignment proceeds and an assignment exceeding it is refused; assignments are freely changeable until the Team is submitted final; the Commissioner sees a live per-Team completion roster; Managers with unassigned Players are reminded by Discord mention at a Commissioner-configured interval. **No length is ever assigned by default.** The deadline notifies but resolves nothing; the export stays blocked until every Auction Contract has a length, and an unresponsive Team is resolved by the Commissioner assigning on its behalf under CAP-14 with a recorded reason — a visible act rather than a silent fallback. The Commissioner can extend the deadline and the extension is logged; Existing Contracts cannot be altered here. PRD §10 example 14 passes. Realizes FR-28, FR-29.

- **CAP-13 — Export the result to Fantrax and archive the auction**
  - **intent:** The Commissioner can review the complete outcome on screen and download it in Fantrax's import shape — both the Auction Contracts and every Team's full post-auction roster — then mark the auction archived.
  - **success:** Export is blocked, naming the offending Teams, if any Team's post-auction salary exceeds $165,000,000, any Team's Roster Count is other than exactly 12 (below leaves a hole, above breaches CAP-19's ceiling), or any Auction Contract still lacks a contract length; Free Agents not won need no export treatment and are omitted entirely — they simply remain free agents in Fantrax; both exports carry stable Fantrax player and team IDs so rows match without name reconciliation, and emit winning amount and Cap Hit as distinct values; the two exports reconcile — every Auction Contract appears in the roster export at the same amount and Slot; export is re-downloadable and logged each time; the app states plainly that upload to Fantrax is a manual Commissioner step; an archived auction accepts no further Bids, Nominations, assignments or overrides while its board, Audit Log and export stay viewable by all Managers. Realizes FR-30, FR-31, FR-36.

- **CAP-14 — Override, pause, and account for it**
  - **intent:** The Commissioner can void a Bid, adjust Cap Space, terminate an Auction, release a Nomination Slot, extend or expire any Clock, assign a contract length on a Team's behalf, and pause and resume the whole auction — with every one of those acts visible to the league.
  - **success:** Every override requires a free-text reason before it commits and is written with actor, timestamp, before-state, after-state and reason; voiding a Bid restores the Auction to its prior Leading Bidder and prior Auction Clock value **and recomputes the League Clock without that Bid's reset**, which can end the Auction Phase sooner — prospectively only, so nothing accepted in the interim is invalidated; overrides are refused once archived; while paused every Clock stops advancing and Bids and Nominations are refused with the pause stated, and on resume each Clock continues with exactly the remaining time it held; pause and resume are announced and posted to Discord; the Audit Log is readable by any Manager, filterable by Team, Player and event type, exportable, and cannot be edited or deleted by any role including the Commissioner. Realizes FR-32 – FR-34.

- **CAP-15 — Replay the auction against a synthetic clock**
  - **intent:** The whole auction can be run against an injected clock at arbitrary speed, so a time-compressed rehearsal against a fake 30-team league is possible before the real one opens.
  - **success:** A rehearsal drives a full auction — nomination, bidding, lottery, closes, phase end, assignment, export — end to end with no wall-clock waiting and no code path special-cased for the rehearsal; a close sweep running late produces exactly the outcome an on-time sweep would have, because `now` for each Auction is that Auction's own nominal expiry. Realizes AD-3.

- **CAP-16 — Detect that the tick has stopped**
  - **intent:** The operator is alerted when the close sweep stops running, when the outbox backs up, when a projection disagrees with a rebuild, or when free-tier quota is heading for exhaustion.
  - **success:** Every tick writes a heartbeat; a detector outside both Netlify and Supabase, sharing no component with the outbox path and not routed through Discord, alerts on a stale heartbeat, a growing backlog, and a projection-integrity mismatch; Netlify credit burn and Supabase Edge invocation burn are alerted on well before their ceilings; the alert reaches a sleeping operator. Realizes AD-19.

- **CAP-17 — Reconstruct the auction from outside Supabase**
  - **intent:** The auction can be restored from a copy held in a third failure domain, because the chosen free tier has no automatic backups, no point-in-time recovery, and no SLA.
  - **success:** A scheduled export of the event log plus the reference data a fold needs runs for the duration of the Auction Phase to storage outside Supabase and Netlify; a restore is rehearsed at least once before the auction opens and reproduces state that reconciles against the Discord record. A Discord incoming webhook is write-only and is not a restore path. Realizes AD-21.

## Constraints

- **Rule correctness is the product.** Determinism, reproducibility and auditability are bought at the direct expense of throughput. A rules bug is a worse failure than an outage. Scale is not a concern — 31 users, ~30 concurrent Auctions, a few thousand events.
- **Functional core / imperative shell, event-sourced auction domain.** Every rule lives in a pure core that reads no clock, no database and no random source, and imports nothing outside the TypeScript standard library. One core directory, imported by both the Node and Deno runtimes, never forked or re-implemented in SQL. It exposes exactly two entry points: `evaluate(state, command, now) → GateResults`, total, returning **every** gate's outcome with its own arithmetic; and `decide(state, command, now, seed) → Accepted<Event[]> | Rejected<GateResults>`. Server time and lottery seed are arguments. A rejection is a returned value, never a thrown exception. **"Exactly two entry points" governs the rules surface, not the whole core directory:** `core/money.ts` already holds pure money arithmetic reached by neither, and CAP-20's League Median belongs there alongside it. A read-model aggregate authorises nothing and refuses nothing, so it is not a rule and must never be reached through `evaluate()` or `decide()` — but it is money arithmetic, so it lives in the core, uses the branded integer type, and is covered by the §10 suite like any other.
- **One evaluator, two consumers.** `decide()` obtains its gate outcomes by calling `evaluate()`, never by re-deriving them, and the read path calls `evaluate()` directly to disable controls with stated reasons and to render the Maximum Bid breakdown. A control that says a bid is impossible and a refusal that explains why cannot disagree, because they are the same function. The gate set is fixed per command type, so no caller receives a partial record to guess at.
- **State mutation is serialized globally, not per Auction.** Maximum Bid depends on the bidding Team's leading positions in *other* Auctions, so two Managers of one co-managed Team bidding on two different Auctions each pass a per-Auction check and jointly breach the cap. The Team is raced, not the Auction. This supersedes the per-Auction wording the PRD originally carried.
- **The auction log is insert-only for every role, the Commissioner included.** The Audit Log is a read of that log, not a second table. Corrections append compensating events. Measurement fields (device class on Bids and Nominations, dispatch and delivery outcome on notifications) must be captured from the first event onward — an insert-only log cannot be backfilled.
- **Derived money is never stored.** Available Cap Space, Committed Bids, Minors Exposure, Overflow Count, Roster Reserve, Projected Active/Bench Additions and Maximum Bid are computed from committed state at validation time, against the hypothetical state that would exist *if the prospective Bid were accepted*. A displayed figure is a rendering; only a freshly computed figure authorises a Bid.
- **Money is integer dollars, branded at every runtime boundary.** No floats, no decimal library, no cents. Winning amount and Cap Hit are distinct persisted fields — a Minor League placement yields a $0 Cap Hit while the winning amount stands.
- **The abbreviated money rendering never reaches a CSV cell.** Money renders as `$14.5M` — always exactly one decimal, never dropped — in the UI and in Discord payloads, which are a view of the same figures for the same readers. It is lossless only because every BBSL figure sits on the $500,000 grid. Exports emit exact integer dollars; an abbreviated figure in an export would silently corrupt the Fantrax round-trip. **The grid is a product-wide invariant, not a bidding rule: any derived or aggregate figure the app displays must land on it.** Aggregates are where this is easiest to lose — a conventional median across an even number of Teams lands at $250,000 granularity, which is why CAP-20's League Median is defined as the lower middle value. A new derived money figure that cannot be shown to sit on the grid does not ship.
- **The import is thirty-one files, staged, then promoted atomically.** Parsed rows land in staging keyed by Team (and the pool as its own source), never straight into live reference tables. Per-file parse status survives between requests, one Team's file can be re-supplied without touching the other twenty-nine, and promotion is a single all-or-nothing transaction. A partially imported League is never a reachable state.
- **A displayed figure carries its freshness, and a stale one disables what it would authorise.** Every projection read carries one global event-`seq` watermark. The client derives exactly one of Live / Reconnecting / Stale from connection status **and** a positive periodic liveness check; in anything but Live, money either carries its age or the control it would authorise is disabled. **A stalled watermark must never imply staleness** — this league runs as a relay of timezone clusters and six silent hours at 4am Pacific is a healthy auction. The question is "can I still reach the server?", never "has anything changed?". Countdowns are exempt; they derive from absolute close times the client already holds.
- **Phase and role gate the surface from one server-resolved source.** The League phase and the viewer's role resolve server-side per request and produce one destination list, rendered in more than one place but never maintained as more than one list. A destination not live in the current phase is not rendered *and* its route refuses server-side. Phase is a projection folded from the log, never a flag anyone sets by hand.
- **Expiry is authoritative for validation from the instant it passes**, regardless of whether the sweep has recorded the close. A stalled sweep must produce *late* closes, never *wrong* ones.
- **The lottery seed is commit-reveal and unreadable before the draw** by any client-facing role. The builder of this app is also the Commissioner and a competing manager; this is what makes that acceptable.
- **No client write path.** No client-facing database role holds INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE on any table; the browser key is read-only and used only for Realtime. The Manager→Team binding and Commissioner flag are resolved server-side from application tables, never from a JWT claim the client can influence. Client-side validation only disables controls and pre-fills amounts.
- **Discord is both the only identity provider and the only outbound transport, and this is a knowingly accepted single point of failure.** This system sends no email for any purpose. Mitigations are mandatory, not optional: ≥30-day sessions, a Commissioner sign-in independent of Discord, and liveness alerting that does not route through Discord.
- **Zero budget.** Stacked free tiers with no SLA between them, two Supabase projects total (dev and prod, no third environment), a Netlify credit ceiling and a Supabase Edge invocation ceiling that are each silent-outage sources.
- **Availability is a posture, not a percentage.** No uptime figure is committed to, because nothing in this stack can underwrite one. The requirement is that an outage be *survivable rather than decisive*: late closes instead of wrong ones, a reachable pause, and a restorable auction. Any outage beyond 15 minutes is a pause plus a compensating clock adjustment, not a wait-and-see.
- **Mobile-first at 375px**, WCAG 2.1 AA contrast, touch targets ≥44×44px on every bidding and nomination control, and Auction state never conveyed by colour alone — every state carries a word **and** a shape, and a greyscale screenshot of any surface must stay fully readable. `DESIGN.md` holds the measured contrast ratios and the component anatomy; `EXPERIENCE.md` holds the information architecture, state patterns and key flows. Both are companions and both win over any mock.
- **No urgency design.** Countdown pressure, one-tap raises without confirmation, and suggested bid amounts are rejected outright — they would raise engagement and degrade the auction. The app's posture is referee, not croupier.
- **Performance floor:** board and Auction views interactive within 2 seconds on mobile data, a Bid submission acknowledged within 1 second, Maximum Bid recomputed within 1 second of any state change, and a board change reflected within 5 seconds of the originating action. These are floors, not optimisation targets — nothing above trades correctness for them.
- **Auditability over convenience.** Where a design choice trades away a record for a simpler flow, keep the record. This is the tiebreaker whenever two designs are otherwise equal.
- **PRD §10 examples 1–28 are the executable specification.** Each exists as a named test calling the core directly, with no database, HTTP, clock mocking or fixtures beyond a state literal. A rule change altering any example's outcome changes the PRD in the same commit. The suite is green before any production deploy.
- **Money and slots are two independent gates, and neither short-circuits the other.** Every Bid passes both Maximum Bid and Roster Capacity, with distinct refusal reasons and distinct arithmetic. An unbounded Maximum Bid under CAP-6 exempts nothing from CAP-19. Both outcomes are evaluated and returned whether or not the first passed, because a refusal reports the gate that **passed** alongside the one that refused — proving every check ran and this is the only obstacle.
- **The outage recovery procedure must be written before the auction opens** — how a >15-minute outage maps onto a pause plus a compensating clock adjustment. It is scheduled work inside the build epics, not an up-front document, and auction open is blocked on it.
- **Stack is pinned:** SvelteKit 2.70.2 (not 3.x), `@sveltejs/adapter-netlify` 6.0.4 with `edge: false`, TypeScript strict with `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, Node 24 LTS, Deno for the tick, Supabase-managed Postgres ≥15.1.1.61, one Supabase Cron schedule invoking one Edge Function.
- **AD-1 through AD-30 in `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` are binding in full.** The constraints above are the ones that bend decisions before you open the spine; they do not replace it.

## Non-goals

- **Not a Fantrax replacement.** Scoring, lineups, trades, waivers and in-season roster management stay in Fantrax. This app is live for one offseason phase per year and goes quiet.
- **Not writing to Fantrax.** v1 never mutates Fantrax state; the Commissioner uploads the export by hand. Fantrax exposes no documented write path and omits salaries from its documented read surface.
- **Not enforcing activation.** Promoting a stashed Player out of a Minor League Slot requires absorbing his full contract against the cap. That rule is what makes unbounded eligible bidding self-correcting, and it is enforced in Fantrax in-season. This app never models it, never warns about it, and never blocks a Bid on account of it. A Team $2M under the cap winning a $30M eligible Player is correct behaviour, not a defect.
- **Not multi-tenant.** One League, hard-wired rules. League constants are named values in `core/constants.ts`; no admin UI edits them, no league creation, no other sports.
- **Not a valuation engine.** No projections, rankings, ADP, or suggested bid. The app enforces rules; it does not advise.
- **No proxy or max auto-bidding.** Rejected outright, not deferred — it changes auction dynamics and interacts badly with the lottery rule.
- **No overnight clock freeze and no anti-snipe extension.** Rejected for v1 as rules changes requiring league buy-in, not engineering decisions.
- **Not a draft tool and not a trade machine.** No rookie draft, no pick trading, no trades during the auction.
- **Not a chat app.** Discussion stays in Discord. The app posts to Discord; it does not host conversation.
- **No public or spectator access.** Authentication is required for everything; there is no anonymous read-only board.
- **No web push or PWA notifications**, no future-year cap projection, no Existing-Contract extensions or restructures, and no historical archive across offseasons. v1 covers one auction.

## Success signal

The BBSL runs its 2026 offseason auction entirely in this app and finishes with **no result contested on the grounds that the app computed a cap figure, clock, increment, or lottery draw incorrectly** — demonstrable as fewer than five Commissioner overrides across the whole auction, none of them correcting an app error, against a complete Audit Log the league can read. The auction ends on its own League Clock expiry, contract assignment completes, and the Fantrax export passes its cap and roster checks on the first attempt.

## Assumptions

- **When voiding a Bid recomputes the League Clock to an instant already past, the Auction Phase ends at the *next* clock evaluation and takes effect prospectively only** — nothing accepted in the interim is invalidated. Derived from the commissioner's OQ-7 answer rather than stated by it; the alternative, retroactive invalidation, contradicts the append-only log and the "late, not wrong" posture the close sweep runs on. **This is the only live inference in the spec.**
- Minors Exposure is sized to the sum of the *largest* surplus Eligible Leading Bids, because Slot Placement follows close order and no bidder can steer which of its wins lands in minors. A cheaper worst-case assumption would let a Team over-commit.
- Outbid notices cannot be muted, on the grounds that muting them undermines the fairness premise of a 24/7 clock.
- An unbid Nomination holds its Team's Nomination Slot for the remainder of the Auction Phase. Confirmed by the league as the accepted consequence of the rules as written; the app surfaces it rather than mitigating it.
- Verifiable Randomizer fairness — recorded seed and Contender list, independently reproducible — is a hard requirement rather than a nicety.
- The Commissioner pause exists as an outage and dispute escape hatch, despite the league running clocks continuously by rule. It is an administrative tool, not a scheduled freeze.
- Minor League Eligibility defaults to *not* eligible on import, so an unset flag fails safe rather than permissive. The commissioner's answer settled that the flag is hand-set; the direction of the default is this spec's call.
- **League Median takes the lower of the two middle values rather than their mean.** The commissioner asked for a median and did not specify the even-count rule; across 30 Teams the conventional definition lands at $250,000 granularity, off the $500,000 grid and not losslessly renderable at one decimal. The lower middle value is always a figure a real Team holds. Rounding the mean to the grid was rejected — it publishes a number nobody holds and no manager can reproduce — as was a two-decimal rendering, which would make the median the only money figure in the product with its own format.

*Retired 2026-08-17 when the commissioner answered the open questions:* the $100,000 granularity assumption (the real figure is $500,000), the imported-eligibility-flag assumption (the export does not carry it), and the 1-year assignment default (removed — the export blocks instead).

*Retired 2026-08-18, surfaced by UX journey work:* the **two-file import** assumption. CAP-1 previously read "the auction refuses to open unless **both** imports are committed", and FR-1 described "a Fantrax-exported roster/salary file" covering all 30 Teams. The real Fantrax workflow produces **thirty-one** exports — one Free Agent pool plus one per Team. CAP-1, FR-1 and FR-3 are corrected accordingly. This changes the import surface materially (batch supply, per-file parse status, Team resolution by name, outstanding-Team naming, single-Team re-supply) and is the reason `EXPERIENCE.md` specifies an import status list rather than two upload controls.

*Retired 2026-08-18 when the commissioner named the missing Teams index (CAP-20, FR-39):* the **partial-information** assumption. CAP-10 previously reserved Free Minor League Slots and Minors Exposure to the viewer's own Team. Neither reservation was enforceable — Free Minor League Slots is `3 −` an occupancy CAP-10 already publishes, and Minors Exposure is Committed Bids less a Team's non-eligible leading amounts, every one of which is public on the board. A thirty-row index would have handed both figures for the whole league to anyone at a glance. **Every Team figure is now explicitly public**, Maximum Bid excepted on the different ground that it is per-Auction. Concealment that survives only until someone bothers to subtract is worse than none, because it misleads exactly the managers who trust it.
